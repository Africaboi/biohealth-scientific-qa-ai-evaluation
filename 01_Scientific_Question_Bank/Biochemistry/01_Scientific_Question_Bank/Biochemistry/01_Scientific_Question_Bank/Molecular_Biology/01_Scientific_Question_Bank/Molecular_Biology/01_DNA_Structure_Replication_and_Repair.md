
# DNA Structure, Replication and Repair

## Introduction

This question set assesses advanced understanding of DNA structure, DNA replication, replication fidelity, DNA damage, and molecular repair mechanisms.

Each question includes the correct answer, a detailed scientific explanation, and an evaluation of the alternative options.

## Question 1: DNA Double-Helix Structure

Which interaction contributes most directly to the stabilisation of the DNA double helix through interactions between adjacent stacked base pairs?

A. Peptide bonds  
B. Base-stacking interactions  
C. Disulphide bonds  
D. Phosphodiester bonds between complementary bases

**Correct Answer: B. Base-stacking interactions**

### Scientific Explanation

DNA stability arises from several molecular interactions. Base stacking involves interactions between adjacent aromatic nucleotide bases, including van der Waals forces and hydrophobic effects. These interactions make a major contribution to double-helix stability.

Hydrogen bonds between complementary bases also contribute to DNA structure and help maintain base-pairing specificity:

- Adenine pairs with thymine through two hydrogen bonds.
- Guanine pairs with cytosine through three hydrogen bonds.

The sugar-phosphate backbone is held together by covalent phosphodiester bonds.

### Evaluation of Alternative Answers

- A. Incorrect. Peptide bonds link amino acids in proteins.
- C. Incorrect. Disulphide bonds stabilise certain protein structures.
- D. Incorrect. Phosphodiester bonds link adjacent nucleotides along a DNA strand, not complementary bases across the two strands.

## Question 2: DNA Replication Directionality

DNA polymerases generally synthesise a new DNA strand in which direction?

A. 3′ to 5′  
B. 5′ to 3′  
C. Both 3′ to 5′ and 5′ to 3′  
D. In either direction depending on the organism

**Correct Answer: B. 5′ to 3′**

### Scientific Explanation

DNA polymerases add incoming deoxyribonucleoside triphosphates (dNTPs) to the free 3′-hydroxyl group of the growing DNA strand.

Consequently, DNA synthesis proceeds in the 5′ to 3′ direction.

The template strand is read in the 3′ to 5′ direction. This directional requirement explains why the two newly synthesised strands at a replication fork are produced differently:

- The leading strand is synthesised continuously towards the replication fork.
- The lagging strand is synthesised discontinuously, producing Okazaki fragments.

### Evaluation of Alternative Answers

- A. Incorrect. DNA polymerases read the template in this direction but synthesise the new strand in the opposite direction.
- C. Incorrect. Standard DNA polymerases extend DNA in the 5′ to 3′ direction.
- D. Incorrect. The directionality of DNA polymerase-mediated synthesis is generally conserved.

## Question 3: Role of DNA Helicase

What is the primary function of DNA helicase during DNA replication?

A. Joining Okazaki fragments  
B. Separating the two parental DNA strands  
C. Synthesising RNA primers  
D. Removing incorrectly incorporated nucleotides

**Correct Answer: B. Separating the two parental DNA strands**

### Scientific Explanation

DNA helicase is an ATP-dependent motor protein that unwinds the parental DNA double helix at the replication fork.

By disrupting the interactions that maintain base pairing, helicase separates the two DNA strands, allowing each strand to serve as a template for synthesis of a complementary strand.

Other proteins also participate in replication:

- Primase synthesises RNA primers.
- DNA polymerases synthesise new DNA.
- DNA ligase seals remaining nicks between DNA fragments.
- Single-strand DNA-binding proteins stabilise separated DNA strands.

### Evaluation of Alternative Answers

- A. Incorrect. DNA ligase joins Okazaki fragments.
- C. Incorrect. Primase synthesises RNA primers.
- D. Incorrect. Proofreading exonuclease activity of certain DNA polymerases removes incorrectly incorporated nucleotides.

## Question 4: Okazaki Fragments

Why are Okazaki fragments produced during DNA replication?

A. DNA polymerase can synthesise DNA only in the 5′ to 3′ direction.  
B. Helicase can unwind only one DNA strand.  
C. DNA ligase prevents continuous DNA synthesis.  
D. The leading strand contains RNA instead of DNA.

**Correct Answer: A. DNA polymerase can synthesise DNA only in the 5′ to 3′ direction.**

### Scientific Explanation

The two strands of DNA are antiparallel. Because DNA polymerases synthesise new DNA exclusively in the 5′ to 3′ direction, the two strands are copied differently at the replication fork.

The leading strand is synthesised continuously in the direction of fork movement.

The lagging strand is synthesised discontinuously as short DNA segments known as Okazaki fragments.

Each Okazaki fragment begins with an RNA primer. The primers are subsequently removed, the gaps are filled with DNA, and DNA ligase joins the fragments.

### Evaluation of Alternative Answers

- B. Incorrect. Helicase unwinds the parental double helix rather than explaining the discontinuous synthesis of the lagging strand.
- C. Incorrect. DNA ligase joins fragments after their synthesis.
- D. Incorrect. Both newly synthesised strands consist primarily of DNA, although RNA primers are temporarily required.

## Question 5: DNA Polymerase Proofreading

Which enzymatic activity enables certain DNA polymerases to remove incorrectly incorporated nucleotides during replication?

A. 5′ to 3′ exonuclease activity  
B. 3′ to 5′ exonuclease activity  
C. Endonuclease activity  
D. DNA ligase activity

**Correct Answer: B. 3′ to 5′ exonuclease activity**

### Scientific Explanation

Many replicative DNA polymerases possess proofreading activity mediated by a 3′ to 5′ exonuclease domain.

When an incorrect nucleotide is incorporated, the resulting mismatch can disrupt proper base pairing and slow further DNA synthesis.

The polymerase transfers the newly synthesised DNA end to its exonuclease site, where the incorrectly incorporated nucleotide is removed.

DNA synthesis can then resume with the correct nucleotide.

This proofreading mechanism significantly improves replication fidelity.

### Evaluation of Alternative Answers

- A. Incorrect. 5′ to 3′ exonuclease activity has other roles in certain DNA-processing reactions and is not the proofreading activity described here.
- C. Incorrect. Endonucleases cleave internal phosphodiester bonds within nucleic acid strands.
- D. Incorrect. DNA ligase seals breaks in the DNA backbone.

## Question 6: DNA Repair Mechanisms

Which DNA repair pathway primarily removes bulky, helix-distorting DNA lesions such as ultraviolet-induced cyclobutane pyrimidine dimers in humans?

A. Mismatch repair  
B. Nucleotide excision repair  
C. Base excision repair  
D. Homologous recombination

**Correct Answer: B. Nucleotide excision repair**

### Scientific Explanation

Nucleotide excision repair (NER) removes bulky DNA lesions that distort the DNA double helix.

Ultraviolet radiation can induce cyclobutane pyrimidine dimers and other photoproducts that interfere with normal DNA replication and transcription.

In humans, NER involves:

1. Recognition of DNA damage.
2. Local unwinding of the affected DNA region.
3. Incision of the damaged strand on both sides of the lesion.
4. Removal of the damaged oligonucleotide.
5. DNA resynthesis using the undamaged strand as a template.
6. Sealing of the remaining nick by DNA ligase.

Defects in NER proteins are associated with xeroderma pigmentosum.

### Evaluation of Alternative Answers

- A. Incorrect. Mismatch repair primarily corrects replication-associated base-pairing errors and insertion/deletion loops.
- C. Incorrect. Base excision repair primarily removes small, non-helix-distorting base lesions.
- D. Incorrect. Homologous recombination repairs double-strand breaks using a homologous DNA template.

## Question 7: Mismatch Repair

What is the primary role of the mismatch repair system?

A. Repairing replication-associated base mismatches and small insertion/deletion loops  
B. Removing all RNA primers during replication  
C. Synthesising new DNA strands  
D. Unwinding the parental DNA double helix

**Correct Answer: A. Repairing replication-associated base mismatches and small insertion/deletion loops**

### Scientific Explanation

Mismatch repair (MMR) corrects errors that escape the proofreading activity of DNA polymerases.

These errors include:

- Incorrectly paired nucleotides.
- Small insertion/deletion loops, particularly in repetitive DNA sequences.

In humans, mismatch recognition involves proteins such as MSH2 and MSH6, while other proteins, including MLH1 and PMS2, participate in subsequent repair steps.

The repair machinery identifies the newly synthesised strand, removes the region containing the mismatch, and uses the original strand as a template for accurate resynthesis.

Inherited defects in mismatch repair genes are associated with Lynch syndrome.

### Evaluation of Alternative Answers

- B. Incorrect. RNA primer removal is performed by specialised DNA-processing enzymes.
- C. Incorrect. DNA polymerases synthesise new DNA strands.
- D. Incorrect. DNA helicases unwind the parental DNA double helix.

## Question 8: Homologous Recombination

Which statement best describes homologous recombination as a DNA double-strand break repair mechanism?

A. It repairs DNA exclusively by removing damaged nitrogenous bases.  
B. It uses a homologous DNA sequence as a template for accurate repair.  
C. It joins broken DNA ends without requiring sequence homology.  
D. It repairs only RNA molecules.

**Correct Answer: B. It uses a homologous DNA sequence as a template for accurate repair.**

### Scientific Explanation

Homologous recombination (HR) is an important mechanism for repairing DNA double-strand breaks.

In human cells, HR commonly uses the sister chromatid as a template, making it particularly important during the S and G2 phases of the cell cycle.

A simplified description of the process includes:

1. Recognition and processing of the DNA double-strand break.
2. Resection of DNA ends to generate single-stranded DNA.
3. Assembly of RAD51-containing nucleoprotein filaments.
4. Homology searching and strand invasion.
5. DNA synthesis using the homologous template.
6. Resolution or dissolution of recombination intermediates.

Proteins such as BRCA1 and BRCA2 contribute to the regulation and efficient execution of homologous recombination.

### Evaluation of Alternative Answers

- A. Incorrect. Removal of damaged bases is primarily associated with base excision repair.
- C. Incorrect. Joining DNA ends without requiring extensive sequence homology is characteristic of non-homologous end joining.
- D. Incorrect. Homologous recombination is a DNA repair mechanism.

## Question 9: Telomeres and DNA Replication

What is the principal function of telomerase in eukaryotic cells?

A. Removing RNA primers from Okazaki fragments  
B. Extending chromosome ends by adding repetitive DNA sequences  
C. Repairing all DNA double-strand breaks  
D. Separating sister chromatids during mitosis

**Correct Answer: B. Extending chromosome ends by adding repetitive DNA sequences**

### Scientific Explanation

Telomerase is a ribonucleoprotein enzyme that helps maintain telomere length at the ends of linear chromosomes.

It contains:

- Telomerase reverse transcriptase (TERT), which has reverse transcriptase activity.
- An internal RNA component (TERC), which serves as a template for telomeric DNA synthesis.

Telomerase extends the 3′ ends of chromosomes by adding repetitive DNA sequences.

This activity helps compensate for the end-replication problem associated with conventional DNA replication.

Telomerase activity is prominent in germline cells, certain stem cells, and many cancer cells, although its activity varies across normal somatic tissues.

### Evaluation of Alternative Answers

- A. Incorrect. RNA primer removal involves specialised enzymes and DNA-processing pathways.
- C. Incorrect. Telomerase does not repair all double-strand breaks.
- D. Incorrect. Sister chromatid separation is mediated by chromosome segregation machinery during cell division.

## Question 10: DNA Replication and ATP

Which statement best explains the energetic basis of DNA polymerisation?

A. DNA polymerases directly use ATP hydrolysis as the sole energy source for every nucleotide addition.  
B. Incorporation of an incoming deoxyribonucleoside triphosphate releases pyrophosphate, and subsequent pyrophosphate hydrolysis helps drive the reaction forward.  
C. DNA polymerisation occurs without any energetically favourable chemical reaction.  
D. DNA ligase supplies all the energy required for DNA chain elongation.

**Correct Answer: B. Incorporation of an incoming deoxyribonucleoside triphosphate releases pyrophosphate, and subsequent pyrophosphate hydrolysis helps drive the reaction forward.**

### Scientific Explanation

DNA polymerisation involves the addition of an incoming deoxyribonucleoside triphosphate (dNTP) to the 3′-hydroxyl group of the growing DNA strand.

The reaction forms a new phosphodiester bond and releases pyrophosphate (PPi).

Subsequent hydrolysis of pyrophosphate into inorganic phosphate helps make the overall process energetically favourable and effectively drives DNA chain elongation forward.

The incoming dNTP therefore provides the chemical energy associated with nucleotide incorporation.

### Evaluation of Alternative Answers

- A. Incorrect. DNA polymerases use the incoming dNTP substrate rather than requiring ATP hydrolysis as the sole energy source for each nucleotide addition.
- C. Incorrect. DNA polymerisation is coupled to energetically favourable chemical reactions.
- D. Incorrect. DNA ligase seals nicks in the DNA backbone; it does not supply all the energy for DNA chain elongation.
