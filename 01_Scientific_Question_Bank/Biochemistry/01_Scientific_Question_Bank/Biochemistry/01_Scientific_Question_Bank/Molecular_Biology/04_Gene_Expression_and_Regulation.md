
# Gene Expression and Regulation

## Overview

This question set assesses intermediate-to-advanced understanding of gene expression and its regulation in prokaryotic and eukaryotic cells. It covers transcriptional regulation, transcription factors, operons, enhancers, epigenetic modifications, and post-transcriptional control mechanisms.

---

## Question 1: Gene Expression

**Question:** Which statement best describes gene expression?

A. The permanent alteration of a gene's DNA sequence.  
B. The process through which genetic information is used to produce a functional RNA or protein product.  
C. The duplication of the entire genome before cell division.  
D. The removal of all non-coding DNA sequences from chromosomes.

**Correct Answer:** B. The process through which genetic information is used to produce a functional RNA or protein product.

**Explanation:** Gene expression involves the utilisation of genetic information to produce functional gene products. Protein-coding genes are generally expressed through transcription into RNA followed by translation into proteins. Non-protein-coding genes produce functional RNA molecules, such as ribosomal RNA, transfer RNA and microRNA.

**Distractor Evaluation:**
- A describes a genetic mutation rather than gene expression.
- C describes DNA replication.
- D is incorrect because non-coding DNA has diverse regulatory and structural functions and is not universally removed.

## Question 2: Lac Operon Regulation

**Question:** In *Escherichia coli*, what happens to the lac operon when lactose is available and glucose concentration is low?

A. The lac repressor remains permanently bound to the operator.  
B. All transcription of the lac operon stops.  
C. Allolactose inactivates the lac repressor, while the cAMP–CAP complex enhances transcription.  
D. The lac operon is converted into a constitutively expressed gene.

**Correct Answer:** C. Allolactose inactivates the lac repressor, while the cAMP–CAP complex enhances transcription.

**Explanation:** The lac operon is regulated by both negative and positive control. Allolactose, an isomer of lactose, binds to the lac repressor and reduces its affinity for the operator, allowing transcription to proceed. When glucose is low, intracellular cAMP increases and binds to catabolite activator protein (CAP). The cAMP–CAP complex binds near the promoter and facilitates transcription. This combination supports strong lac operon expression.

**Distractor Evaluation:**
- A is incorrect because allolactose reduces repressor binding to the operator.
- B is incorrect because lactose availability permits transcription.
- D is incorrect because lac operon expression remains subject to regulatory control.

## Question 3: Transcription Factors

**Question:** What is the primary role of transcription factors in eukaryotic gene regulation?

A. They directly catalyse DNA replication.  
B. They bind specific DNA sequences or interact with other regulatory proteins to influence transcription.  
C. They convert mRNA into amino acids.  
D. They remove all introns from genomic DNA.

**Correct Answer:** B. They bind specific DNA sequences or interact with other regulatory proteins to influence transcription.

**Explanation:** Transcription factors are proteins that regulate gene transcription by recognising specific DNA sequences, interacting with other transcriptional regulators, or recruiting transcriptional machinery. Activators generally enhance transcription, while repressors reduce transcription. Their effects depend on promoter and enhancer organisation, chromatin structure and cellular context.

**Distractor Evaluation:**
- A is incorrect because DNA replication is performed by replication machinery.
- C is incorrect because translation machinery decodes mRNA into a polypeptide.
- D is incorrect because intron removal occurs during RNA processing, not through transcription factors.

## Question 4: Enhancers and Silencers

**Question:** Which statement best describes the function of enhancers in eukaryotic gene regulation?

A. They are DNA sequences that can increase transcription through interactions with transcriptional activators and promoter-associated machinery.  
B. They are RNA molecules that directly translate proteins.  
C. They permanently prevent RNA polymerase from binding to every promoter.  
D. They are DNA sequences responsible exclusively for terminating transcription.

**Correct Answer:** A. They are DNA sequences that can increase transcription through interactions with transcriptional activators and promoter-associated machinery.

**Explanation:** Enhancers are cis-regulatory DNA elements that increase transcription of target genes. They may be located upstream, downstream or within introns of their target genes and can function over considerable genomic distances. DNA looping facilitates interactions between enhancer-bound regulatory proteins and promoter-associated transcriptional machinery. Silencers, by contrast, are regulatory elements associated with transcriptional repression.

**Distractor Evaluation:**
- B is incorrect because enhancers are DNA regulatory elements.
- C is incorrect because enhancers generally promote rather than universally prevent transcription.
- D is incorrect because transcription termination involves specialised termination mechanisms.

## Question 5: DNA Methylation

**Question:** What is the most common association between promoter DNA methylation and gene expression in mammalian cells?

A. Increased promoter methylation generally enhances transcription.  
B. Increased promoter methylation is frequently associated with transcriptional repression.  
C. DNA methylation always causes permanent gene deletion.  
D. DNA methylation converts DNA into RNA.

**Correct Answer:** B. Increased promoter methylation is frequently associated with transcriptional repression.

**Explanation:** DNA methylation commonly occurs at cytosine residues within CpG dinucleotides in mammalian genomes. Increased methylation at gene promoters is frequently associated with reduced transcription because it can interfere with transcription factor binding and recruit proteins that promote repressive chromatin states. However, the relationship between DNA methylation and gene expression depends on genomic location and cellular context.

**Distractor Evaluation:**
- A is incorrect as a general description of promoter methylation.
- C is incorrect because methylation is an epigenetic modification, not gene deletion.
- D is incorrect because DNA methylation does not convert DNA into RNA.

## Question 6: Histone Acetylation

**Question:** How does histone acetylation commonly influence gene expression?

A. It generally promotes a more accessible chromatin environment that favours transcription.  
B. It causes the complete destruction of nucleosomes.  
C. It prevents all transcription factor binding.  
D. It directly changes the nucleotide sequence of DNA.

**Correct Answer:** A. It generally promotes a more accessible chromatin environment that favours transcription.

**Explanation:** Histone acetyltransferases (HATs) add acetyl groups to lysine residues on histone proteins. This modification reduces the positive charge of lysine residues and can weaken interactions between histones and negatively charged DNA. Histone acetylation is commonly associated with more accessible chromatin and increased transcriptional activity. Histone deacetylases (HDACs) remove acetyl groups and can promote transcriptional repression.

**Distractor Evaluation:**
- B is incorrect because acetylation does not destroy nucleosomes.
- C is incorrect because histone acetylation often facilitates transcription factor access.
- D is incorrect because histone acetylation changes chromatin-associated proteins rather than the DNA nucleotide sequence.

## Question 7: Epigenetic Regulation

**Question:** Which of the following is an example of an epigenetic mechanism of gene regulation?

A. A single-nucleotide substitution in a protein-coding gene.  
B. DNA methylation that alters gene expression without changing the underlying DNA sequence.  
C. Deletion of an entire chromosome.  
D. A frameshift mutation caused by nucleotide insertion.

**Correct Answer:** B. DNA methylation that alters gene expression without changing the underlying DNA sequence.

**Explanation:** Epigenetic regulation involves changes in gene activity that occur without altering the underlying DNA sequence. Important mechanisms include DNA methylation, histone modifications and regulation by non-coding RNAs. Some epigenetic states can be maintained through cell division, although their stability and reversibility vary.

**Distractor Evaluation:**
- A is a genetic mutation.
- C is a chromosomal alteration.
- D is a genetic mutation that can alter the reading frame of a coding sequence.

## Question 8: Feedback Regulation of Gene Expression

**Question:** What is the main biological advantage of negative feedback regulation in gene expression?

A. It ensures that every gene is expressed continuously.  
B. It helps maintain appropriate levels of gene products by reducing further expression when sufficient product has accumulated.  
C. It prevents all forms of protein degradation.  
D. It permanently stops transcription in every cell.

**Correct Answer:** B. It helps maintain appropriate levels of gene products by reducing further expression when sufficient product has accumulated.

**Explanation:** Negative feedback occurs when a gene product or a downstream component of its signalling pathway reduces the activity of the process responsible for its production. This mechanism helps maintain homeostasis, prevents excessive accumulation of gene products and allows cells to respond dynamically to changing conditions.

**Distractor Evaluation:**
- A is incorrect because feedback regulation can reduce or increase gene expression depending on the mechanism.
- C is incorrect because protein degradation is a separate process and is not universally prevented by negative feedback.
- D is incorrect because feedback regulation is generally specific to particular genes or pathways.

## Question 9: Alternative Splicing and Gene Expression

**Question:** How does alternative splicing contribute to the regulation of gene expression in eukaryotic cells?

A. It allows different combinations of exons to be incorporated into mature mRNA from the same pre-mRNA transcript.  
B. It duplicates the entire genome.  
C. It permanently removes all regulatory DNA sequences.  
D. It converts proteins directly into RNA.

**Correct Answer:** A. It allows different combinations of exons to be incorporated into mature mRNA from the same pre-mRNA transcript.

**Explanation:** Alternative splicing enables a single gene to generate multiple mature mRNA variants through different combinations of exon inclusion and exclusion. These transcript variants may produce distinct protein isoforms with different biological properties. Alternative splicing is therefore an important mechanism of transcriptome and proteome diversity.

**Distractor Evaluation:**
- B is incorrect because genome duplication occurs during DNA replication.
- C is incorrect because splicing acts on RNA transcripts, not genomic regulatory DNA.
- D is incorrect because proteins are not directly converted into RNA through alternative splicing.

## Question 10: Gene Regulation in Prokaryotes and Eukaryotes

**Question:** Which statement best distinguishes gene regulation in prokaryotes from gene regulation in eukaryotes?

A. Prokaryotes regulate genes only after translation, while eukaryotes regulate genes only during DNA replication.  
B. Prokaryotic regulation frequently involves operons, whereas eukaryotic regulation commonly involves complex interactions among transcription factors, chromatin modifications and regulatory DNA elements.  
C. Eukaryotic genes cannot be regulated by transcription factors.  
D. Prokaryotes do not regulate gene expression.

**Correct Answer:** B. Prokaryotic regulation frequently involves operons, whereas eukaryotic regulation commonly involves complex interactions among transcription factors, chromatin modifications and regulatory DNA elements.

**Explanation:** Prokaryotic gene regulation often involves operons, allowing coordinated expression of functionally related genes from a shared promoter. Eukaryotic regulation is generally more complex and can occur at transcriptional, post-transcriptional, translational and post-translational levels. Chromatin organisation, enhancers, silencers, transcription factors and non-coding RNAs all contribute to eukaryotic gene regulation.

**Distractor Evaluation:**
- A is incorrect because both prokaryotes and eukaryotes regulate gene expression at multiple stages.
- C is incorrect because transcription factors are central to eukaryotic gene regulation.
- D is incorrect because prokaryotes possess sophisticated gene-regulatory systems.
