
# Microbial Structure and Classification

## Overview

This question set assesses intermediate-to-advanced understanding of microbial structure, cellular organisation, classification and the distinguishing characteristics of bacteria, fungi and viruses.

---

## Question 1: Bacterial Cell Wall Structure

**Question:** Which component is primarily responsible for the structural rigidity of most bacterial cell walls?

A. Cellulose  
B. Peptidoglycan  
C. Chitin  
D. Phospholipid  

**Correct Answer:** B. Peptidoglycan.

**Explanation:** Peptidoglycan is a polymer composed of alternating N-acetylglucosamine (NAG) and N-acetylmuramic acid (NAM) residues cross-linked by short peptide chains. It provides mechanical strength and helps bacteria resist osmotic pressure. Its structure and abundance differ between Gram-positive and Gram-negative bacteria.

**Distractor Evaluation:**
- A is incorrect because cellulose is a major structural component of plant cell walls.
- C is incorrect because chitin is a major component of fungal cell walls.
- D is incorrect because phospholipids are major components of cell membranes rather than the primary structural component of bacterial cell walls.

## Question 2: Gram-Positive and Gram-Negative Bacteria

**Question:** Which structural feature distinguishes Gram-negative bacteria from Gram-positive bacteria?

A. Gram-negative bacteria possess a thick peptidoglycan layer.  
B. Gram-negative bacteria possess an outer membrane containing lipopolysaccharide (LPS).  
C. Gram-negative bacteria lack a cytoplasmic membrane.  
D. Gram-negative bacteria contain chitin in their cell walls.

**Correct Answer:** B. Gram-negative bacteria possess an outer membrane containing lipopolysaccharide (LPS).

**Explanation:** Gram-negative bacteria possess a relatively thin peptidoglycan layer located within the periplasmic space between the inner cytoplasmic membrane and an outer membrane. The outer membrane contains LPS, which includes lipid A, the core oligosaccharide and the O-antigen. Gram-positive bacteria generally possess a much thicker peptidoglycan layer and lack this outer membrane.

**Distractor Evaluation:**
- A describes the characteristic thick peptidoglycan layer of Gram-positive bacteria.
- C is incorrect because Gram-negative bacteria possess a cytoplasmic membrane.
- D is incorrect because chitin is associated primarily with fungal cell walls.

## Question 3: Bacterial Ribosomes

**Question:** What is the sedimentation coefficient of bacterial cytoplasmic ribosomes?

A. 40S  
B. 60S  
C. 70S  
D. 80S  

**Correct Answer:** C. 70S.

**Explanation:** Most bacterial cytoplasmic ribosomes are 70S particles composed of a 30S small subunit and a 50S large subunit. These subunits associate during translation. The Svedberg unit measures sedimentation behaviour and is not arithmetically additive; therefore, 30S and 50S combine to form a 70S ribosome rather than a 80S ribosome.

**Distractor Evaluation:**
- A and B represent the sedimentation coefficients of eukaryotic cytoplasmic ribosomal subunits.
- D is the sedimentation coefficient of typical eukaryotic cytoplasmic ribosomes.

## Question 4: Bacterial Capsules

**Question:** Which function is commonly associated with bacterial capsules?

A. Direct synthesis of ATP  
B. Protection against phagocytosis and contribution to microbial adhesion  
C. Formation of ribosomal subunits  
D. Replication of bacterial chromosomes  

**Correct Answer:** B. Protection against phagocytosis and contribution to microbial adhesion.

**Explanation:** Bacterial capsules are organised extracellular layers, commonly composed of polysaccharides, although some are polypeptide-based. They can contribute to bacterial adhesion, biofilm formation and resistance to phagocytosis. These properties may enhance bacterial survival and pathogenic potential.

**Distractor Evaluation:**
- A is incorrect because ATP synthesis is associated with cellular energy metabolism.
- C is incorrect because ribosomal subunits are assembled through ribosomal RNA and proteins.
- D is incorrect because chromosome replication is performed by DNA replication machinery.

## Question 5: Bacterial Endospores

**Question:** Which bacterial genera are particularly well known for producing endospores?

A. Escherichia and Salmonella  
B. Bacillus and Clostridium  
C. Staphylococcus and Streptococcus  
D. Neisseria and Vibrio  

**Correct Answer:** B. Bacillus and Clostridium.

**Explanation:** Species belonging to the genera Bacillus and Clostridium are well known for their ability to form endospores. Endospores are dormant, highly resistant structures that enable bacterial survival under unfavourable environmental conditions. Their resistance is associated with features such as low core water content, calcium-dipicolinate and specialised protective proteins.

**Distractor Evaluation:**
- A contains important bacterial genera that do not characteristically produce endospores.
- C contains non-spore-forming bacterial genera.
- D contains non-spore-forming bacterial genera.

## Question 6: Fungal Cell Wall Composition

**Question:** Which combination of substances is characteristic of fungal cell walls?

A. Peptidoglycan and lipopolysaccharide  
B. Chitin and β-glucans  
C. Cellulose and peptidoglycan  
D. Lipid A and teichoic acid  

**Correct Answer:** B. Chitin and β-glucans.

**Explanation:** Fungal cell walls contain chitin, β-glucans and mannoproteins in varying proportions depending on the fungal species and growth conditions. These components provide structural support and help maintain cellular integrity. Their composition distinguishes fungal cell walls from bacterial cell walls.

**Distractor Evaluation:**
- A describes components associated with Gram-negative bacterial envelopes.
- C is incorrect because peptidoglycan is characteristic of bacterial cell walls.
- D combines components associated with bacterial cell envelopes.

## Question 7: Viral Structure

**Question:** What is the primary function of a viral capsid?

A. Production of ATP for viral replication  
B. Protection of the viral genome and assistance in delivering it into host cells  
C. Independent synthesis of viral proteins  
D. Production of ribosomes for viral translation  

**Correct Answer:** B. Protection of the viral genome and assistance in delivering it into host cells.

**Explanation:** The viral capsid is a protein structure surrounding the viral nucleic acid. It protects the viral genome and contributes to viral attachment, entry and delivery of genetic material into host cells. Some viruses also possess an external lipid envelope derived from host-cell membranes.

**Distractor Evaluation:**
- A is incorrect because viruses depend on host-cell energy systems.
- C is incorrect because viruses rely on host-cell machinery for protein synthesis.
- D is incorrect because viruses do not independently produce functional ribosomes.

## Question 8: Bacterial Cell Membrane

**Question:** Which statement best describes the role of the bacterial cytoplasmic membrane?

A. It is primarily responsible for storing genetic information.  
B. It regulates substance transport and supports several energy-generating processes.  
C. It is composed exclusively of peptidoglycan.  
D. It is absent in all prokaryotic cells.

**Correct Answer:** B. It regulates substance transport and supports several energy-generating processes.

**Explanation:** The bacterial cytoplasmic membrane is a selectively permeable phospholipid bilayer containing proteins. It regulates the movement of substances into and out of the cell. In many bacteria, it also houses components of the electron transport chain and ATP synthase involved in oxidative phosphorylation.

**Distractor Evaluation:**
- A is incorrect because bacterial genetic information is primarily stored in the chromosome and other genetic elements.
- C is incorrect because peptidoglycan is a cell wall component, not the principal membrane material.
- D is incorrect because prokaryotic cells possess cytoplasmic membranes.

## Question 9: Bacterial Classification

**Question:** Which characteristic is commonly used in the conventional classification and identification of bacteria?

A. Gram-staining reaction  
B. Presence of a nuclear membrane  
C. Presence of mitochondria  
D. Presence of chloroplasts in all species  

**Correct Answer:** A. Gram-staining reaction.

**Explanation:** Gram staining is a widely used differential staining technique that distinguishes bacteria according to differences in cell envelope structure. Gram-positive bacteria generally retain the crystal violet–iodine complex and appear purple, whereas Gram-negative bacteria are decolourised and take up the counterstain, appearing pink or red. Other identification methods include morphology, biochemical characteristics, antigenic properties and molecular sequencing.

**Distractor Evaluation:**
- B is incorrect because bacteria lack a membrane-bound nucleus.
- C is incorrect because bacteria do not possess mitochondria.
- D is incorrect because chloroplasts are not present in all bacteria.

## Question 10: Bacterial Plasmids

**Question:** Which statement best describes bacterial plasmids?

A. They are essential components of every bacterial chromosome.  
B. They are usually extrachromosomal DNA molecules capable of independent replication.  
C. They are protein structures responsible for bacterial movement.  
D. They are membrane-bound organelles involved in ATP synthesis.

**Correct Answer:** B. They are usually extrachromosomal DNA molecules capable of independent replication.

**Explanation:** Plasmids are genetic elements, commonly circular double-stranded DNA molecules, that replicate independently of the bacterial chromosome. They may carry genes that provide advantages under particular conditions, including antimicrobial resistance, virulence factors and specialised metabolic functions. Some plasmids can be transferred between bacterial cells through horizontal gene transfer.

**Distractor Evaluation:**
- A is incorrect because plasmids are generally distinct from the main bacterial chromosome and are not universally present.
- C is incorrect because bacterial movement is commonly mediated by flagella and other specialised structures.
- D is incorrect because bacteria lack membrane-bound mitochondria.
