
# Microbial Genetics

## Overview
This question set assesses understanding of microbial genetic organisation, gene transfer, mutation, genetic recombination, and mechanisms of gene regulation. It focuses primarily on bacterial genetics, with relevant concepts from other microorganisms.

---

## Question 1: Bacterial Chromosomes and Plasmids

**Which statement best distinguishes a typical bacterial chromosome from a plasmid?**

A. The bacterial chromosome contains only non-essential genes, whereas plasmids contain all essential genes.

B. The bacterial chromosome generally carries genes required for normal cellular functions, while plasmids often carry additional genes that may confer advantages under particular environmental conditions.

C. Plasmids are always integrated into the bacterial chromosome.

D. Bacterial chromosomes cannot replicate independently of the bacterial cell.

**Correct Answer: B**

**Explanation:**

The bacterial chromosome typically contains genes essential for cellular growth, metabolism, and reproduction. Plasmids are usually smaller, independently replicating DNA molecules that may carry genes associated with antibiotic resistance, virulence, or specialised metabolic capabilities. Although plasmids can provide selective advantages, they are not necessarily essential for bacterial survival under all conditions.

**Evaluation of Distractors:**

- **A:** Incorrect. The chromosome generally contains essential genes, although it also contains non-essential genes. Plasmids may carry advantageous but non-essential genes.
- **C:** Incorrect. Many plasmids exist independently of the chromosome, although some can integrate into it.
- **D:** Incorrect. Bacterial chromosomes replicate within cells using cellular replication machinery; they do not need to replicate independently of the cell.

## Question 2: Bacterial Transformation

**A bacterial cell acquires genetic material released into its environment by another bacterial cell. Which mechanism is responsible?**

A. Conjugation

B. Transformation

C. Transduction

D. Binary fission

**Correct Answer: B**

**Explanation:**

Transformation is a form of horizontal gene transfer in which a competent bacterial cell takes up extracellular DNA from its surroundings. The acquired DNA may be incorporated into the bacterial chromosome through recombination or maintained as a plasmid if it possesses the appropriate replication elements.

Natural competence occurs in certain bacterial species and may be regulated by environmental conditions and cellular physiological states.

**Evaluation of Distractors:**

- **A:** Incorrect. Conjugation involves DNA transfer through direct contact between bacterial cells.
- **C:** Incorrect. Transduction involves bacteriophages transferring bacterial genetic material between cells.
- **D:** Incorrect. Binary fission is a form of bacterial reproduction, not horizontal gene transfer.

## Question 3: Generalised and Specialised Transduction

**Which statement correctly distinguishes generalised transduction from specialised transduction?**

A. Generalised transduction transfers only plasmid DNA, while specialised transduction transfers only chromosomal DNA.

B. Generalised transduction can transfer different regions of bacterial DNA, while specialised transduction transfers specific bacterial genes associated with the integration site of a temperate bacteriophage.

C. Generalised transduction requires direct bacterial contact, while specialised transduction requires extracellular DNA uptake.

D. Both processes occur exclusively through bacterial conjugation.

**Correct Answer: B**

**Explanation:**

Transduction is bacteriophage-mediated transfer of genetic material between bacterial cells.

In generalised transduction, bacterial DNA may be mistakenly packaged into phage particles during the production of new bacteriophages. Consequently, different regions of the bacterial chromosome may be transferred.

Specialised transduction occurs when a temperate bacteriophage excises incorrectly from the bacterial chromosome after integration. The resulting phage DNA may contain specific bacterial genes located near the original integration site.

**Evaluation of Distractors:**

- **A:** Incorrect. Generalised transduction can transfer chromosomal DNA, and specialised transduction may transfer specific chromosomal genes.
- **C:** Incorrect. Direct contact characterises conjugation, while extracellular DNA uptake characterises transformation.
- **D:** Incorrect. Both processes are mediated by bacteriophages rather than conjugation.

## Question 4: Bacterial Conjugation and the F Plasmid

**What is the principal role of the F plasmid in bacterial conjugation?**

A. It provides genetic information required for the formation of structures involved in DNA transfer.

B. It destroys foreign DNA entering the bacterial cell.

C. It prevents bacterial chromosome replication.

D. It converts bacterial RNA into DNA.

**Correct Answer: A**

**Explanation:**

The fertility (F) plasmid contains genes involved in bacterial conjugation, including genes responsible for the formation of the conjugative transfer apparatus. During conjugation, DNA can be transferred from a donor cell carrying the conjugative plasmid to a recipient cell.

The F plasmid can also integrate into the bacterial chromosome in certain circumstances. Cells containing an integrated F factor are referred to as Hfr (high-frequency recombination) cells and can transfer chromosomal genes during conjugation.

**Evaluation of Distractors:**

- **B:** Incorrect. Restriction-modification systems and other defence mechanisms contribute to the recognition and restriction of foreign DNA.
- **C:** Incorrect. The F plasmid does not generally prevent chromosome replication.
- **D:** Incorrect. RNA-to-DNA conversion is carried out by reverse transcriptase, not the F plasmid.

## Question 5: Transposable Genetic Elements

**Which of the following best describes a bacterial transposon?**

A. A DNA element capable of moving from one genomic location to another.

B. A protein that directly synthesises bacterial ribosomes.

C. A membrane structure responsible for bacterial motility.

D. A DNA molecule that cannot change its genomic position.

**Correct Answer: A**

**Explanation:**

Transposons are mobile genetic elements that can move between different DNA locations, including chromosomal regions and plasmids. Their movement is known as transposition and commonly involves the enzyme transposase.

Some transposons carry genes associated with antibiotic resistance. Their movement can contribute to the spread of resistance determinants within and between bacterial genomes.

Insertion sequences are simpler mobile elements that generally contain the genetic information required for their own transposition.

**Evaluation of Distractors:**

- **B:** Incorrect. Ribosome synthesis involves several cellular processes and is not the function of transposons.
- **C:** Incorrect. Bacterial motility is commonly associated with structures such as flagella.
- **D:** Incorrect. The defining characteristic of transposable elements is their capacity to move between DNA locations.

## Question 6: Regulation of Gene Expression in Bacteria

**In the tryptophan (trp) operon of Escherichia coli, what happens when tryptophan is abundant in the cell?**

A. The repressor becomes inactive, allowing continuous transcription.

B. Tryptophan acts as a corepressor, enabling the repressor to bind the operator and reduce transcription.

C. Tryptophan directly destroys the bacterial chromosome.

D. The operon permanently increases the production of tryptophan-synthesising enzymes.

**Correct Answer: B**

**Explanation:**

The trp operon is a repressible operon involved in tryptophan biosynthesis. When intracellular tryptophan levels are high, tryptophan binds to the trp repressor and acts as a corepressor. This interaction enables the repressor to bind the operator region, thereby reducing transcription of the operon.

When tryptophan levels are low, the repressor remains largely inactive, allowing transcription of genes involved in tryptophan biosynthesis.

**Evaluation of Distractors:**

- **A:** Incorrect. Tryptophan activates the repressor rather than inactivating it.
- **C:** Incorrect. Tryptophan does not destroy bacterial DNA.
- **D:** Incorrect. High tryptophan availability generally reduces the expression of genes responsible for its biosynthesis.

## Question 7: Horizontal Gene Transfer and Antibiotic Resistance

**Which process provides a mechanism through which antibiotic resistance genes can spread between bacterial cells?**

A. Horizontal gene transfer.

B. Binary fission only.

C. Cellular respiration.

D. Protein denaturation.

**Correct Answer: A**

**Explanation:**

Horizontal gene transfer enables genetic material to move between bacterial cells independently of parent-to-offspring inheritance. The three major mechanisms are transformation, transduction, and conjugation.

Resistance genes may be carried by plasmids, transposons, integrons, and other mobile genetic elements. Their movement contributes to the dissemination of antimicrobial resistance within bacterial populations and across different bacterial groups.

Horizontal transfer can therefore accelerate the spread of resistance traits, particularly under conditions that favour the survival and multiplication of resistant organisms.

**Evaluation of Distractors:**

- **B:** Incorrect. Binary fission transmits genetic information from parent cells to daughter cells through vertical inheritance. It can propagate resistance but is not horizontal gene transfer.
- **C:** Incorrect. Cellular respiration produces energy and does not directly transfer resistance genes.
- **D:** Incorrect. Protein denaturation changes protein structure and is not a genetic transfer mechanism.

## Question 8: Mutations in Bacterial DNA

**A single nucleotide is inserted into the coding region of a bacterial gene. The insertion is not a multiple of three nucleotides. What is the most likely consequence?**

A. A silent mutation.

B. A frameshift mutation.

C. A synonymous substitution.

D. No possible change in the protein sequence.

**Correct Answer: B**

**Explanation:**

A frameshift mutation occurs when nucleotide insertions or deletions alter the reading frame of a protein-coding sequence. Because bacterial and other protein-coding sequences are read in groups of three nucleotides (codons), an insertion or deletion that is not a multiple of three can change the downstream codon sequence.

This may alter several amino acids and can introduce a premature stop codon, potentially affecting protein function.

**Evaluation of Distractors:**

- **A:** Incorrect. A silent mutation does not change the encoded amino acid.
- **C:** Incorrect. A synonymous substitution is a nucleotide substitution that does not alter the amino acid encoded.
- **D:** Incorrect. A frameshift can substantially alter the translated protein sequence.

## Question 9: Genetic Recombination in Bacteria

**Which bacterial protein plays a central role in homologous recombination by promoting the exchange of DNA strands between homologous DNA molecules?**

A. RecA

B. DNA ligase only

C. RNA polymerase

D. Peptidoglycan synthase

**Correct Answer: A**

**Explanation:**

RecA is a bacterial protein that plays a major role in homologous recombination and DNA repair. It facilitates the search for homologous DNA sequences and promotes strand exchange between compatible DNA molecules.

RecA also participates in the bacterial SOS response, a coordinated cellular response to DNA damage. Its activity contributes to the maintenance of genomic integrity and the repair of damaged DNA.

**Evaluation of Distractors:**

- **B:** Incorrect. DNA ligase joins DNA fragments by forming phosphodiester bonds, but it does not perform the central homology-search and strand-exchange functions of RecA.
- **C:** Incorrect. RNA polymerase synthesises RNA from a DNA template.
- **D:** Incorrect. Peptidoglycan synthase participates in bacterial cell wall synthesis.

## Question 10: CRISPR-Cas Systems in Bacteria

**What is the primary biological function of naturally occurring CRISPR-Cas systems in bacteria and archaea?**

A. Protein synthesis.

B. Adaptive defence against invading genetic elements.

C. ATP production during cellular respiration.

D. Formation of bacterial endospores.

**Correct Answer: B**

**Explanation:**

CRISPR-Cas systems provide adaptive immune defence in many bacteria and archaea against invading genetic elements, including bacteriophages and certain plasmids.

During adaptation, fragments of invading genetic material may be incorporated into CRISPR arrays as spacers. These sequences are subsequently transcribed and processed into CRISPR RNAs (crRNAs). Together with Cas proteins, the crRNAs help recognise complementary foreign nucleic acid sequences during subsequent encounters.

CRISPR-Cas systems are also important in biotechnology because their sequence-recognition properties have been adapted for genome editing and molecular applications.

**Evaluation of Distractors:**

- **A:** Incorrect. Protein synthesis is performed by ribosomes and associated translation machinery.
- **C:** Incorrect. ATP production is associated with metabolic pathways and energy-conserving membrane processes.
- **D:** Incorrect. Endospore formation is a specialised survival process found in certain bacteria and is not the primary function of CRISPR-Cas systems.
