## Question 1: Gram Staining

**Question:** What is the primary structural basis for the difference in Gram-staining reactions between Gram-positive and Gram-negative bacteria?

A. Differences in the thickness and organisation of their peptidoglycan layers and the presence or absence of an outer membrane.

B. Differences in the number of chromosomes in each bacterial cell.

C. Differences in their ability to synthesise ATP.

D. Differences in the presence of mitochondria.

**Correct Answer: A**

**Explanation:**

Gram staining differentiates bacteria based mainly on differences in their cell-envelope structures.

Gram-positive bacteria possess a relatively thick peptidoglycan layer and generally retain the crystal violet–iodine complex during decolourisation, appearing purple.

Gram-negative bacteria possess a thinner peptidoglycan layer and an outer membrane. They are generally decolourised by alcohol or acetone and subsequently take up the counterstain, appearing pink or red.

**Evaluation of Distractors:**

- B is incorrect because chromosome number is not the basis of Gram-stain differentiation.
- C is incorrect because ATP synthesis does not determine Gram-staining reactions.
- D is incorrect because bacteria lack mitochondria.

## Question 2: Autoclaving

**Question:** Which principle explains the effectiveness of autoclaving in microbial sterilisation?

A. Exposure to dry air at room temperature.

B. Application of saturated steam under pressure to achieve temperatures above the normal boiling point of water.

C. Exposure to visible light without heat.

D. Removal of all oxygen from a laboratory environment.

**Correct Answer: B**

**Explanation:**

Autoclaving is a sterilisation method that uses saturated steam under pressure. The increased pressure allows steam to reach temperatures above 100°C.

Moist heat causes microbial proteins and other essential cellular components to become irreversibly damaged. Properly validated autoclave cycles can destroy vegetative microorganisms and bacterial endospores.

The effectiveness of a cycle depends on factors such as temperature, exposure time, steam penetration, load arrangement, and equipment performance.

**Evaluation of Distractors:**

- A describes neither the principle nor the conditions of autoclaving.
- C is incorrect because visible light alone is not the basis of autoclave sterilisation.
- D is incorrect because oxygen removal is not the principal mechanism of autoclaving.

## Question 3: Aseptic Technique

**Question:** What is the main objective of aseptic technique in microbiological laboratory work?

A. To prevent unintended contamination of samples, cultures, personnel, and the laboratory environment.

B. To increase the mutation rate of microorganisms.

C. To guarantee that every microbial culture grows at the same rate.

D. To replace all laboratory sterilisation procedures.

**Correct Answer: A**

**Explanation:**

Aseptic technique comprises practices designed to minimise contamination during microbiological procedures.

It helps maintain the purity of cultures, protects the reliability of experimental results, and reduces the risk of unintended exposure to microorganisms.

Aseptic practices complement, rather than replace, appropriate sterilisation, disinfection, containment, and laboratory biosafety procedures.

**Evaluation of Distractors:**

- B is incorrect because aseptic technique is not intended to increase microbial mutation.
- C is incorrect because growth rate depends on biological and environmental factors.
- D is incorrect because aseptic technique does not eliminate the need for sterilisation.

## Question 4: Serial Dilution and Viable Plate Count

**Question:** A microbial suspension is diluted by a factor of 10⁻⁵. A volume of 0.1 mL from this dilution produces 80 colonies on an agar plate. What is the estimated original concentration?

A. 8.0 × 10⁵ CFU/mL

B. 8.0 × 10⁶ CFU/mL

C. 8.0 × 10⁷ CFU/mL

D. 8.0 × 10⁸ CFU/mL

**Correct Answer: C**

**Explanation:**

The original concentration can be estimated using:

CFU/mL = Number of colonies / (Dilution × Volume plated in mL)

Substituting the values:

CFU/mL = 80 / (10⁻⁵ × 0.1)

CFU/mL = 80 / 10⁻⁶

CFU/mL = 8.0 × 10⁷ CFU/mL

Therefore, option C is correct.

**Evaluation of Distractors:**

- A is incorrect because it underestimates the original concentration by a factor of 100.
- B is incorrect because it underestimates the original concentration by a factor of 10.
- D is incorrect because it overestimates the original concentration by a factor of 10.

## Question 5: Selective and Differential Media

**Question:** Which statement best distinguishes selective media from differential media?

A. Selective media favour the growth of certain microorganisms while inhibiting others, whereas differential media distinguish organisms based on visible biochemical or physiological differences.

B. Selective media are exclusively used for viruses, whereas differential media are exclusively used for fungi.

C. Both media always produce identical growth patterns.

D. Differential media prevent all microbial growth.

**Correct Answer: A**

**Explanation:**

Selective media contain components that favour the growth of particular groups of microorganisms while suppressing others.

Differential media contain indicators or substrates that allow microorganisms to be distinguished based on observable differences, such as colour changes resulting from biochemical reactions.

Some media combine both selective and differential properties.

For example, MacConkey agar is selective for many Gram-negative bacteria and differentiates lactose fermenters from non-lactose fermenters.

**Evaluation of Distractors:**

- B is incorrect because these media are commonly used for bacterial isolation and identification.
- C is incorrect because selective and differential media have different purposes.
- D is incorrect because differential media are designed to support microbial growth while revealing differences.

## Question 6: Polymerase Chain Reaction (PCR)

**Question:** What is the primary function of the denaturation step during conventional PCR?

A. To separate double-stranded DNA into single strands.

B. To enable DNA polymerase to synthesise proteins.

C. To join DNA fragments through peptide bonds.

D. To convert DNA into RNA.

**Correct Answer: A**

**Explanation:**

Conventional PCR involves repeated cycles of three principal stages:

1. Denaturation: separation of double-stranded DNA into single strands.
2. Annealing: binding of primers to complementary target sequences.
3. Extension: synthesis of new DNA strands by a DNA polymerase.

These repeated cycles produce amplification of a selected DNA region.

PCR is widely used in microbial identification, pathogen detection, genetic analysis, and molecular diagnostics.

**Evaluation of Distractors:**

- B is incorrect because DNA polymerases synthesise DNA, not proteins.
- C is incorrect because peptide bonds join amino acids, not DNA fragments.
- D is incorrect because DNA-to-RNA conversion is transcription.

## Question 7: Molecular Identification of Microorganisms

**Question:** Why is the 16S rRNA gene widely used in bacterial identification and phylogenetic studies?

A. It contains conserved and variable regions that support broad bacterial comparison and differentiation.

B. It is present only in pathogenic bacteria.

C. It is responsible for bacterial cell-wall synthesis.

D. It is found exclusively in bacterial plasmids.

**Correct Answer: A**

**Explanation:**

The bacterial 16S rRNA gene encodes ribosomal RNA and contains both conserved and variable regions.

Conserved regions provide suitable sites for broadly applicable primers, while variable regions provide sequence differences that can help distinguish bacterial groups.

Comparison of 16S rRNA gene sequences is widely used for bacterial identification and phylogenetic analysis.

However, closely related species may have highly similar sequences, and additional molecular or phenotypic methods may be required for reliable species-level identification.

**Evaluation of Distractors:**

- B is incorrect because the 16S rRNA gene is present in bacteria generally, not only pathogens.
- C is incorrect because peptidoglycan synthesis involves specialised enzymes rather than the 16S rRNA gene.
- D is incorrect because the 16S rRNA gene is generally chromosomal, not exclusively plasmid-associated.

## Question 8: Antimicrobial Susceptibility Testing

**Question:** In the Kirby–Bauer disc diffusion method, what does the diameter of the inhibition zone represent?

A. The degree of microbial growth inhibition around an antimicrobial disc under defined test conditions.

B. The total number of bacterial chromosomes.

C. The molecular weight of the antimicrobial agent.

D. The exact concentration of the antimicrobial agent inside every bacterial cell.

**Correct Answer: A**

**Explanation:**

The Kirby–Bauer disc diffusion method assesses bacterial susceptibility to antimicrobial agents.

Antimicrobial-impregnated discs are placed on an appropriate inoculated agar surface. As the agent diffuses through the agar, it may inhibit bacterial growth around the disc.

The diameter of the inhibition zone is measured and interpreted using standardised criteria, such as those published by CLSI or EUCAST.

Zone diameter alone does not provide an exact MIC value. Interpretation also depends on the antimicrobial agent, organism, testing conditions, and applicable breakpoints.

**Evaluation of Distractors:**

- B is incorrect because inhibition-zone measurement does not determine chromosome number.
- C is incorrect because molecular weight is a chemical property determined by other analytical methods.
- D is incorrect because the inhibition zone does not directly measure intracellular drug concentration.

## Question 9: Quality Control in Microbiology

**Question:** What is the primary purpose of using reference strains in microbiological laboratory quality control?

A. To assess whether laboratory procedures and test systems produce expected and reliable results.

B. To ensure that every microorganism in a sample is pathogenic.

C. To eliminate the need for laboratory documentation.

D. To guarantee that all test results are identical regardless of experimental conditions.

**Correct Answer: A**

**Explanation:**

Reference strains are characterised microorganisms used to evaluate the performance of laboratory methods and testing systems.

In antimicrobial susceptibility testing, for example, quality-control strains help determine whether the method produces results within established acceptable ranges.

Quality assurance also includes appropriate documentation, equipment maintenance, reagent control, staff competency, and adherence to validated procedures.

**Evaluation of Distractors:**

- B is incorrect because reference strains are not used to classify every microorganism as pathogenic.
- C is incorrect because documentation is an essential component of laboratory quality management.
- D is incorrect because laboratory variation exists and must be monitored rather than assumed absent.

## Question 10: ELISA in Microbial Diagnostics

**Question:** What is the fundamental principle of an enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA)?

A. Specific antigen–antibody recognition is detected through an enzyme-linked signal.

B. Microbial cells are identified exclusively through their colony morphology.

C. DNA is amplified through repeated thermal cycling.

D. Bacterial cell walls are separated according to their peptidoglycan thickness.

**Correct Answer: A**

**Explanation:**

ELISA is an immunological assay based on specific antigen–antibody interactions.

An enzyme is linked to an antibody or detection reagent. Following appropriate binding and washing steps, a substrate is added, producing a measurable signal.

Depending on the assay design, ELISA can detect microbial antigens, host antibodies against infectious agents, or other target molecules.

It is widely applied in infectious disease research, diagnostic testing, and immunological investigations.

**Evaluation of Distractors:**

- B describes culture-based identification rather than ELISA.
- C describes PCR.
- D describes a structural distinction assessed through Gram staining.
