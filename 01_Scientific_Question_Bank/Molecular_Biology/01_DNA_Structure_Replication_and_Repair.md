
# DNA Structure, Replication and Repair

## Overview

This topic assesses understanding of DNA structure, complementary base pairing, DNA replication, and mechanisms responsible for maintaining genome integrity.

## Question 1: DNA Structure

Which feature of DNA is primarily responsible for stabilising the double-helical structure through complementary interactions between the two strands?

A. Peptide bonds between nucleotides  
B. Hydrogen bonds between complementary nitrogenous bases  
C. Phosphodiester bonds between complementary bases  
D. Ionic bonds between deoxyribose molecules  

**Correct Answer: B. Hydrogen bonds between complementary nitrogenous bases**

**Explanation:** Adenine pairs with thymine through two hydrogen bonds, while guanine pairs with cytosine through three hydrogen bonds. These interactions contribute to the stability and specificity of the DNA double helix. Base-stacking interactions also make a substantial contribution to helix stability.

**Distractor Evaluation:**
- A: Peptide bonds link amino acids in proteins.
- C: Phosphodiester bonds form the sugar-phosphate backbone of each DNA strand.
- D: Ionic bonds between deoxyribose molecules do not establish complementary base pairing.

**Quality Assurance Note:** DNA stability should not be attributed exclusively to hydrogen bonding; base stacking is also important.

## Question 2: DNA Replication

Which statement best describes the semiconservative model of DNA replication?

A. Both newly synthesised DNA molecules contain entirely new strands.  
B. Each daughter DNA molecule contains one parental strand and one newly synthesised strand.  
C. One daughter molecule contains only parental DNA, while the other contains only new DNA.  
D. Parental DNA is degraded before two new molecules are synthesised.

**Correct Answer: B. Each daughter DNA molecule contains one parental strand and one newly synthesised strand.**

**Explanation:** During semiconservative replication, the two parental DNA strands separate and each serves as a template for synthesis of a complementary strand. Consequently, each daughter DNA molecule contains one original strand and one newly synthesised strand.

**Distractor Evaluation:**
- A: This describes a conservative model rather than semiconservative replication.
- C: This does not represent the semiconservative model.
- D: Parental DNA strands are retained as templates rather than completely degraded.

**Quality Assurance Note:** The distinction between semiconservative and conservative replication should remain explicit.

## Question 3: DNA Polymerase Activity

DNA polymerases generally synthesise a new DNA strand in which direction?

A. 3′ to 5′  
B. 5′ to 3′  
C. Both directions simultaneously along the same growing strand  
D. Without a directional requirement  

**Correct Answer: B. 5′ to 3′**

**Explanation:** DNA polymerases add incoming nucleotides to the free 3′-hydroxyl group of the growing strand. Therefore, new DNA is synthesised in the 5′ to 3′ direction while the template strand is read in the 3′ to 5′ direction.

**Distractor Evaluation:**
- A: This is the direction in which the template is read, not the direction of new-strand synthesis.
- C: Individual DNA strands are synthesised in the 5′ to 3′ direction.
- D: DNA synthesis requires a defined strand polarity.

**Quality Assurance Note:** Distinguish clearly between the direction of template reading and the direction of strand synthesis.

## Question 4: DNA Replication Enzymes

Which enzyme is primarily responsible for relieving torsional strain ahead of a replication fork?

A. DNA ligase  
B. DNA helicase  
C. Topoisomerase  
D. Primase  

**Correct Answer: C. Topoisomerase**

**Explanation:** DNA unwinding generates torsional strain and positive supercoiling ahead of the replication fork. Topoisomerases relieve this strain by transiently breaking and rejoining DNA strands.

**Distractor Evaluation:**
- A: DNA ligase joins adjacent DNA fragments by forming phosphodiester bonds.
- B: Helicase separates the two DNA strands by disrupting base-pairing interactions.
- D: Primase synthesises short RNA primers required for DNA synthesis.

**Quality Assurance Note:** Helicase and topoisomerase have different but complementary roles during DNA replication.

## Question 5: Leading and Lagging Strands

Why is the lagging strand synthesised discontinuously during DNA replication?

A. DNA polymerase cannot form phosphodiester bonds on the lagging strand.  
B. The two DNA template strands have opposite orientations, while DNA polymerase synthesises DNA only in the 5′ to 3′ direction.  
C. The lagging strand contains RNA instead of DNA.  
D. DNA ligase prevents continuous synthesis.

**Correct Answer: B. The two DNA template strands have opposite orientations, while DNA polymerase synthesises DNA only in the 5′ to 3′ direction.**

**Explanation:** Because DNA strands are antiparallel, the lagging-strand template is oriented so that synthesis proceeds discontinuously away from the advancing replication fork. Short DNA segments called Okazaki fragments are produced and subsequently joined.

**Distractor Evaluation:**
- A: DNA polymerase can synthesise DNA on both leading and lagging strands.
- C: The lagging strand is DNA; RNA primers are used to initiate its synthesis.
- D: DNA ligase joins Okazaki fragments but does not prevent continuous synthesis.

**Quality Assurance Note:** The explanation should connect strand polarity with the directional limitation of DNA polymerase.

## Question 6: DNA Replication Fidelity

Which property of many replicative DNA polymerases contributes directly to replication fidelity by removing incorrectly incorporated nucleotides?

A. 5′ to 3′ exonuclease activity  
B. 3′ to 5′ exonuclease proofreading activity  
C. RNA polymerase activity  
D. DNA ligase activity  

**Correct Answer: B. 3′ to 5′ exonuclease proofreading activity**

**Explanation:** Many replicative DNA polymerases possess 3′ to 5′ exonuclease activity. This proofreading function removes incorrectly incorporated nucleotides from the growing strand, allowing DNA synthesis to continue with improved accuracy.

**Distractor Evaluation:**
- A: 5′ to 3′ exonuclease activity has other functions in certain DNA polymerases, including primer removal in some systems.
- C: RNA polymerases synthesise RNA rather than proofreading newly synthesised DNA in this manner.
- D: DNA ligase seals breaks in the DNA backbone.

**Quality Assurance Note:** Not all DNA polymerases possess identical proofreading capabilities.

## Question 7: DNA Repair

Which DNA repair pathway primarily removes bulky, helix-distorting lesions such as those produced by ultraviolet radiation?

A. Mismatch repair  
B. Nucleotide excision repair  
C. Base excision repair  
D. Homologous recombination  

**Correct Answer: B. Nucleotide excision repair**

**Explanation:** Nucleotide excision repair recognises bulky DNA lesions that distort the double helix. A short segment containing the damaged region is removed, followed by DNA resynthesis and ligation.

**Distractor Evaluation:**
- A: Mismatch repair corrects replication-associated base mismatches and small insertion or deletion loops.
- C: Base excision repair primarily removes small, chemically modified or inappropriate bases.
- D: Homologous recombination repairs certain double-strand breaks using homologous DNA as a template.

**Quality Assurance Note:** Ultraviolet-induced pyrimidine dimers are a standard example of lesions repaired through nucleotide excision repair.

## Question 8: Mismatch Repair

What is the principal function of the mismatch repair system?

A. Removal of incorrectly paired bases that escape DNA polymerase proofreading  
B. Removal of all RNA primers from newly synthesised DNA  
C. Repair of every type of double-strand DNA break  
D. Prevention of DNA transcription  

**Correct Answer: A. Removal of incorrectly paired bases that escape DNA polymerase proofreading**

**Explanation:** Mismatch repair recognises and corrects base-pair mismatches and small insertion or deletion loops that remain after DNA replication. This pathway provides an additional layer of replication fidelity.

**Distractor Evaluation:**
- B: RNA primer removal is performed by other replication-associated enzymes.
- C: Double-strand break repair involves pathways such as homologous recombination and non-homologous end joining.
- D: Mismatch repair is not a mechanism for preventing transcription.

**Quality Assurance Note:** Mismatch repair complements, rather than replaces, the proofreading activity of DNA polymerases.

## Question 9: Telomeres

What is the principal biological significance of telomeres in eukaryotic chromosomes?

A. They encode all proteins required for DNA replication.  
B. They protect chromosome ends from degradation and inappropriate recognition as DNA breaks.  
C. They initiate transcription of every gene.  
D. They permanently prevent chromosome replication.

**Correct Answer: B. They protect chromosome ends from degradation and inappropriate recognition as DNA breaks.**

**Explanation:** Telomeres consist of repetitive DNA sequences and associated proteins that protect chromosome ends. They help maintain chromosome stability and address problems associated with complete replication of linear chromosome ends.

**Distractor Evaluation:**
- A: Telomeres are not universal protein-coding regions.
- C: Telomeres do not initiate transcription of every gene.
- D: Telomeres do not prevent chromosome replication.

**Quality Assurance Note:** Telomere shortening and telomerase activity vary among cell types and biological conditions.

## Question 10: Homologous Recombination

Which statement best describes homologous recombination as a DNA repair mechanism?

A. It repairs DNA exclusively by removing damaged nitrogenous bases.  
B. It uses a homologous DNA sequence as a template to support accurate repair of certain double-strand breaks.  
C. It joins DNA fragments without requiring sequence homology.  
D. It prevents all mutations during DNA replication.

**Correct Answer: B. It uses a homologous DNA sequence as a template to support accurate repair of certain double-strand breaks.**

**Explanation:** Homologous recombination uses an undamaged homologous DNA sequence, commonly the sister chromatid, as a template for repairing certain double-strand breaks. It is an important mechanism for maintaining genome integrity.

**Distractor Evaluation:**
- A: Removal of damaged bases is characteristic of base excision repair.
- C: Joining DNA ends without an extended homologous template is characteristic of non-homologous end joining.
- D: No repair mechanism prevents every possible mutation.

**Quality Assurance Note:** Homologous recombination is generally more accurate than repair through direct end joining when an appropriate homologous template is available.
