
# RNA Transcription and Processing

## Overview

This topic assesses understanding of RNA synthesis, transcriptional regulation, RNA processing, and the functional roles of different RNA molecules in eukaryotic and prokaryotic cells.

## Question 1: RNA Polymerase

Which enzyme is primarily responsible for synthesising messenger RNA (mRNA) from a DNA template in eukaryotic cells?

A. DNA polymerase I  
B. RNA polymerase I  
C. RNA polymerase II  
D. RNA polymerase III  

**Correct Answer: C. RNA polymerase II**

**Explanation:** In eukaryotic cells, RNA polymerase II synthesises precursor messenger RNA (pre-mRNA), which is subsequently processed to produce mature mRNA. RNA polymerase I mainly synthesises the large ribosomal RNA precursors, while RNA polymerase III synthesises transfer RNA and 5S ribosomal RNA.

**Distractor Evaluation:**
- A: DNA polymerase I participates in DNA synthesis and primer processing in bacteria.
- B: RNA polymerase I primarily synthesises large ribosomal RNA precursors.
- D: RNA polymerase III synthesises tRNA, 5S rRNA, and certain other small RNAs.

**Quality Assurance Note:** The question specifically refers to eukaryotic mRNA synthesis because RNA polymerase organisation differs between prokaryotes and eukaryotes.

## Question 2: Transcription Initiation

What is the principal function of a promoter during transcription?

A. It terminates translation.  
B. It provides a DNA sequence where transcription machinery assembles to initiate transcription.  
C. It removes introns from precursor RNA.  
D. It catalyses the formation of peptide bonds.

**Correct Answer: B. It provides a DNA sequence where transcription machinery assembles to initiate transcription.**

**Explanation:** A promoter is a regulatory DNA region that helps recruit and position RNA polymerase and associated transcription factors. It influences the location, direction, and regulation of transcription initiation.

**Distractor Evaluation:**
- A: Translation termination occurs when a stop codon is recognised by the translation machinery.
- C: Intron removal is performed by the RNA-splicing machinery.
- D: Peptide bond formation occurs during protein translation.

**Quality Assurance Note:** Promoters are DNA regulatory sequences, not RNA-processing enzymes.

## Question 3: Template Strand

During transcription, which DNA strand is used by RNA polymerase as the template?

A. The coding strand only  
B. The non-template strand only  
C. The template strand, which is read in the 3′ to 5′ direction  
D. Both strands are always transcribed simultaneously into the same RNA molecule.

**Correct Answer: C. The template strand, which is read in the 3′ to 5′ direction**

**Explanation:** RNA polymerase reads the DNA template strand in the 3′ to 5′ direction while synthesising RNA in the 5′ to 3′ direction. The resulting RNA sequence is complementary to the template strand and generally resembles the coding strand, except that uracil replaces thymine.

**Distractor Evaluation:**
- A: The coding strand is not the strand directly read by RNA polymerase.
- B: The non-template strand is another name for the coding strand.
- D: A particular transcription unit generally uses one DNA strand as its template, although different genes may be transcribed from opposite strands.

**Quality Assurance Note:** Strand orientation should be stated explicitly to avoid confusion between coding and template strands.

## Question 4: RNA Processing

Which modification is normally added to the 5′ end of eukaryotic precursor mRNA?

A. A polyadenylate tail  
B. A 7-methylguanosine cap  
C. A long sequence of thymine residues  
D. A peptide chain  

**Correct Answer: B. A 7-methylguanosine cap**

**Explanation:** Eukaryotic pre-mRNA undergoes 5′ capping through the addition of a modified guanosine nucleotide. The cap contributes to mRNA stability, supports nuclear export, and assists recognition by translation initiation machinery.

**Distractor Evaluation:**
- A: The polyadenylate tail is added to the 3′ end of most eukaryotic mRNAs.
- C: Thymine is a DNA base; RNA contains uracil instead.
- D: Peptide chains are products of translation, not RNA processing.

**Quality Assurance Note:** The 5′ cap and 3′ poly(A) tail are distinct processing modifications with related protective and regulatory functions.

## Question 5: RNA Splicing

What is the main function of RNA splicing in eukaryotic gene expression?

A. Removal of introns and joining of exons in precursor mRNA  
B. Replication of DNA before cell division  
C. Addition of amino acids to transfer RNA  
D. Conversion of ribosomal RNA into DNA  

**Correct Answer: A. Removal of introns and joining of exons in precursor mRNA**

**Explanation:** RNA splicing removes non-coding intronic sequences from precursor mRNA and joins the remaining exons. This process is mainly carried out by the spliceosome, a complex containing small nuclear ribonucleoproteins and associated proteins.

**Distractor Evaluation:**
- B: DNA replication is a separate process involving DNA polymerases and other replication proteins.
- C: Amino acid attachment to tRNA is catalysed by aminoacyl-tRNA synthetases.
- D: Ribosomal RNA is not routinely converted into DNA as part of RNA processing.

**Quality Assurance Note:** Some introns can undergo self-splicing, but spliceosome-mediated splicing is the major pathway for many eukaryotic nuclear pre-mRNAs.

## Question 6: Alternative Splicing

Which statement best describes alternative RNA splicing?

A. It produces different mature mRNA transcripts from the same precursor RNA by varying exon inclusion.  
B. It converts every intron into a protein.  
C. It prevents transcription from occurring.  
D. It permanently changes the DNA sequence of a gene.

**Correct Answer: A. It produces different mature mRNA transcripts from the same precursor RNA by varying exon inclusion.**

**Explanation:** Alternative splicing allows different combinations of exons to be retained in mature mRNA molecules derived from the same precursor transcript. This increases transcript and protein diversity without requiring a corresponding increase in gene number.

**Distractor Evaluation:**
- B: Introns are generally removed during splicing and are not automatically converted into proteins.
- C: Alternative splicing occurs after transcription has produced precursor RNA.
- D: Alternative splicing changes RNA processing patterns rather than the underlying genomic DNA sequence.

**Quality Assurance Note:** Alternative splicing can generate multiple protein isoforms, although not every transcript necessarily produces a stable or functional protein.

## Question 7: Transcription Termination

What is the principal outcome of transcription termination?

A. RNA polymerase stops RNA synthesis and releases the RNA transcript.  
B. Ribosomes immediately degrade the DNA template.  
C. DNA polymerase begins translating the transcript.  
D. All introns are converted into exons.

**Correct Answer: A. RNA polymerase stops RNA synthesis and releases the RNA transcript.**

**Explanation:** Transcription termination marks the end of RNA synthesis at a transcription unit. The RNA transcript is released, and RNA polymerase disengages from the DNA template. The mechanisms differ between prokaryotes and eukaryotes.

**Distractor Evaluation:**
- B: Ribosomes participate in translation and do not degrade DNA templates.
- C: DNA polymerases synthesise DNA; they do not translate RNA.
- D: Intron and exon processing is not the defining event of transcription termination.

**Quality Assurance Note:** Avoid assuming that prokaryotic and eukaryotic transcription termination use identical mechanisms.

## Question 8: RNA Stability

Which feature generally contributes to the stability of mature eukaryotic mRNA?

A. Absence of all RNA-processing modifications  
B. Presence of a 5′ cap and a 3′ poly(A) tail  
C. Permanent attachment to genomic DNA  
D. Conversion of uracil into thymine  

**Correct Answer: B. Presence of a 5′ cap and a 3′ poly(A) tail**

**Explanation:** The 5′ cap and 3′ poly(A) tail protect many eukaryotic mRNAs from degradation and contribute to efficient nuclear export and translation. Their effects on stability and translation depend on interactions with associated proteins and cellular regulatory mechanisms.

**Distractor Evaluation:**
- A: Processing modifications generally contribute to the maturation and protection of eukaryotic mRNA.
- C: Mature mRNA is not permanently attached to genomic DNA.
- D: Uracil is a normal RNA base and is not generally converted into thymine during mRNA maturation.

**Quality Assurance Note:** The stability of individual mRNAs also depends on sequence elements, RNA-binding proteins, and cellular conditions.

## Question 9: Prokaryotic Transcription

Which characteristic is commonly associated with transcription in prokaryotic cells?

A. Transcription and translation can occur simultaneously in the cytoplasm.  
B. All RNA transcripts undergo extensive spliceosomal processing.  
C. Transcription occurs exclusively inside a membrane-bound nucleus.  
D. Every bacterial gene contains multiple introns.

**Correct Answer: A. Transcription and translation can occur simultaneously in the cytoplasm.**

**Explanation:** Prokaryotic cells lack a membrane-bound nucleus. Consequently, ribosomes can begin translating an mRNA while RNA polymerase is still transcribing it. This coupling allows rapid coordination of gene expression.

**Distractor Evaluation:**
- B: Extensive spliceosomal processing is characteristic of many eukaryotic pre-mRNAs, not a general feature of bacterial transcripts.
- C: Prokaryotes do not possess a membrane-bound nucleus.
- D: Introns are not a universal feature of bacterial genes.

**Quality Assurance Note:** The statement is framed as a common characteristic rather than an absolute rule because some prokaryotic RNA-processing mechanisms exist.

## Question 10: Non-Coding RNA

Which RNA molecule primarily functions in delivering amino acids to the ribosome during protein synthesis?

A. Messenger RNA  
B. Ribosomal RNA  
C. Transfer RNA  
D. Small nuclear RNA  

**Correct Answer: C. Transfer RNA**

**Explanation:** Transfer RNA acts as an adaptor molecule during translation. Each tRNA carries a specific amino acid and contains an anticodon that recognises a complementary codon in mRNA. This helps ensure that amino acids are incorporated into the growing polypeptide in the correct sequence.

**Distractor Evaluation:**
- A: mRNA carries the codon sequence that specifies the amino acid order.
- B: rRNA forms the structural and catalytic core of the ribosome.
- D: Small nuclear RNA participates in spliceosome function and pre-mRNA processing.

**Quality Assurance Note:** The roles of mRNA, tRNA, rRNA, and snRNA should remain clearly distinguished.
