
# Protein Synthesis and Translation

## Overview

This topic assesses understanding of the molecular mechanisms of protein synthesis, including genetic code interpretation, ribosomal function, translation initiation, elongation, termination, and post-translational processing.

## Question 1: Genetic Code

Which statement best describes the genetic code?

A. Each amino acid is encoded by exactly one codon.  
B. Each codon consists of two nucleotides.  
C. The genetic code is degenerate because multiple codons can specify the same amino acid.  
D. Every codon specifies an amino acid.

**Correct Answer: C. The genetic code is degenerate because multiple codons can specify the same amino acid.**

**Explanation:** The genetic code consists of three-nucleotide codons that specify amino acids or translation termination signals. It is described as degenerate because most amino acids are encoded by more than one codon. However, each codon has a defined meaning within a given genetic code.

**Distractor Evaluation:**
- A: Several amino acids are encoded by multiple codons.
- B: A codon consists of three nucleotides, not two.
- D: Three codons generally function as stop signals rather than specifying amino acids.

**Quality Assurance Note:** Distinguish genetic-code degeneracy from ambiguity. A codon does not ordinarily specify several different amino acids within the same genetic code.

## Question 2: Translation Initiation

Which codon most commonly serves as the initiation codon in the standard genetic code?

A. UAA  
B. AUG  
C. UAG  
D. UGA  

**Correct Answer: B. AUG**

**Explanation:** AUG is the most common initiation codon. It specifies methionine in eukaryotes and initiates bacterial translation using a specialised initiator tRNA carrying formylmethionine. Alternative initiation codons can occur in some biological systems.

**Distractor Evaluation:**
- A: UAA is a stop codon.
- C: UAG is a stop codon.
- D: UGA is a stop codon in the standard genetic code.

**Quality Assurance Note:** The initiation role of AUG should be distinguished from its role as a methionine codon during internal translation.

## Question 3: Ribosomal Function

What is the primary role of ribosomal RNA (rRNA) in protein synthesis?

A. It stores hereditary information permanently.  
B. It contributes to ribosome structure and catalyses peptide bond formation.  
C. It transports amino acids from the cytoplasm to the nucleus.  
D. It removes introns from DNA.

**Correct Answer: B. It contributes to ribosome structure and catalyses peptide bond formation.**

**Explanation:** Ribosomal RNA forms a major structural and functional component of ribosomes. In the large ribosomal subunit, rRNA provides the catalytic activity responsible for peptide bond formation, making the ribosome a ribozyme.

**Distractor Evaluation:**
- A: DNA is the principal long-term repository of hereditary information in most cellular organisms.
- C: Transfer RNA transports amino acids to the ribosome.
- D: RNA splicing removes introns from precursor RNA; rRNA does not remove introns from DNA.

**Quality Assurance Note:** Ribosomes are ribonucleoprotein complexes containing both rRNA and proteins.

## Question 4: Transfer RNA

Which region of a transfer RNA molecule recognises the complementary codon on messenger RNA?

A. Anticodon loop  
B. Poly(A) tail  
C. Promoter region  
D. Shine-Dalgarno sequence  

**Correct Answer: A. Anticodon loop**

**Explanation:** The anticodon loop contains a three-nucleotide anticodon that base-pairs with a complementary codon on mRNA during translation. This interaction helps ensure that the correct amino acid is incorporated into the growing polypeptide.

**Distractor Evaluation:**
- B: A poly(A) tail is associated with the 3′ end of many eukaryotic mRNAs.
- C: A promoter is a DNA regulatory sequence involved in transcription initiation.
- D: The Shine-Dalgarno sequence is a bacterial mRNA ribosome-binding site.

**Quality Assurance Note:** Codon-anticodon pairing is generally antiparallel and may involve wobble pairing at the third codon position.

## Question 5: Translation Elongation

During translation elongation, what is the main function of elongation factors?

A. They assist the delivery and movement of tRNAs and ribosomes during protein synthesis.  
B. They remove introns from mRNA.  
C. They synthesise DNA primers.  
D. They degrade all newly formed proteins.

**Correct Answer: A. They assist the delivery and movement of tRNAs and ribosomes during protein synthesis.**

**Explanation:** Elongation factors facilitate several stages of translation, including the delivery of aminoacyl-tRNAs to the ribosome and ribosomal translocation along the mRNA. Some elongation factors use GTP hydrolysis to support these processes.

**Distractor Evaluation:**
- B: Introns are removed during RNA processing.
- C: DNA primers are produced during DNA replication.
- D: Protein degradation is carried out by cellular proteolytic systems.

**Quality Assurance Note:** Elongation factors have different functions in bacteria and eukaryotes, although their overall roles are comparable.

## Question 6: Peptide Bond Formation

Which ribosomal component is primarily responsible for catalysing peptide bond formation?

A. The small ribosomal subunit protein components alone  
B. Ribosomal RNA in the large subunit  
C. Messenger RNA  
D. The aminoacyl-tRNA synthetase enzyme  

**Correct Answer: B. Ribosomal RNA in the large subunit**

**Explanation:** The peptidyl transferase centre of the large ribosomal subunit catalyses peptide bond formation. This catalytic activity is principally associated with ribosomal RNA rather than being performed by a conventional protein enzyme.

**Distractor Evaluation:**
- A: Ribosomal proteins support ribosome structure and function but are not the primary catalytic component.
- C: mRNA provides the codon sequence that directs amino acid order.
- D: Aminoacyl-tRNA synthetases attach specific amino acids to their corresponding tRNAs.

**Quality Assurance Note:** The ribosome is an important example of catalytic RNA activity in biological systems.

## Question 7: Translation Termination

What happens when a stop codon enters the ribosomal A site?

A. A new aminoacyl-tRNA always binds to it.  
B. A release factor recognises the stop codon and promotes release of the polypeptide.  
C. DNA replication begins immediately.  
D. The ribosome permanently attaches to the DNA template.

**Correct Answer: B. A release factor recognises the stop codon and promotes release of the polypeptide.**

**Explanation:** Stop codons are recognised by protein release factors rather than conventional aminoacyl-tRNAs. Release-factor activity promotes hydrolysis of the bond linking the completed polypeptide to the tRNA in the ribosomal P site, allowing translation to terminate.

**Distractor Evaluation:**
- A: Standard stop codons are not normally recognised by conventional aminoacyl-tRNAs.
- C: Translation termination does not directly initiate DNA replication.
- D: Ribosomes translate RNA and do not permanently attach to DNA.

**Quality Assurance Note:** The standard stop codons are UAA, UAG, and UGA, although biological exceptions such as recoding occur.

## Question 8: Aminoacyl-tRNA Synthetases

What is the principal function of aminoacyl-tRNA synthetases?

A. They attach the appropriate amino acid to its corresponding tRNA.  
B. They synthesise mRNA from DNA.  
C. They catalyse DNA strand separation.  
D. They remove stop codons from mRNA.

**Correct Answer: A. They attach the appropriate amino acid to its corresponding tRNA.**

**Explanation:** Aminoacyl-tRNA synthetases recognise particular amino acids and their corresponding tRNAs. They use ATP to attach the amino acid to the 3′ end of the appropriate tRNA, producing an aminoacyl-tRNA.

**Distractor Evaluation:**
- B: RNA polymerase synthesises RNA from a DNA template.
- C: DNA helicases separate DNA strands during replication and other DNA transactions.
- D: Stop codons are recognised during translation termination rather than removed by aminoacyl-tRNA synthetases.

**Quality Assurance Note:** Accurate aminoacylation is essential for translation fidelity because the ribosome primarily checks codon-anticodon pairing rather than directly verifying the attached amino acid.

## Question 9: Polyribosomes

What is a polyribosome (polysome)?

A. A group of DNA molecules attached to a chromosome  
B. Multiple ribosomes simultaneously translating the same mRNA molecule  
C. A collection of amino acids stored in the nucleus  
D. A complex responsible for removing introns from pre-mRNA  

**Correct Answer: B. Multiple ribosomes simultaneously translating the same mRNA molecule**

**Explanation:** A polyribosome consists of several ribosomes translating one mRNA molecule at the same time. This arrangement allows a cell to produce multiple copies of a protein from a single transcript efficiently.

**Distractor Evaluation:**
- A: A polyribosome is a translation complex, not a DNA structure.
- C: Amino acids are not stored as a polyribosome.
- D: The spliceosome is primarily responsible for spliceosome-mediated intron removal.

**Quality Assurance Note:** Polyribosomes can occur in both prokaryotic and eukaryotic cells.

## Question 10: Post-Translational Modification

Which of the following is an example of a post-translational modification that can regulate protein activity?

A. Phosphorylation  
B. DNA replication  
C. Transcription initiation  
D. RNA splicing  

**Correct Answer: A. Phosphorylation**

**Explanation:** Protein phosphorylation involves the addition of a phosphate group, commonly to serine, threonine, or tyrosine residues in eukaryotic proteins. Protein kinases catalyse phosphorylation, while protein phosphatases remove phosphate groups. These processes regulate many cellular activities, including signalling, metabolism, and protein function.

**Distractor Evaluation:**
- B: DNA replication duplicates genetic material and is not a protein modification.
- C: Transcription initiation is part of gene expression at the DNA-to-RNA stage.
- D: RNA splicing is an RNA-processing event.

**Quality Assurance Note:** Phosphorylation can activate or inhibit a protein depending on the protein and the specific modification site.
