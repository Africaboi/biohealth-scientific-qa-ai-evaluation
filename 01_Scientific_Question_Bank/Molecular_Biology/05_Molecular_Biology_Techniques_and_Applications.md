
# Molecular Biology Techniques and Applications

## Overview

This topic assesses understanding of important molecular biology techniques used in research, genetic analysis, molecular diagnostics, and biotechnology. It covers PCR, gel electrophoresis, DNA sequencing, recombinant DNA technology, hybridisation, and gene-expression analysis.

## Question 1: Polymerase Chain Reaction

What is the primary purpose of polymerase chain reaction (PCR)?

A. To amplify a specific DNA sequence in vitro  
B. To translate messenger RNA into protein  
C. To separate proteins according to their molecular weight  
D. To destroy all DNA molecules in a sample  

**Correct Answer: A. To amplify a specific DNA sequence in vitro**

**Explanation:** PCR is a molecular technique used to generate many copies of a selected DNA region. It involves repeated cycles of DNA denaturation, primer annealing, and extension by a thermostable DNA polymerase.

**Distractor Evaluation:**
- B: Translation is the process by which ribosomes synthesise proteins from mRNA.
- C: SDS-PAGE is commonly used to separate proteins according to molecular weight.
- D: PCR amplifies selected DNA sequences rather than destroying DNA.

**Quality Assurance Note:** PCR amplifies DNA. When RNA is the starting material, reverse transcription is first required to produce complementary DNA.

## Question 2: PCR Components

Which component provides the free 3′-hydroxyl group required for DNA polymerase to begin extending a newly synthesised DNA strand?

A. DNA primer  
B. Sodium chloride  
C. Ethidium bromide  
D. Agarose  

**Correct Answer: A. DNA primer**

**Explanation:** DNA polymerases require an existing primer-template junction with a free 3′-hydroxyl group. The primer provides this starting point for nucleotide addition during DNA synthesis.

**Distractor Evaluation:**
- B: Sodium chloride can influence reaction conditions but does not provide the required starting 3′-hydroxyl group.
- C: Ethidium bromide is a DNA-binding fluorescent stain historically used for visualising nucleic acids.
- D: Agarose is used as a matrix for gel electrophoresis.

**Quality Assurance Note:** PCR requires primers, template DNA, nucleotides, a suitable DNA polymerase, and an appropriate reaction buffer.

## Question 3: Gel Electrophoresis

What is the principal basis for separating DNA fragments during standard agarose gel electrophoresis?

A. Differences in DNA colour  
B. Differences in fragment size  
C. Differences in amino acid composition  
D. Differences in protein folding  

**Correct Answer: B. Differences in fragment size**

**Explanation:** DNA has a negatively charged phosphate backbone and migrates towards the positive electrode in an electric field. In agarose gels, smaller DNA fragments generally migrate faster and farther than larger fragments under comparable conditions.

**Distractor Evaluation:**
- A: DNA fragments are not separated according to their natural colour.
- C: DNA does not consist of amino acids.
- D: Protein folding is not the basis of DNA fragment separation in agarose gels.

**Quality Assurance Note:** DNA migration is influenced by fragment size, gel concentration, DNA conformation, and electrophoresis conditions.

## Question 4: DNA Sequencing

What is the main objective of DNA sequencing?

A. To determine the order of nucleotides in a DNA molecule  
B. To measure the total protein concentration in a sample  
C. To identify the colour of a bacterial colony  
D. To determine the rate of cellular respiration directly  

**Correct Answer: A. To determine the order of nucleotides in a DNA molecule**

**Explanation:** DNA sequencing determines the nucleotide order within a DNA molecule. Sequencing information can be used to identify genetic variations, investigate evolutionary relationships, characterise genes, and support molecular diagnostic research.

**Distractor Evaluation:**
- B: Protein assays are used to estimate protein concentration.
- C: Colony colour may be assessed through microbiological or biochemical methods but is not the purpose of DNA sequencing.
- D: Cellular respiration is investigated using metabolic and biochemical measurements.

**Quality Assurance Note:** DNA sequencing determines nucleotide sequence; interpretation of biological function requires additional analysis.

## Question 5: Sanger Sequencing

Which feature distinguishes the chain-termination method used in Sanger DNA sequencing?

A. Incorporation of dideoxynucleotides that terminate DNA-chain extension  
B. Complete removal of all DNA primers before synthesis  
C. Use of ribosomes to synthesise DNA  
D. Conversion of DNA into amino acids  

**Correct Answer: A. Incorporation of dideoxynucleotides that terminate DNA-chain extension**

**Explanation:** Sanger sequencing uses dideoxynucleotides (ddNTPs), which lack the 3′-hydroxyl group needed for further nucleotide addition. Their incorporation terminates DNA-chain extension, producing fragments that can be analysed to determine the sequence.

**Distractor Evaluation:**
- B: Primers are required to initiate DNA synthesis.
- C: Ribosomes synthesise proteins rather than DNA.
- D: DNA is not converted into amino acids.

**Quality Assurance Note:** The absence of a 3′-hydroxyl group is the key chemical feature responsible for chain termination.

## Question 6: Recombinant DNA Technology

What is recombinant DNA?

A. DNA formed by joining genetic material from different sources  
B. DNA that contains no nucleotide bases  
C. RNA produced exclusively during translation  
D. A protein formed from several amino acids  

**Correct Answer: A. DNA formed by joining genetic material from different sources**

**Explanation:** Recombinant DNA contains DNA sequences combined from different sources through molecular biology techniques. This technology is used in research, biotechnology, and the production of certain therapeutic proteins.

**Distractor Evaluation:**
- B: DNA necessarily contains nucleotide bases.
- C: RNA is chemically distinct from DNA.
- D: Proteins consist of amino acids and are not recombinant DNA molecules.

**Quality Assurance Note:** Recombinant DNA technology involves the construction and analysis of DNA molecules containing selected sequences.

## Question 7: Restriction Enzymes

What is the primary function of restriction endonucleases in molecular biology?

A. To recognise particular DNA sequences and cleave DNA at or near those sites  
B. To synthesise proteins from mRNA  
C. To attach amino acids to transfer RNA  
D. To remove phosphate groups from proteins  

**Correct Answer: A. To recognise particular DNA sequences and cleave DNA at or near those sites**

**Explanation:** Restriction endonucleases recognise specific DNA sequences and cut DNA molecules at defined positions. They are important tools in recombinant DNA technology, DNA mapping, and molecular analysis.

**Distractor Evaluation:**
- B: Protein synthesis is performed by ribosomes.
- C: Aminoacyl-tRNA synthetases attach amino acids to tRNA.
- D: Protein phosphatases remove phosphate groups from proteins.

**Quality Assurance Note:** Restriction enzymes differ in their recognition sequences and cleavage patterns. Not all produce sticky ends; some produce blunt ends.

## Question 8: Southern Blotting

Which molecular biology technique is specifically used to detect a particular DNA sequence within a complex DNA sample?

A. Northern blotting  
B. Western blotting  
C. Southern blotting  
D. ELISA  

**Correct Answer: C. Southern blotting**

**Explanation:** Southern blotting detects specific DNA sequences through hybridisation with a labelled complementary probe. DNA fragments are separated, transferred to a membrane, and detected using the probe.

**Distractor Evaluation:**
- A: Northern blotting is used to detect specific RNA molecules.
- B: Western blotting is used to detect specific proteins.
- D: ELISA is an immunological assay commonly used to detect or quantify antigens or antibodies.

**Quality Assurance Note:** The three blotting methods should be distinguished clearly: Southern for DNA, Northern for RNA, and Western for proteins.

## Question 9: Reverse Transcription PCR

What is the role of reverse transcriptase in reverse transcription PCR?

A. It converts RNA into complementary DNA (cDNA).  
B. It converts proteins into DNA.  
C. It degrades all RNA molecules in a sample.  
D. It joins two separate chromosomes.

**Correct Answer: A. It converts RNA into complementary DNA (cDNA).**

**Explanation:** Reverse transcriptase is an enzyme that synthesises complementary DNA using RNA as a template. In reverse transcription PCR workflows, cDNA is subsequently amplified using PCR. This approach is useful for studying RNA transcripts and gene expression.

**Distractor Evaluation:**
- B: Reverse transcriptase does not convert proteins into DNA.
- C: Its primary function is DNA synthesis using an RNA template.
- D: Chromosome joining is not the function of reverse transcriptase.

**Quality Assurance Note:** Reverse transcription PCR and quantitative reverse transcription PCR are related but distinct approaches; quantitative measurement requires an appropriate real-time detection system.

## Question 10: Molecular Diagnostics

Which statement best describes the application of molecular diagnostics?

A. Detection of disease-associated genetic material or molecular markers to support diagnosis  
B. Measurement of body temperature alone  
C. Identification of organisms exclusively through their visible colony colour  
D. Determination of blood pressure without laboratory analysis  

**Correct Answer: A. Detection of disease-associated genetic material or molecular markers to support diagnosis**

**Explanation:** Molecular diagnostics uses techniques such as PCR, nucleic acid hybridisation, and sequencing to detect genetic material or molecular markers associated with infectious diseases, inherited conditions, and other biological states. Results must be interpreted alongside clinical information and appropriate quality controls.

**Distractor Evaluation:**
- B: Temperature measurement is a clinical observation, not molecular diagnosis.
- C: Colony colour may provide useful microbiological information but does not constitute molecular diagnosis.
- D: Blood pressure measurement is a physiological assessment rather than a molecular diagnostic method.

**Quality Assurance Note:** A molecular test result should be interpreted according to the assay's validated performance, limitations, and intended clinical application.
