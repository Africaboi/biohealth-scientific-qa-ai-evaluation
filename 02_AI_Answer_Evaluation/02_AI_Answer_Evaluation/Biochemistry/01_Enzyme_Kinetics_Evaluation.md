## 2. Scientific Question

An enzyme-catalysed reaction follows Michaelis–Menten kinetics. The measured maximum reaction velocity (Vmax) is 100 µmol/min, while the Michaelis constant (Km) is 5 mmol/L.

What is the reaction velocity when the substrate concentration is 5 mmol/L?

## 3. AI-Generated Answer (Illustrative Sample)

**AI Response:**

The reaction velocity is 100 µmol/min because the substrate concentration is equal to the Michaelis constant. When substrate concentration reaches Km, the enzyme becomes fully saturated and operates at its maximum velocity.

## 4. Scientific Assessment

The response contains a significant conceptual error.

Under Michaelis–Menten kinetics, the Michaelis constant (Km) is the substrate concentration at which the reaction velocity is half of Vmax, provided the enzyme follows the standard Michaelis–Menten model.

Therefore, when substrate concentration equals Km, the enzyme does not operate at its maximum velocity.

The correct reaction velocity is 50 µmol/min.

## 5. Identification of Scientific Errors

### Error 1: Incorrect interpretation of Km

**AI Claim:** When substrate concentration reaches Km, the enzyme becomes fully saturated.

**Assessment:** Incorrect.

**Correction:** Km is the substrate concentration at which the reaction velocity is half of Vmax under Michaelis–Menten conditions. It is not the concentration at which the enzyme becomes fully saturated.

### Error 2: Incorrect reaction velocity

**AI Claim:** The reaction velocity is 100 µmol/min.

**Assessment:** Incorrect.

**Correction:** The reaction velocity is 50 µmol/min because the substrate concentration equals Km.

## 6. Evidence-Based Scientific Correction

The Michaelis–Menten equation is:

v = (Vmax × [S]) / (Km + [S])

Where:

- v = Initial reaction velocity
- Vmax = Maximum reaction velocity
- [S] = Substrate concentration
- Km = Michaelis constant

Given:

- Vmax = 100 µmol/min
- Km = 5 mmol/L
- [S] = 5 mmol/L

Substituting:

v = (100 × 5) / (5 + 5)

v = 500 / 10

**Correct reaction velocity = 50 µmol/min.**

## 7. Corrected Scientific Answer

When the substrate concentration equals the Michaelis constant (Km), the reaction velocity is half of the maximum velocity (Vmax).

Therefore:

v = ½ × 100

**Answer: 50 µmol/min.**

This relationship is a fundamental feature of the Michaelis–Menten model.
