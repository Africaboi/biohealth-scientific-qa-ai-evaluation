
# Carbohydrate Metabolism

## Section Overview

This question set assesses understanding of carbohydrate digestion, absorption, glycolysis, gluconeogenesis, glycogen metabolism, the pentose phosphate pathway, and hormonal regulation of blood glucose.

The questions are designed at intermediate-to-advanced undergraduate and postgraduate levels.

## Question 1: Glycolysis

Which enzyme catalyses the first committed and major regulatory step of glycolysis?

A. Hexokinase

B. Phosphofructokinase-1 (PFK-1)

C. Pyruvate kinase

D. Glucose-6-phosphate dehydrogenase

**Correct Answer: B**

**Explanation:**

Phosphofructokinase-1 catalyses the conversion of fructose-6-phosphate to fructose-1,6-bisphosphate using ATP.

This is the first committed step of glycolysis and a major regulatory point. PFK-1 is activated by AMP and fructose-2,6-bisphosphate and inhibited by ATP and citrate.

**Evaluation of Options:**

- A: Incorrect. Hexokinase catalyses glucose phosphorylation, an early step that is not committed exclusively to glycolysis.
- B: Correct. PFK-1 catalyses the first committed step of glycolysis.
- C: Incorrect. Pyruvate kinase catalyses the final step of glycolysis.
- D: Incorrect. Glucose-6-phosphate dehydrogenase functions in the pentose phosphate pathway.

## Question 2: ATP Yield from Glycolysis

What is the net ATP yield from the conversion of one molecule of glucose to two molecules of pyruvate through glycolysis?

A. 1 ATP

B. 2 ATP

C. 4 ATP

D. 8 ATP

**Correct Answer: B**

**Explanation:**

Glycolysis consumes two ATP molecules during its energy-investment phase and produces four ATP molecules during its energy-payoff phase.

Therefore, the net ATP yield is:

4 ATP produced − 2 ATP consumed = 2 ATP.

Two NADH molecules are also produced per glucose molecule.

**Evaluation of Options:**

- A: Incorrect. The net yield is greater than one ATP.
- B: Correct. Glycolysis produces a net gain of two ATP.
- C: Incorrect. Four ATP molecules are produced gross, not net.
- D: Incorrect. Eight ATP is not the direct net ATP yield of glycolysis.

## Question 3: Gluconeogenesis

Which of the following enzymes catalyses the conversion of oxaloacetate to phosphoenolpyruvate during gluconeogenesis?

A. Pyruvate kinase

B. Phosphoenolpyruvate carboxykinase (PEPCK)

C. Phosphofructokinase-1

D. Lactate dehydrogenase

**Correct Answer: B**

**Explanation:**

Phosphoenolpyruvate carboxykinase catalyses the conversion of oxaloacetate to phosphoenolpyruvate using GTP.

This reaction is one of the bypass steps that allow gluconeogenesis to overcome the irreversible pyruvate kinase reaction of glycolysis.

**Evaluation of Options:**

- A: Incorrect. Pyruvate kinase catalyses phosphoenolpyruvate conversion to pyruvate during glycolysis.
- B: Correct. PEPCK catalyses the conversion of oxaloacetate to phosphoenolpyruvate.
- C: Incorrect. PFK-1 functions in glycolysis.
- D: Incorrect. Lactate dehydrogenase interconverts lactate and pyruvate.

## Question 4: Glycogenesis

Which enzyme is primarily responsible for adding glucose residues to a growing glycogen chain during glycogenesis?

A. Glycogen phosphorylase

B. Glycogen synthase

C. Glucose-6-phosphatase

D. Debranching enzyme

**Correct Answer: B**

**Explanation:**

Glycogen synthase catalyses the formation of α-1,4-glycosidic bonds during glycogen synthesis.

It uses UDP-glucose as the activated glucose donor. The branching enzyme subsequently introduces α-1,6-glycosidic linkages.

**Evaluation of Options:**

- A: Incorrect. Glycogen phosphorylase breaks down glycogen.
- B: Correct. Glycogen synthase elongates glycogen chains.
- C: Incorrect. Glucose-6-phosphatase releases free glucose from glucose-6-phosphate.
- D: Incorrect. The debranching enzyme participates in glycogen breakdown.

## Question 5: Glycogenolysis

Which enzyme catalyses the phosphorolytic cleavage of α-1,4-glycosidic bonds during glycogen breakdown?

A. Glycogen synthase

B. Glycogen phosphorylase

C. Glucokinase

D. Phosphoglucomutase

**Correct Answer: B**

**Explanation:**

Glycogen phosphorylase removes glucose residues from the non-reducing ends of glycogen by breaking α-1,4-glycosidic bonds through phosphorolysis.

The principal product is glucose-1-phosphate, which can subsequently be converted to glucose-6-phosphate by phosphoglucomutase.

**Evaluation of Options:**

- A: Incorrect. Glycogen synthase functions in glycogen synthesis.
- B: Correct. Glycogen phosphorylase catalyses glycogen phosphorolysis.
- C: Incorrect. Glucokinase phosphorylates glucose.
- D: Incorrect. Phosphoglucomutase converts glucose-1-phosphate to glucose-6-phosphate.

## Question 6: Pentose Phosphate Pathway

What is the principal function of the oxidative phase of the pentose phosphate pathway?

A. Production of ATP through substrate-level phosphorylation.

B. Generation of NADPH and ribulose-5-phosphate.

C. Direct conversion of glucose to pyruvate.

D. Synthesis of glycogen.

**Correct Answer: B**

**Explanation:**

The oxidative phase of the pentose phosphate pathway converts glucose-6-phosphate into ribulose-5-phosphate while generating NADPH and releasing carbon dioxide.

NADPH supports reductive biosynthesis and helps maintain reduced glutathione, which is important for cellular antioxidant defence.

**Evaluation of Options:**

- A: Incorrect. ATP production is not the primary purpose of this pathway.
- B: Correct. NADPH and ribulose-5-phosphate are important products.
- C: Incorrect. This describes glycolysis.
- D: Incorrect. Glycogen synthesis occurs through glycogenesis.

## Question 7: Regulation of Blood Glucose

Which hormone primarily promotes glucose uptake and storage following an increase in blood glucose concentration?

A. Glucagon

B. Insulin

C. Cortisol

D. Adrenaline

**Correct Answer: B**

**Explanation:**

Insulin is released by pancreatic beta cells in response to elevated blood glucose.

It promotes glucose uptake in skeletal muscle and adipose tissue through GLUT4 translocation and stimulates glycogen synthesis and other anabolic processes.

**Evaluation of Options:**

- A: Incorrect. Glucagon primarily promotes hepatic glucose production.
- B: Correct. Insulin promotes glucose utilisation and storage.
- C: Incorrect. Cortisol generally supports increased blood glucose availability.
- D: Incorrect. Adrenaline promotes metabolic responses that increase glucose availability.

## Question 8: Cori Cycle

Which statement best describes the Cori cycle?

A. Conversion of fatty acids into ketone bodies in the liver.

B. Transport of lactate from peripheral tissues to the liver for conversion into glucose.

C. Conversion of amino acids into glycogen in skeletal muscle.

D. Conversion of glucose into fatty acids in adipose tissue.

**Correct Answer: B**

**Explanation:**

During anaerobic glycolysis, tissues such as exercising skeletal muscle produce lactate.

Lactate enters the circulation and is transported to the liver, where it can be converted to glucose through gluconeogenesis. The glucose can then return to peripheral tissues.

This metabolic relationship is known as the Cori cycle.

**Evaluation of Options:**

- A: Incorrect. This describes ketogenesis.
- B: Correct. It describes the exchange of lactate and glucose between peripheral tissues and the liver.
- C: Incorrect. This is not the Cori cycle.
- D: Incorrect. This describes aspects of lipogenesis.

## Question 9: Pyruvate Dehydrogenase Complex

What is the principal function of the pyruvate dehydrogenase complex?

A. Conversion of pyruvate to lactate.

B. Conversion of pyruvate to acetyl-CoA with the release of carbon dioxide.

C. Conversion of acetyl-CoA to pyruvate.

D. Conversion of glucose-6-phosphate to fructose-6-phosphate.

**Correct Answer: B**

**Explanation:**

The pyruvate dehydrogenase complex catalyses the oxidative decarboxylation of pyruvate to acetyl-CoA.

This reaction links glycolysis to the citric acid cycle. It also produces NADH and carbon dioxide.

The complex requires several cofactors, including thiamine pyrophosphate, lipoamide, coenzyme A, FAD and NAD+.

**Evaluation of Options:**

- A: Incorrect. Lactate dehydrogenase catalyses pyruvate conversion to lactate.
- B: Correct. This is the principal reaction catalysed by the pyruvate dehydrogenase complex.
- C: Incorrect. The reaction is not reversible through the pyruvate dehydrogenase complex.
- D: Incorrect. Phosphoglucose isomerase catalyses this reaction.

## Question 10: Glucose-6-Phosphatase

In which tissues is glucose-6-phosphatase particularly important for maintaining blood glucose during fasting?

A. Skeletal muscle and adipose tissue.

B. Liver and kidney.

C. Brain and red blood cells.

D. Cardiac muscle and skin.

**Correct Answer: B**

**Explanation:**

Glucose-6-phosphatase catalyses the conversion of glucose-6-phosphate to free glucose and inorganic phosphate.

The enzyme is present in tissues such as the liver and kidney, allowing them to release glucose into the bloodstream during fasting.

Skeletal muscle lacks significant glucose-6-phosphatase activity and therefore cannot directly release free glucose from its glycogen stores into the circulation.

**Evaluation of Options:**

- A: Incorrect. Skeletal muscle and adipose tissue do not perform this function to the same extent as liver and kidney.
- B: Correct. Liver and kidney contribute to maintaining blood glucose through this enzyme.
- C: Incorrect. These tissues are not major sites of glucose release through glucose-6-phosphatase.
- D: Incorrect. These are not the principal tissues involved.
