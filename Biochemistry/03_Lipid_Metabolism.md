
# Lipid Metabolism

## Section Overview

This question set assesses understanding of lipid digestion, absorption, transport, fatty acid oxidation, fatty acid synthesis, cholesterol metabolism, ketone body metabolism, and the biochemical regulation of lipid homeostasis.

The questions are designed at intermediate-to-advanced undergraduate and postgraduate levels.

## Question 1: Beta-Oxidation of Fatty Acids

Where does the beta-oxidation of most long-chain fatty acids primarily occur in eukaryotic cells?

A. Cytosol

B. Mitochondrial matrix

C. Nucleus

D. Golgi apparatus

**Correct Answer: B**

**Explanation:**

Beta-oxidation of most fatty acids occurs in the mitochondrial matrix. During this process, fatty acyl-CoA molecules are progressively shortened by two carbon atoms per cycle, producing acetyl-CoA, NADH and FADH2.

Very-long-chain fatty acids may undergo initial oxidation in peroxisomes before their shortened products are further metabolised.

**Evaluation of Options:**

- A: Incorrect. Cytosolic fatty acid metabolism includes fatty acid synthesis, not the principal site of mitochondrial beta-oxidation.
- B: Correct. The mitochondrial matrix is the principal site of beta-oxidation for most fatty acids.
- C: Incorrect. The nucleus is not the principal site of fatty acid oxidation.
- D: Incorrect. The Golgi apparatus is not the principal site of beta-oxidation.

## Question 2: Carnitine Shuttle

What is the primary function of the carnitine shuttle?

A. Transport of glucose into mitochondria.

B. Transport of long-chain fatty acyl groups across the inner mitochondrial membrane.

C. Transport of amino acids into the nucleus.

D. Synthesis of triglycerides in the cytosol.

**Correct Answer: B**

**Explanation:**

Long-chain fatty acyl-CoA molecules cannot directly cross the inner mitochondrial membrane.

The carnitine shuttle facilitates their entry into the mitochondrial matrix. Carnitine palmitoyltransferase I (CPT-I) transfers the fatty acyl group to carnitine, allowing transport through the carnitine-acylcarnitine translocase. CPT-II subsequently regenerates fatty acyl-CoA in the matrix.

Malonyl-CoA inhibits CPT-I, thereby coordinating fatty acid synthesis with fatty acid oxidation.

**Evaluation of Options:**

- A: Incorrect. The carnitine shuttle transports long-chain fatty acyl groups.
- B: Correct. It enables long-chain fatty acids to enter the mitochondrial matrix for oxidation.
- C: Incorrect. It is not involved in nuclear amino acid transport.
- D: Incorrect. Triglyceride synthesis is a separate metabolic process.

## Question 3: Fatty Acid Synthesis

Which enzyme catalyses the committed and rate-limiting step of fatty acid synthesis?

A. Acetyl-CoA carboxylase.

B. Carnitine palmitoyltransferase I.

C. Hormone-sensitive lipase.

D. Acyl-CoA dehydrogenase.

**Correct Answer: A**

**Explanation:**

Acetyl-CoA carboxylase (ACC) catalyses the ATP-dependent conversion of acetyl-CoA to malonyl-CoA.

This is the committed and major regulatory step of fatty acid synthesis. The enzyme requires biotin as a cofactor.

ACC is activated by citrate and inhibited by long-chain fatty acyl-CoA molecules and phosphorylation mediated by AMP-activated protein kinase.

**Evaluation of Options:**

- A: Correct. ACC produces malonyl-CoA, the key precursor for fatty acid synthesis.
- B: Incorrect. CPT-I regulates mitochondrial fatty acid entry.
- C: Incorrect. Hormone-sensitive lipase participates in stored triacylglycerol mobilisation.
- D: Incorrect. Acyl-CoA dehydrogenase catalyses an early step in beta-oxidation.

## Question 4: Ketogenesis

In which organ does ketone body synthesis primarily occur?

A. Skeletal muscle.

B. Liver.

C. Brain.

D. Adipose tissue.

**Correct Answer: B**

**Explanation:**

Ketogenesis occurs primarily in the mitochondria of liver cells.

During prolonged fasting or carbohydrate restriction, increased fatty acid oxidation generates acetyl-CoA, which can be converted into ketone bodies.

The major ketone bodies are acetoacetate, beta-hydroxybutyrate and acetone.

The liver produces ketone bodies but cannot utilise them efficiently because it lacks the enzyme succinyl-CoA:3-oxoacid CoA transferase (SCOT).

**Evaluation of Options:**

- A: Incorrect. Skeletal muscle can utilise ketone bodies but is not the principal site of their synthesis.
- B: Correct. The liver is the main organ responsible for ketogenesis.
- C: Incorrect. The brain can use ketone bodies during prolonged fasting but does not primarily synthesise them.
- D: Incorrect. Adipose tissue supplies fatty acids for ketogenesis but is not the principal site.

## Question 5: Lipoprotein Function

Which lipoprotein is primarily responsible for transporting endogenous triacylglycerols from the liver to peripheral tissues?

A. Chylomicrons.

B. Very-low-density lipoproteins (VLDL).

C. High-density lipoproteins (HDL).

D. Low-density lipoproteins (LDL).

**Correct Answer: B**

**Explanation:**

VLDL particles are synthesised and secreted by the liver to transport endogenous triacylglycerols to peripheral tissues.

Lipoprotein lipase hydrolyses VLDL triacylglycerols, releasing fatty acids for uptake by tissues. VLDL is progressively converted into intermediate-density lipoprotein and subsequently LDL.

**Evaluation of Options:**

- A: Incorrect. Chylomicrons transport dietary triacylglycerols from the intestine.
- B: Correct. VLDL transports endogenous triacylglycerols from the liver.
- C: Incorrect. HDL is primarily involved in reverse cholesterol transport.
- D: Incorrect. LDL primarily delivers cholesterol to peripheral tissues.

## Question 6: Cholesterol Biosynthesis

Which enzyme catalyses the rate-limiting step in cholesterol biosynthesis?

A. Lipoprotein lipase.

B. HMG-CoA reductase.

C. Hormone-sensitive lipase.

D. Acetyl-CoA carboxylase.

**Correct Answer: B**

**Explanation:**

3-Hydroxy-3-methylglutaryl-CoA reductase (HMG-CoA reductase) catalyses the conversion of HMG-CoA to mevalonate.

This is the rate-limiting step of cholesterol biosynthesis and the principal target of statin drugs.

The enzyme is regulated by feedback mechanisms involving cellular cholesterol levels, phosphorylation and hormonal signalling.

**Evaluation of Options:**

- A: Incorrect. Lipoprotein lipase hydrolyses triacylglycerols in circulating lipoproteins.
- B: Correct. HMG-CoA reductase catalyses the rate-limiting step of cholesterol biosynthesis.
- C: Incorrect. Hormone-sensitive lipase mobilises stored lipids.
- D: Incorrect. Acetyl-CoA carboxylase participates in fatty acid synthesis.

## Question 7: Lipolysis

Which enzyme plays a major role in the hydrolysis of stored triacylglycerols in adipose tissue?

A. Hormone-sensitive lipase.

B. Glycogen synthase.

C. Pyruvate kinase.

D. Glucose-6-phosphate dehydrogenase.

**Correct Answer: A**

**Explanation:**

Hormone-sensitive lipase (HSL) participates in the mobilisation of stored triacylglycerols and diacylglycerols in adipose tissue.

Catecholamine signalling can stimulate lipolysis through cyclic AMP-dependent protein kinase pathways.

However, adipose triglyceride lipase (ATGL) catalyses the initial hydrolysis of triacylglycerols to diacylglycerols and is a major initiator of adipose tissue lipolysis.

**Evaluation of Options:**

- A: Correct. HSL is an important enzyme in stored lipid mobilisation.
- B: Incorrect. Glycogen synthase functions in glycogen synthesis.
- C: Incorrect. Pyruvate kinase participates in glycolysis.
- D: Incorrect. Glucose-6-phosphate dehydrogenase functions in the pentose phosphate pathway.

## Question 8: Essential Fatty Acids

Which pair consists of essential fatty acids in humans?

A. Palmitic acid and stearic acid.

B. Linoleic acid and alpha-linolenic acid.

C. Oleic acid and palmitoleic acid.

D. Arachidic acid and lauric acid.

**Correct Answer: B**

**Explanation:**

Humans cannot synthesise linoleic acid (omega-6) and alpha-linolenic acid (omega-3) because they lack the desaturase enzymes required to introduce double bonds beyond the ninth carbon position from the carboxyl end.

These fatty acids must therefore be obtained from dietary sources.

They also serve as precursors for the synthesis of longer-chain polyunsaturated fatty acids.

**Evaluation of Options:**

- A: Incorrect. Palmitic and stearic acids can be synthesised in the human body.
- B: Correct. Both linoleic acid and alpha-linolenic acid are essential fatty acids.
- C: Incorrect. Oleic and palmitoleic acids are non-essential fatty acids.
- D: Incorrect. These are not the two essential fatty acids.

## Question 9: Lipoprotein Lipase

What is the principal function of lipoprotein lipase?

A. Synthesis of cholesterol in the liver.

B. Hydrolysis of triacylglycerols in circulating chylomicrons and VLDL.

C. Conversion of glucose into glycogen.

D. Synthesis of ketone bodies.

**Correct Answer: B**

**Explanation:**

Lipoprotein lipase (LPL) is located on the luminal surface of capillary endothelial cells, particularly in adipose tissue and skeletal and cardiac muscle.

It hydrolyses triacylglycerols in chylomicrons and VLDL, releasing fatty acids that can be taken up by surrounding tissues.

Apolipoprotein C-II is an important activator of LPL.

**Evaluation of Options:**

- A: Incorrect. HMG-CoA reductase is the rate-limiting enzyme in cholesterol biosynthesis.
- B: Correct. LPL hydrolyses triacylglycerols in circulating lipoproteins.
- C: Incorrect. Glycogen synthase catalyses glycogen synthesis.
- D: Incorrect. Ketone body synthesis occurs mainly in liver mitochondria.

## Question 10: Fatty Acid Oxidation and Energy Production

Why does the complete oxidation of palmitate generate substantially more ATP than the oxidation of one molecule of glucose?

A. Palmitate contains fewer carbon atoms than glucose.

B. Palmitate is more highly reduced and produces more reducing equivalents during oxidation.

C. Palmitate does not require metabolic enzymes.

D. Palmitate produces ATP directly through glycolysis.

**Correct Answer: B**

**Explanation:**

Palmitate is a 16-carbon saturated fatty acid with a high proportion of reduced carbon atoms.

Its complete oxidation produces acetyl-CoA, NADH and FADH2. These products support ATP generation through the citric acid cycle and oxidative phosphorylation.

Using the conventional modern P/O ratios of approximately 2.5 ATP per NADH and 1.5 ATP per FADH2, complete oxidation of palmitate yields approximately 106 ATP net.

By comparison, complete aerobic oxidation of one glucose molecule commonly yields approximately 30–32 ATP, depending on the shuttle system used to transfer cytosolic reducing equivalents into mitochondria.

**Evaluation of Options:**

- A: Incorrect. Palmitate contains 16 carbon atoms, whereas glucose contains six.
- B: Correct. The greater degree of reduction of fatty acids explains their high energy yield.
- C: Incorrect. Fatty acid oxidation requires several enzymes.
- D: Incorrect. Fatty acids are not oxidised through glycolysis.
