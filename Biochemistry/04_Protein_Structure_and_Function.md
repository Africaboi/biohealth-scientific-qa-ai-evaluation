
# Protein Structure and Function

## Overview

This section contains intermediate-to-advanced questions on protein structure, folding, stability and biological function. The questions assess understanding of the relationship between protein structure and function, as well as the biochemical principles governing protein interactions.

## Question 1: Levels of Protein Structure

Which level of protein structure is primarily determined by the amino acid sequence?

A. Primary structure  
B. Secondary structure  
C. Tertiary structure  
D. Quaternary structure  

**Correct Answer: A. Primary structure**

**Explanation:** Primary structure refers to the linear sequence of amino acids joined by peptide bonds. This sequence provides the fundamental information that influences the protein's higher-order folding and structure.

**Distractor Evaluation:**
- B: Secondary structure involves local arrangements such as alpha-helices and beta-sheets.
- C: Tertiary structure describes the overall three-dimensional folding of a polypeptide chain.
- D: Quaternary structure describes the arrangement of multiple polypeptide subunits.

## Question 2: Peptide Bond Formation

Which functional groups participate directly in peptide bond formation?

A. Two amino groups  
B. An amino group and a carboxyl group  
C. Two carboxyl groups  
D. A hydroxyl group and a phosphate group  

**Correct Answer: B. An amino group and a carboxyl group**

**Explanation:** A peptide bond forms between the carboxyl group of one amino acid and the amino group of another. The resulting linkage is an amide bond.

**Distractor Evaluation:**
- A: Two amino groups do not form the peptide linkage.
- C: Two carboxyl groups do not form a peptide bond.
- D: These groups are not the functional groups directly responsible for peptide bond formation.

## Question 3: Alpha-Helix Stabilisation

What interaction primarily stabilises an alpha-helix in a protein?

A. Hydrogen bonds between peptide backbone groups  
B. Disulfide bonds between adjacent amino acids  
C. Ionic bonds between all neighbouring side chains  
D. Covalent bonds between separate polypeptide chains  

**Correct Answer: A. Hydrogen bonds between peptide backbone groups**

**Explanation:** Alpha-helices are stabilised mainly by hydrogen bonds between the carbonyl oxygen of one peptide bond and the amide hydrogen of a peptide bond approximately four residues ahead in the sequence.

**Distractor Evaluation:**
- B: Disulfide bonds can stabilise tertiary structure but are not the primary stabilising interaction in an alpha-helix.
- C: Ionic interactions may contribute to protein stability but do not primarily stabilise the alpha-helix.
- D: Covalent bonds between separate chains are not responsible for alpha-helical stabilisation.

## Question 4: Protein Denaturation

Which statement best describes protein denaturation?

A. Complete breakdown of all peptide bonds  
B. Disruption of higher-order protein structure without necessarily breaking peptide bonds  
C. Conversion of amino acids into glucose  
D. Formation of additional peptide bonds  

**Correct Answer: B. Disruption of higher-order protein structure without necessarily breaking peptide bonds**

**Explanation:** Denaturation involves disruption of a protein's secondary, tertiary or quaternary structure. It commonly results from heat, extreme pH or chemical agents. The primary amino acid sequence generally remains intact.

**Distractor Evaluation:**
- A: Breaking peptide bonds is protein hydrolysis rather than ordinary denaturation.
- C: Amino acid conversion into glucose involves metabolic pathways.
- D: Denaturation does not involve the formation of additional peptide bonds.

## Question 5: Disulfide Bond Formation

Which amino acid can form disulfide bonds within or between polypeptide chains?

A. Methionine  
B. Cysteine  
C. Serine  
D. Lysine  

**Correct Answer: B. Cysteine**

**Explanation:** Two cysteine residues can undergo oxidation to form a covalent disulfide bond between their sulphur-containing side chains. These bonds can contribute to protein stability.

**Distractor Evaluation:**
- A: Methionine contains sulphur but does not normally form disulfide bonds.
- C: Serine contains a hydroxyl group rather than a thiol group.
- D: Lysine has a positively charged amino-containing side chain.

## Question 6: Protein Folding

Which cellular protein complex assists the correct folding of newly synthesised or stress-denatured proteins?

A. Chaperones  
B. Ribosomes alone  
C. DNA polymerases  
D. Restriction endonucleases  

**Correct Answer: A. Chaperones**

**Explanation:** Molecular chaperones assist protein folding, prevent inappropriate aggregation and help maintain protein quality. They do not generally determine the final amino acid sequence.

**Distractor Evaluation:**
- B: Ribosomes synthesise polypeptides but are not the general cellular protein-folding machinery.
- C: DNA polymerases catalyse DNA synthesis.
- D: Restriction endonucleases recognise and cleave specific DNA sequences.

## Question 7: Quaternary Protein Structure

Which of the following proteins is a classic example of a protein with quaternary structure?

A. Myoglobin  
B. Haemoglobin  
C. A single polypeptide insulin chain  
D. Ribonuclease A  

**Correct Answer: B. Haemoglobin**

**Explanation:** Adult haemoglobin A is composed of four polypeptide subunits: two alpha and two beta chains. Their association produces quaternary structure.

**Distractor Evaluation:**
- A: Myoglobin is a single polypeptide chain and has tertiary structure.
- C: A single polypeptide chain does not possess quaternary structure.
- D: Ribonuclease A is a single-chain protein.

## Question 8: Isoelectric Point

What is the isoelectric point (pI) of a protein?

A. The pH at which the protein has a net electrical charge of zero  
B. The pH at which all peptide bonds are broken  
C. The temperature at which the protein denatures  
D. The pH at which the protein reaches maximum solubility in every solution  

**Correct Answer: A. The pH at which the protein has a net electrical charge of zero**

**Explanation:** The isoelectric point is the pH at which the net electrical charge of a protein is zero. At this pH, protein solubility may be reduced, although this depends on the protein and solution conditions.

**Distractor Evaluation:**
- B: Peptide bond hydrolysis is not related to the definition of pI.
- C: Denaturation temperature is a different physicochemical property.
- D: Protein solubility is influenced by several factors and is not universally maximal at the pI.

## Question 9: Protein Structure and Function

Why can a change in a single amino acid sometimes significantly affect protein function?

A. It always destroys every peptide bond in the protein  
B. It can alter local interactions, folding, stability or an important functional site  
C. It necessarily increases the protein's molecular mass by half  
D. It always converts the protein into a carbohydrate  

**Correct Answer: B. It can alter local interactions, folding, stability or an important functional site**

**Explanation:** A single amino acid substitution can affect the chemical interactions that maintain protein structure or directly influence a binding or catalytic site. The magnitude of the effect depends on the location and properties of the substituted residue.

**Distractor Evaluation:**
- A: Amino acid substitution does not automatically destroy peptide bonds.
- C: A single substitution does not necessarily cause a large change in molecular mass.
- D: Proteins are not converted into carbohydrates by amino acid substitution.

## Question 10: Protein Structure Determination

Which technique is widely used to determine the three-dimensional structures of purified proteins at high resolution?

A. X-ray crystallography  
B. Gram staining  
C. Agarose gel electrophoresis alone  
D. Simple light microscopy  

**Correct Answer: A. X-ray crystallography**

**Explanation:** X-ray crystallography uses diffraction patterns generated by X-rays passing through a protein crystal to determine the protein's atomic structure. Other important structural methods include nuclear magnetic resonance spectroscopy and cryo-electron microscopy.

**Distractor Evaluation:**
- B: Gram staining differentiates bacterial cell-wall characteristics.
- C: Agarose gel electrophoresis separates nucleic acids and is not a method for determining protein atomic structures.
- D: Conventional light microscopy does not provide the resolution needed to determine atomic protein structures.
