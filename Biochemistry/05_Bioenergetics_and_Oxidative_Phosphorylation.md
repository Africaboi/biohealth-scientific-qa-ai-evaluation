
# Bioenergetics and Oxidative Phosphorylation

## Overview

This section contains intermediate-to-advanced questions on cellular energy transformation, mitochondrial function, the electron transport chain and oxidative phosphorylation. The questions assess understanding of ATP production, redox reactions, proton gradients and the regulation of energy metabolism.

## Question 1: ATP as an Energy Currency

Which feature makes ATP suitable as an immediate energy currency in cells?

A. Its hydrolysis can be coupled to energy-requiring cellular processes  
B. It stores all cellular energy permanently  
C. It cannot participate in enzymatic reactions  
D. It is used exclusively in mitochondria  

**Correct Answer: A. Its hydrolysis can be coupled to energy-requiring cellular processes**

**Explanation:** ATP hydrolysis is thermodynamically favourable under standard biochemical conditions and can be coupled to processes such as biosynthesis, active transport and muscle contraction. ATP is continually regenerated rather than serving as a permanent energy store.

**Distractor Evaluation:**
- B: Long-term energy storage is mainly associated with molecules such as glycogen and triacylglycerols.
- C: ATP is a substrate for numerous enzymes and biochemical reactions.
- D: ATP is used throughout the cell, including in the cytosol and nucleus.

## Question 2: Location of the Electron Transport Chain

In eukaryotic cells, where is the mitochondrial electron transport chain located?

A. Outer mitochondrial membrane  
B. Inner mitochondrial membrane  
C. Mitochondrial matrix  
D. Cytosol  

**Correct Answer: B. Inner mitochondrial membrane**

**Explanation:** The electron transport chain consists of protein complexes and associated electron carriers embedded in the inner mitochondrial membrane. This membrane also supports the proton gradient required for ATP synthesis.

**Distractor Evaluation:**
- A: The outer membrane surrounds the mitochondrion but does not house the respiratory chain complexes.
- C: The matrix contains enzymes involved in processes such as the citric acid cycle.
- D: The cytosol contains glycolytic enzymes but not the mitochondrial electron transport chain.

## Question 3: Function of NADH

What is the primary role of NADH in oxidative phosphorylation?

A. It donates electrons to Complex I of the electron transport chain  
B. It directly hydrolyses ATP  
C. It transports oxygen into the mitochondrion  
D. It converts carbon dioxide into glucose  

**Correct Answer: A. It donates electrons to Complex I of the electron transport chain**

**Explanation:** NADH transfers high-energy electrons to Complex I. Electron transfer through the respiratory chain supports proton translocation across the inner mitochondrial membrane.

**Distractor Evaluation:**
- B: ATP hydrolysis is catalysed by ATPases and other enzymes.
- C: Oxygen diffuses across biological membranes and is not transported by NADH.
- D: Carbon dioxide fixation into carbohydrates is associated with pathways such as the Calvin cycle in photosynthetic organisms.

## Question 4: Role of Oxygen

What is the function of oxygen in the mitochondrial electron transport chain?

A. It acts as the final electron acceptor  
B. It donates electrons to Complex I  
C. It directly phosphorylates ADP  
D. It transports protons into the mitochondrial matrix  

**Correct Answer: A. It acts as the final electron acceptor**

**Explanation:** Oxygen accepts electrons at Complex IV and combines with protons to form water. This allows electron transport to continue under aerobic conditions.

**Distractor Evaluation:**
- B: NADH is the electron donor to Complex I.
- C: ATP synthase catalyses ATP formation using the proton-motive force.
- D: Oxygen does not function as a proton transporter.

## Question 5: Proton-Motive Force

What generates the proton-motive force across the inner mitochondrial membrane?

A. Accumulation of protons in the intermembrane space and an electrical potential across the membrane  
B. Equal proton concentrations on both sides of the membrane  
C. Breakdown of mitochondrial DNA  
D. Direct hydrolysis of glucose by ATP synthase  

**Correct Answer: A. Accumulation of protons in the intermembrane space and an electrical potential across the membrane**

**Explanation:** Electron transport drives proton pumping from the matrix into the intermembrane space. This produces both a chemical gradient and an electrical potential, together known as the proton-motive force.

**Distractor Evaluation:**
- B: Equal proton concentrations would eliminate the chemical component of the gradient.
- C: Mitochondrial DNA breakdown is not responsible for generating the proton-motive force.
- D: ATP synthase uses the proton-motive force; it does not hydrolyse glucose.

## Question 6: Function of ATP Synthase

Which statement best describes the function of mitochondrial ATP synthase?

A. It uses the energy of proton movement to drive ATP synthesis  
B. It transfers electrons directly from NADH to oxygen  
C. It breaks down fatty acids into acetyl-CoA  
D. It synthesises mitochondrial DNA  

**Correct Answer: A. It uses the energy of proton movement to drive ATP synthesis**

**Explanation:** ATP synthase allows protons to move down their electrochemical gradient through its membrane-associated component. The resulting conformational and rotational changes drive ATP formation from ADP and inorganic phosphate.

**Distractor Evaluation:**
- B: Electron transfer to oxygen occurs through the electron transport chain, particularly Complex IV.
- C: Fatty acid beta-oxidation involves enzymes in the mitochondrial matrix and other cellular compartments.
- D: Mitochondrial DNA replication involves specialised DNA replication proteins.

## Question 7: Uncoupling of Oxidative Phosphorylation

What is the principal effect of an uncoupling agent on mitochondrial oxidative phosphorylation?

A. It dissipates the proton gradient, reducing ATP synthesis efficiency  
B. It permanently stops glycolysis  
C. It increases ATP production without affecting oxygen consumption  
D. It prevents all electron transfer reactions in the respiratory chain  

**Correct Answer: A. It dissipates the proton gradient, reducing ATP synthesis efficiency**

**Explanation:** Uncoupling agents allow protons to return across the inner mitochondrial membrane without passing through ATP synthase. Electron transport and oxygen consumption may continue, but less ATP is generated. Some of the energy is released as heat.

**Distractor Evaluation:**
- B: Uncoupling does not necessarily stop glycolysis.
- C: Uncoupling generally decreases ATP produced per unit of oxygen consumed.
- D: Electron transport can continue despite dissipation of the proton gradient.

## Question 8: P/O Ratio

What does the P/O ratio describe in mitochondrial bioenergetics?

A. The number of ATP molecules synthesised relative to oxygen reduced  
B. The amount of glucose consumed during glycolysis  
C. The ratio of proteins to lipids in a membrane  
D. The concentration of oxygen in the cytosol  

**Correct Answer: A. The number of ATP molecules synthesised relative to oxygen reduced**

**Explanation:** The P/O ratio describes the relationship between ATP synthesis and oxygen reduction during oxidative phosphorylation. Modern approximate estimates are about 2.5 ATP per NADH and 1.5 ATP per FADH₂.

**Distractor Evaluation:**
- B: Glucose consumption is not the definition of the P/O ratio.
- C: Membrane composition is unrelated to this bioenergetic measurement.
- D: Oxygen concentration in the cytosol does not define the P/O ratio.

## Question 9: Inhibitors of the Electron Transport Chain

Which mitochondrial respiratory complex is inhibited by cyanide?

A. Complex I  
B. Complex II  
C. Complex III  
D. Complex IV  

**Correct Answer: D. Complex IV**

**Explanation:** Cyanide inhibits cytochrome c oxidase (Complex IV), preventing the transfer of electrons to oxygen. This disrupts mitochondrial respiration and cellular aerobic ATP production.

**Distractor Evaluation:**
- A: Complex I transfers electrons from NADH to ubiquinone.
- B: Complex II participates in electron transfer from succinate to ubiquinone.
- C: Complex III transfers electrons from ubiquinol to cytochrome c.

## Question 10: Reactive Oxygen Species

Which statement best describes the relationship between mitochondrial electron transport and reactive oxygen species (ROS)?

A. Electron leakage can lead to partial reduction of oxygen and ROS formation  
B. ROS are produced only during DNA replication  
C. Mitochondria cannot generate ROS  
D. All ROS are harmless metabolic products  

**Correct Answer: A. Electron leakage can lead to partial reduction of oxygen and ROS formation**

**Explanation:** Some electrons can escape from components of the electron transport chain and react with oxygen, generating superoxide. Mitochondrial antioxidant systems help regulate ROS levels and protect cellular components from oxidative damage.

**Distractor Evaluation:**
- B: ROS formation is not restricted to DNA replication.
- C: Mitochondria are an important cellular source of ROS.
- D: Excessive ROS can damage proteins, lipids and nucleic acids.
