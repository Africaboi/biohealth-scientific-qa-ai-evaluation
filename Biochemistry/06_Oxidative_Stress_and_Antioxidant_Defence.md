
# Oxidative Stress and Antioxidant Defence

## Overview

This section contains intermediate-to-advanced questions on reactive oxygen species (ROS), reactive nitrogen species (RNS), oxidative damage and cellular antioxidant defence mechanisms. The questions assess understanding of redox biology, enzymatic and non-enzymatic antioxidants, and the relationship between oxidative stress and disease processes.

## Question 1: Definition of Oxidative Stress

Which statement best defines oxidative stress?

A. A condition in which antioxidant activity is always higher than ROS production  
B. An imbalance in redox processes that results in disruption of redox signalling and/or molecular damage  
C. Complete absence of oxygen in the body  
D. A condition caused exclusively by excessive dietary fat consumption  

**Correct Answer: B. An imbalance in redox processes that results in disruption of redox signalling and/or molecular damage**

**Explanation:** Oxidative stress occurs when oxidant production and antioxidant defence become imbalanced in a way that disrupts normal redox regulation or causes oxidative damage to cellular components.

**Distractor Evaluation:**
- A: This describes a situation favouring antioxidant protection rather than oxidative stress.
- C: Absence of oxygen is called anoxia, not oxidative stress.
- D: Dietary fat consumption alone does not define oxidative stress.

## Question 2: Reactive Oxygen Species

Which of the following is classified as a reactive oxygen species?

A. Superoxide anion  
B. Sodium chloride  
C. Glucose  
D. Urea  

**Correct Answer: A. Superoxide anion**

**Explanation:** Superoxide anion is generated through the one-electron reduction of molecular oxygen. It is an important ROS involved in cellular redox reactions and oxidative stress.

**Distractor Evaluation:**
- B: Sodium chloride is an ionic compound and not an ROS.
- C: Glucose is a metabolic substrate, not an ROS.
- D: Urea is a nitrogen-containing metabolic waste product.

## Question 3: Superoxide Dismutase

What is the primary function of superoxide dismutase (SOD)?

A. Conversion of superoxide anion into hydrogen peroxide and oxygen  
B. Conversion of hydrogen peroxide into water and oxygen  
C. Direct synthesis of glutathione  
D. Breakdown of lipid membranes  

**Correct Answer: A. Conversion of superoxide anion into hydrogen peroxide and oxygen**

**Explanation:** SOD catalyses the dismutation of superoxide radicals into hydrogen peroxide and molecular oxygen. Hydrogen peroxide can subsequently be removed by catalase, glutathione peroxidases and other antioxidant systems.

**Distractor Evaluation:**
- B: Catalase catalyses the conversion of hydrogen peroxide into water and oxygen.
- C: Glutathione synthesis involves enzymes responsible for glutathione production.
- D: SOD does not break down lipid membranes.

## Question 4: Catalase Activity

Which reaction is catalysed by catalase?

A. Hydrogen peroxide conversion into water and oxygen  
B. Superoxide conversion into glucose  
C. Lipid oxidation into cholesterol  
D. Glutathione synthesis from amino acids  

**Correct Answer: A. Hydrogen peroxide conversion into water and oxygen**

**Explanation:** Catalase is an antioxidant enzyme that decomposes hydrogen peroxide into water and oxygen, thereby limiting peroxide accumulation in cells.

**Distractor Evaluation:**
- B: Superoxide is converted into hydrogen peroxide and oxygen by SOD.
- C: Catalase does not catalyse cholesterol synthesis or lipid oxidation.
- D: Glutathione synthesis is catalysed by glutamate-cysteine ligase and glutathione synthetase.

## Question 5: Glutathione Peroxidase

Which molecule is commonly used as a reducing substrate by glutathione peroxidase during peroxide detoxification?

A. Reduced glutathione (GSH)  
B. NADP⁺  
C. Acetyl-CoA  
D. Pyruvate  

**Correct Answer: A. Reduced glutathione (GSH)**

**Explanation:** Glutathione peroxidase uses reduced glutathione to reduce hydrogen peroxide and certain organic hydroperoxides. During this reaction, GSH is oxidised to glutathione disulphide (GSSG).

**Distractor Evaluation:**
- B: NADP⁺ is not the direct reducing substrate used by glutathione peroxidase.
- C: Acetyl-CoA participates in several metabolic pathways but is not the substrate for this antioxidant reaction.
- D: Pyruvate is an important metabolic intermediate but does not directly serve this function.

## Question 6: Glutathione Reductase

What is the primary function of glutathione reductase?

A. Regeneration of reduced glutathione from oxidised glutathione  
B. Conversion of glucose into glycogen  
C. Breakdown of superoxide into nitrogen  
D. Synthesis of hydrogen peroxide  

**Correct Answer: A. Regeneration of reduced glutathione from oxidised glutathione**

**Explanation:** Glutathione reductase uses NADPH to reduce oxidised glutathione (GSSG) back to reduced glutathione (GSH). This helps maintain the cellular glutathione redox system.

**Distractor Evaluation:**
- B: Glycogen synthesis is catalysed by glycogen synthase and associated enzymes.
- C: Superoxide dismutase converts superoxide into hydrogen peroxide and oxygen.
- D: Glutathione reductase does not synthesise hydrogen peroxide.

## Question 7: Lipid Peroxidation

Which cellular component is particularly susceptible to lipid peroxidation?

A. Polyunsaturated fatty acids in biological membranes  
B. Saturated hydrocarbons in inert storage containers  
C. Inorganic phosphate ions  
D. Free amino acids in solution only  

**Correct Answer: A. Polyunsaturated fatty acids in biological membranes**

**Explanation:** Polyunsaturated fatty acids contain multiple double bonds and are susceptible to free-radical-mediated oxidation. Lipid peroxidation can alter membrane integrity and generate reactive breakdown products.

**Distractor Evaluation:**
- B: Saturated hydrocarbons lack the multiple double bonds that make polyunsaturated fatty acids particularly susceptible.
- C: Inorganic phosphate ions are not typical substrates for lipid peroxidation.
- D: Free amino acids are not the principal targets in membrane lipid peroxidation.

## Question 8: Malondialdehyde

Why is malondialdehyde (MDA) commonly measured in oxidative stress research?

A. It is a product associated with lipid peroxidation  
B. It is the primary antioxidant enzyme in mitochondria  
C. It directly measures DNA replication  
D. It is the main carrier of oxygen in blood  

**Correct Answer: A. It is a product associated with lipid peroxidation**

**Explanation:** MDA is generated during the breakdown of certain oxidised polyunsaturated fatty acids. It is commonly used as an indicator of lipid peroxidation, although its measurement is not entirely specific to oxidative damage.

**Distractor Evaluation:**
- B: MDA is not an antioxidant enzyme.
- C: MDA measurement does not directly assess DNA replication.
- D: Haemoglobin is the principal oxygen-carrying protein in blood.

## Question 9: Antioxidant Vitamins

Which vitamin is a major lipid-soluble antioxidant that helps protect biological membranes from lipid peroxidation?

A. Vitamin A  
B. Vitamin C  
C. Vitamin E  
D. Vitamin B12  

**Correct Answer: C. Vitamin E**

**Explanation:** Vitamin E, particularly alpha-tocopherol, acts as a lipid-soluble chain-breaking antioxidant. It helps interrupt lipid peroxidation reactions in biological membranes.

**Distractor Evaluation:**
- A: Vitamin A has important biological functions but is not the principal membrane chain-breaking antioxidant.
- B: Vitamin C is water-soluble and contributes to antioxidant defence in aqueous environments.
- D: Vitamin B12 is important in one-carbon metabolism and neurological function, not primarily as a membrane antioxidant.

## Question 10: Oxidative Stress and Disease

Which statement best describes the relationship between oxidative stress and disease?

A. Oxidative stress is associated with several disease processes but is not necessarily the sole cause of disease  
B. Oxidative stress causes every human disease  
C. Oxidative stress occurs only in infectious diseases  
D. Oxidative stress has no relationship with cellular signalling  

**Correct Answer: A. Oxidative stress is associated with several disease processes but is not necessarily the sole cause of disease**

**Explanation:** Oxidative stress has been implicated in the development or progression of several conditions, including cardiovascular, metabolic and neurodegenerative diseases. However, the relationship is complex, and oxidative stress may be a contributing factor, consequence or both.

**Distractor Evaluation:**
- B: Disease development is usually multifactorial, and oxidative stress is not the sole cause of every disease.
- C: Oxidative stress can occur in both infectious and non-infectious conditions.
- D: ROS also participate in normal cellular signalling at controlled concentrations.
