
# Clinical Biochemistry

## Overview

This section contains intermediate-to-advanced questions on the biochemical basis of clinical laboratory investigations, interpretation of biochemical markers, organ function assessment and metabolic disorders. The questions assess the ability to apply biochemical principles to clinical laboratory findings.

## Question 1: Liver Function Assessment

Which enzyme is generally considered more specific for hepatocellular injury than alkaline phosphatase?

A. Alanine aminotransferase (ALT)  
B. Creatine kinase (CK)  
C. Amylase  
D. Lactate dehydrogenase (LDH)  

**Correct Answer: A. Alanine aminotransferase (ALT)**

**Explanation:** ALT is predominantly present in the liver and is commonly measured as a marker of hepatocellular injury. Alkaline phosphatase is particularly useful in evaluating cholestatic patterns, although it can also originate from bone and other tissues.

**Distractor Evaluation:**
- B: CK is mainly associated with muscle injury.
- C: Amylase is commonly assessed in pancreatic and salivary gland disorders.
- D: LDH is widely distributed in tissues and is less specific for liver injury.

## Question 2: Renal Function Assessment

Which biochemical parameter is commonly used to estimate glomerular filtration rate (GFR)?

A. Serum creatinine  
B. Serum albumin  
C. Total bilirubin  
D. Serum amylase  

**Correct Answer: A. Serum creatinine**

**Explanation:** Serum creatinine is commonly used in estimating GFR through validated equations that account for relevant patient characteristics. However, creatinine-based estimates may be influenced by muscle mass, diet and other factors.

**Distractor Evaluation:**
- B: Albumin is useful in assessing nutritional status and protein loss but is not the standard marker used in routine creatinine-based GFR estimation.
- C: Bilirubin is primarily used in assessing bilirubin metabolism and hepatobiliary function.
- D: Amylase is associated with pancreatic and salivary gland function.

## Question 3: Bilirubin Metabolism

Which organ is primarily responsible for conjugating bilirubin?

A. Kidney  
B. Liver  
C. Pancreas  
D. Spleen  

**Correct Answer: B. Liver**

**Explanation:** Hepatocytes take up unconjugated bilirubin and conjugate it with glucuronic acid through the enzyme UDP-glucuronosyltransferase 1A1 (UGT1A1). Conjugated bilirubin is then secreted into bile.

**Distractor Evaluation:**
- A: The kidneys may excrete conjugated bilirubin in urine but do not perform its primary conjugation.
- C: The pancreas does not carry out bilirubin conjugation.
- D: The spleen contributes to the breakdown of aged red blood cells but does not conjugate bilirubin.

## Question 4: Glycated Haemoglobin

What does HbA1c primarily reflect?

A. Average blood glucose exposure over approximately the preceding 2–3 months  
B. Blood glucose concentration at the exact time of sample collection  
C. Insulin concentration during the previous 24 hours  
D. Total cholesterol concentration over one year  

**Correct Answer: A. Average blood glucose exposure over approximately the preceding 2–3 months**

**Explanation:** HbA1c forms through non-enzymatic glycation of haemoglobin. Because red blood cells circulate for approximately 120 days, HbA1c provides an estimate of average glycaemic exposure over the preceding two to three months, with greater influence from more recent weeks.

**Distractor Evaluation:**
- B: A single blood glucose measurement reflects glucose concentration at a particular time.
- C: HbA1c does not measure insulin concentration.
- D: HbA1c is unrelated to long-term cholesterol measurement.

## Question 5: Lipid Profile Interpretation

Which lipoprotein is commonly described as the major carrier of cholesterol from peripheral tissues towards the liver?

A. HDL  
B. LDL  
C. Chylomicrons  
D. VLDL  

**Correct Answer: A. HDL**

**Explanation:** High-density lipoprotein participates in reverse cholesterol transport, facilitating the movement of cholesterol from peripheral tissues towards the liver through several pathways. HDL also has other functions in lipid metabolism.

**Distractor Evaluation:**
- B: LDL primarily delivers cholesterol to peripheral tissues through LDL receptor-mediated uptake.
- C: Chylomicrons transport dietary triglycerides and cholesterol from the intestine.
- D: VLDL primarily transports triglycerides produced by the liver to peripheral tissues.

## Question 6: Cardiac Biomarkers

Which biomarker is widely used in the diagnosis and risk assessment of myocardial injury?

A. Cardiac troponin  
B. Serum urea  
C. Total bilirubin  
D. Serum albumin  

**Correct Answer: A. Cardiac troponin**

**Explanation:** Cardiac troponins I and T are highly sensitive and specific biomarkers of myocardial injury. Their interpretation requires consideration of clinical findings, changes over time and relevant diagnostic criteria.

**Distractor Evaluation:**
- B: Urea is primarily used in evaluating renal function and nitrogen metabolism.
- C: Bilirubin is associated with haemoglobin breakdown and hepatobiliary function.
- D: Albumin is a major plasma protein and is not a specific marker of myocardial injury.

## Question 7: Acid-Base Balance

Which biochemical parameter is most directly used to assess the metabolic component of acid-base balance?

A. Plasma bicarbonate concentration  
B. Serum sodium concentration alone  
C. Total bilirubin concentration  
D. Serum cholesterol concentration  

**Correct Answer: A. Plasma bicarbonate concentration**

**Explanation:** Bicarbonate is the major extracellular buffer and is an important indicator of the metabolic component of acid-base balance. It is interpreted alongside blood pH and carbon dioxide measurements.

**Distractor Evaluation:**
- B: Sodium is an important electrolyte but does not independently indicate the metabolic component of acid-base balance.
- C: Bilirubin is unrelated to the direct assessment of acid-base balance.
- D: Cholesterol concentration is not used to assess acid-base status.

## Question 8: Pancreatic Enzymes

Which enzyme is commonly measured in the assessment of acute pancreatitis?

A. Lipase  
B. Alkaline phosphatase  
C. Creatine kinase  
D. Alanine aminotransferase  

**Correct Answer: A. Lipase**

**Explanation:** Serum lipase is commonly used in the evaluation of acute pancreatitis because it is relatively specific for pancreatic injury and may remain elevated longer than amylase. Results must be interpreted in the clinical context.

**Distractor Evaluation:**
- B: Alkaline phosphatase is commonly associated with cholestasis and bone metabolism.
- C: CK is associated mainly with muscle injury.
- D: ALT is used primarily in assessing hepatocellular injury.

## Question 9: Serum Albumin

Which of the following is an important physiological function of serum albumin?

A. Maintenance of plasma oncotic pressure  
B. Direct transport of oxygen to tissues  
C. Synthesis of red blood cells  
D. Production of digestive enzymes in the pancreas  

**Correct Answer: A. Maintenance of plasma oncotic pressure**

**Explanation:** Albumin is the most abundant plasma protein and contributes substantially to plasma colloid osmotic pressure. It also transports various substances, including fatty acids, hormones, bilirubin and certain drugs.

**Distractor Evaluation:**
- B: Haemoglobin is responsible for most oxygen transport in blood.
- C: Red blood cell production occurs through erythropoiesis in the bone marrow.
- D: Pancreatic acinar cells produce digestive enzymes.

## Question 10: Quality Control in Clinical Biochemistry

What is the primary purpose of internal quality control in a clinical biochemistry laboratory?

A. To monitor analytical performance and detect errors during routine testing  
B. To replace all equipment maintenance procedures  
C. To eliminate the need for external quality assessment  
D. To increase the number of samples processed regardless of accuracy  

**Correct Answer: A. To monitor analytical performance and detect errors during routine testing**

**Explanation:** Internal quality control involves analysing control materials alongside patient samples to monitor the reliability and consistency of analytical methods. It helps identify analytical errors before patient results are released.

**Distractor Evaluation:**
- B: Equipment maintenance remains necessary even when internal quality control is performed.
- C: External quality assessment provides an independent comparison and complements internal quality control.
- D: Laboratory accuracy and reliability are more important than sample throughput alone.
