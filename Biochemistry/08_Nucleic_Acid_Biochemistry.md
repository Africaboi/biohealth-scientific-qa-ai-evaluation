
# Nucleic Acid Biochemistry

## Overview

This section contains intermediate-to-advanced questions on the chemical structure, properties and biological functions of nucleic acids. The questions assess understanding of DNA and RNA structure, nucleotide chemistry, nucleic acid stability, and the biochemical processes involving nucleic acids.

## Question 1: Nucleotide Structure

Which components are present in a nucleotide?

A. Nitrogenous base, pentose sugar and phosphate group  
B. Amino acid, fatty acid and glycerol  
C. Nitrogenous base and amino acid only  
D. Glucose, phosphate and fatty acid  

**Correct Answer: A. Nitrogenous base, pentose sugar and phosphate group**

**Explanation:** A nucleotide consists of a nitrogenous base attached to a pentose sugar and one or more phosphate groups. A nucleoside contains only the nitrogenous base and pentose sugar.

**Distractor Evaluation:**
- B: These components are associated with proteins and lipids rather than nucleotides.
- C: Amino acids are the building blocks of proteins.
- D: Glucose and fatty acids are not the defining components of nucleotides.

## Question 2: DNA Sugar Composition

Which pentose sugar is present in DNA?

A. Ribose  
B. Deoxyribose  
C. Glucose  
D. Fructose  

**Correct Answer: B. Deoxyribose**

**Explanation:** DNA contains 2-deoxyribose, which lacks the hydroxyl group at the 2′ carbon present in ribose. This structural difference contributes to the greater chemical stability of DNA relative to RNA under many conditions.

**Distractor Evaluation:**
- A: Ribose is the pentose sugar found in RNA.
- C: Glucose is a hexose sugar used in energy metabolism.
- D: Fructose is also a hexose sugar and is not a component of DNA.

## Question 3: Purine Bases

Which pair consists exclusively of purine nitrogenous bases?

A. Adenine and guanine  
B. Cytosine and thymine  
C. Adenine and cytosine  
D. Guanine and uracil  

**Correct Answer: A. Adenine and guanine**

**Explanation:** Purines have a double-ring structure. Adenine and guanine are the two purine bases found in both DNA and RNA.

**Distractor Evaluation:**
- B: Cytosine and thymine are pyrimidines.
- C: Cytosine is a pyrimidine.
- D: Uracil is a pyrimidine.

## Question 4: Complementary Base Pairing

Which base pairs are normally found in double-stranded DNA?

A. Adenine–uracil and guanine–cytosine  
B. Adenine–thymine and guanine–cytosine  
C. Adenine–guanine and cytosine–thymine  
D. Adenine–cytosine and guanine–thymine  

**Correct Answer: B. Adenine–thymine and guanine–cytosine**

**Explanation:** In double-stranded DNA, adenine pairs with thymine through two hydrogen bonds, while guanine pairs with cytosine through three hydrogen bonds. Complementary base pairing contributes to the stability and accurate replication of DNA.

**Distractor Evaluation:**
- A: Uracil is normally found in RNA rather than DNA.
- C: Adenine–guanine and cytosine–thymine are not standard complementary DNA base pairs.
- D: These combinations do not represent normal Watson–Crick base pairing.

## Question 5: DNA Backbone

Which type of bond links adjacent nucleotides in the sugar-phosphate backbone of DNA?

A. Peptide bond  
B. Phosphodiester bond  
C. Hydrogen bond  
D. Glycosidic bond  

**Correct Answer: B. Phosphodiester bond**

**Explanation:** Phosphodiester bonds connect the 3′ hydroxyl group of one deoxyribose sugar to the 5′ phosphate group of the next nucleotide. This produces the covalent sugar-phosphate backbone of DNA.

**Distractor Evaluation:**
- A: Peptide bonds link amino acids in proteins.
- C: Hydrogen bonds contribute to interactions between complementary bases in double-stranded DNA.
- D: Glycosidic bonds link the nitrogenous base to the pentose sugar.

## Question 6: RNA Structure

Which nitrogenous base is present in RNA but normally absent from DNA?

A. Thymine  
B. Uracil  
C. Adenine  
D. Guanine  

**Correct Answer: B. Uracil**

**Explanation:** RNA generally contains uracil instead of thymine. Uracil pairs with adenine in RNA, while thymine pairs with adenine in DNA.

**Distractor Evaluation:**
- A: Thymine is normally found in DNA.
- C: Adenine is present in both DNA and RNA.
- D: Guanine is present in both DNA and RNA.

## Question 7: DNA Denaturation

What happens when double-stranded DNA undergoes denaturation?

A. The two strands separate as hydrogen-bonded base pairing is disrupted  
B. All phosphodiester bonds are immediately broken  
C. DNA is converted into protein  
D. The nitrogenous bases are completely removed from the sugar-phosphate backbone  

**Correct Answer: A. The two strands separate as hydrogen-bonded base pairing is disrupted**

**Explanation:** DNA denaturation involves the separation of complementary strands following disruption of the non-covalent interactions that stabilise the double helix. The covalent phosphodiester bonds within each strand generally remain intact.

**Distractor Evaluation:**
- B: Breaking phosphodiester bonds involves DNA degradation rather than ordinary denaturation.
- C: DNA is not converted directly into protein during denaturation.
- D: Denaturation does not normally remove nitrogenous bases from the sugar-phosphate backbone.

## Question 8: DNA Melting Temperature

Which factor generally increases the melting temperature (Tm) of double-stranded DNA?

A. Higher GC content  
B. Lower GC content in all circumstances  
C. Complete removal of hydrogen bonds  
D. Shortening every DNA molecule to a single nucleotide  

**Correct Answer: A. Higher GC content**

**Explanation:** DNA sequences with higher GC content generally have higher melting temperatures under comparable conditions. This is partly associated with the stronger stacking interactions and the three hydrogen bonds in G–C base pairs. Salt concentration, sequence composition and DNA length also influence melting temperature.

**Distractor Evaluation:**
- B: Lower GC content generally reduces melting temperature under comparable conditions.
- C: Removal of stabilising interactions would promote strand separation.
- D: A single nucleotide does not form a double-stranded DNA helix.

## Question 9: Nucleic Acid Absorbance

At which wavelength do nucleic acids exhibit maximum ultraviolet absorbance?

A. 220 nm  
B. 260 nm  
C. 280 nm  
D. 340 nm  

**Correct Answer: B. 260 nm**

**Explanation:** DNA and RNA absorb ultraviolet light strongly at approximately 260 nm because of their nitrogenous bases. This property is used in spectrophotometric estimation of nucleic acid concentration.

**Distractor Evaluation:**
- A: Absorbance around this region may be influenced by other sample components but is not the characteristic nucleic acid maximum.
- C: Absorbance around 280 nm is commonly associated with aromatic amino acids in proteins.
- D: This is not the characteristic absorbance maximum for nucleic acids.

## Question 10: DNA and RNA Stability

Why is DNA generally more chemically stable than RNA under alkaline conditions?

A. DNA lacks the 2′ hydroxyl group present in RNA  
B. DNA contains uracil instead of thymine  
C. DNA contains peptide bonds  
D. DNA is composed entirely of purine bases  

**Correct Answer: A. DNA lacks the 2′ hydroxyl group present in RNA**

**Explanation:** RNA contains a 2′ hydroxyl group that can participate in intramolecular reactions leading to cleavage of its phosphodiester backbone under alkaline conditions. DNA lacks this group and is therefore more resistant to alkaline hydrolysis.

**Distractor Evaluation:**
- B: DNA normally contains thymine, while RNA normally contains uracil.
- C: Peptide bonds are found in proteins, not DNA.
- D: DNA contains both purine and pyrimidine bases.
