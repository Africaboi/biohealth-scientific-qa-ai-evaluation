
# Evaluation 01: Enzyme Kinetics

## 1. Scientific Question

Explain the Michaelis–Menten equation, define the Michaelis constant (Km) and maximum reaction velocity (Vmax), and describe how competitive inhibition affects these kinetic parameters.

## 2. Illustrative AI-Generated Answer

The Michaelis–Menten equation describes the relationship between substrate concentration and the rate of an enzyme-catalysed reaction.

The equation is:

v = (Vmax × [S]) / (Km + [S])

Where:

- v is the initial reaction velocity.
- Vmax is the maximum reaction velocity.
- [S] is the substrate concentration.
- Km is the Michaelis constant.

Km is the substrate concentration at which the reaction velocity is half of Vmax. A lower Km generally indicates a higher apparent affinity between an enzyme and its substrate, although Km is not always a direct measure of binding affinity.

Competitive inhibitors bind to the enzyme's active site and compete with the substrate. Their presence increases the apparent Km because a higher substrate concentration is required to achieve half of Vmax. However, competitive inhibition does not change Vmax under the classical Michaelis–Menten model because sufficiently high substrate concentrations can overcome the inhibition.

## 3. Identification of Scientific Errors and Limitations

The illustrative answer is largely scientifically accurate.

However, the following qualifications are important:

1. **Interpretation of Km:** Km should not automatically be described as a direct measure of enzyme–substrate binding affinity. Its relationship to binding affinity depends on the kinetic mechanism.
2. **Competitive inhibition:** The increase in apparent Km and unchanged Vmax apply to classical reversible competitive inhibition under appropriate Michaelis–Menten conditions.
3. **Reaction conditions:** The Michaelis–Menten model assumes an initial-rate measurement, a suitable enzyme–substrate system, and conditions in which substrate depletion and product accumulation are negligible.

## 4. Scientific Verification

The Michaelis–Menten model is commonly used to describe the initial velocity of many enzyme-catalysed reactions.

The equation is:

v = (Vmax[S])/(Km + [S])

At substrate concentrations equal to Km:

v = Vmax/2

This relationship provides the operational definition of Km.

In classical competitive inhibition:

- The inhibitor competes with the substrate for enzyme binding.
- Apparent Km increases.
- Vmax remains unchanged.
- Increasing substrate concentration can reduce the inhibitor's effect.

These characteristics distinguish classical competitive inhibition from other inhibition patterns.

## 5. Corrected Scientific Answer

The Michaelis–Menten equation describes the relationship between initial reaction velocity and substrate concentration for many enzyme-catalysed reactions:

v = (Vmax[S])/(Km + [S])

Here, Vmax represents the maximum reaction velocity under the specified experimental conditions, while Km is the substrate concentration at which the initial velocity reaches half of Vmax.

Km is an apparent kinetic parameter and should not always be interpreted as a direct measure of enzyme–substrate binding affinity.

In classical reversible competitive inhibition, the inhibitor competes with the substrate for the enzyme's active site. This increases the apparent Km because more substrate is required to achieve half of Vmax. The Vmax remains unchanged because sufficiently high substrate concentrations can outcompete the inhibitor.
