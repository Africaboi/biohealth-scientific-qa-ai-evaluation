
# Evaluation 03: Lipid Metabolism

## 1. Scientific Question

Explain fatty acid beta-oxidation, describe how fatty acids are transported into the mitochondrial matrix, and discuss the regulation of fatty acid oxidation and synthesis.

## 2. Illustrative AI-Generated Answer

Fatty acid beta-oxidation is the process through which fatty acids are broken down to generate acetyl-CoA, NADH, and FADH2. It occurs mainly in the mitochondrial matrix.

Long-chain fatty acids are activated to fatty acyl-CoA before entering the mitochondrial matrix. Their transport involves the carnitine shuttle, which includes carnitine palmitoyltransferase I (CPT-I), carnitine-acylcarnitine translocase, and carnitine palmitoyltransferase II (CPT-II).

Each cycle of beta-oxidation involves four major reactions:

1. Oxidation.
2. Hydration.
3. Oxidation.
4. Thiolysis.

Each cycle generally releases one acetyl-CoA molecule and produces one NADH and one FADH2, while shortening the fatty acyl-CoA chain by two carbon atoms.

Fatty acid oxidation and synthesis are regulated in opposite directions. Malonyl-CoA inhibits CPT-I, thereby reducing the entry of long-chain fatty acids into mitochondria during active fatty acid synthesis.

## 3. Identification of Scientific Errors and Limitations

The illustrative answer is generally accurate but requires several qualifications.

1. **Location of beta-oxidation:** Although mitochondrial beta-oxidation is a major pathway, very-long-chain fatty acids are initially shortened in peroxisomes.
2. **Activation of fatty acids:** Fatty acid activation occurs before mitochondrial entry and requires ATP, producing AMP and pyrophosphate. This represents the equivalent of two high-energy phosphate bonds.
3. **Products of each cycle:** The general description applies to saturated, even-chain fatty acids. Unsaturated and odd-chain fatty acids require additional reactions.
4. **Acetyl-CoA production:** The final cycle of an even-chain fatty acid generally produces two acetyl-CoA molecules rather than one.
5. **Regulation:** Malonyl-CoA inhibits CPT-I, but hormonal and energy-status signals also regulate fatty acid metabolism.

## 4. Scientific Verification

### Fatty Acid Activation and Transport

Before oxidation, fatty acids are converted to fatty acyl-CoA by acyl-CoA synthetases.

Long-chain fatty acyl groups are transported into the mitochondrial matrix through the carnitine shuttle:

- CPT-I transfers the acyl group from CoA to carnitine on the outer mitochondrial membrane.
- Carnitine-acylcarnitine translocase transports acylcarnitine across the inner mitochondrial membrane.
- CPT-II transfers the acyl group back to CoA in the mitochondrial matrix.

CPT-I is a major regulatory point in long-chain fatty acid oxidation.

### The Four Reactions of Beta-Oxidation

Each cycle consists of:

1. **First oxidation:** Acyl-CoA dehydrogenase produces a trans-enoyl-CoA and FADH2-derived reducing equivalents.
2. **Hydration:** Enoyl-CoA hydratase adds water to form hydroxyacyl-CoA.
3. **Second oxidation:** Hydroxyacyl-CoA dehydrogenase forms ketoacyl-CoA and NADH.
4. **Thiolysis:** Thiolase releases acetyl-CoA and a fatty acyl-CoA shortened by two carbon atoms.

The shortened fatty acyl-CoA can enter another cycle.

### Regulation of Fatty Acid Metabolism

Fatty acid synthesis and oxidation are coordinated to avoid unnecessary simultaneous activity.

Important regulatory mechanisms include:

- Malonyl-CoA inhibits CPT-I and reduces mitochondrial entry of long-chain fatty acids.
- Acetyl-CoA carboxylase produces malonyl-CoA and is regulated by energy status and hormonal signals.
- AMPK inhibits acetyl-CoA carboxylase through phosphorylation, lowering malonyl-CoA availability.
- Insulin generally favours fatty acid synthesis in the fed state.
- Glucagon and other fasting-associated signals favour fatty acid mobilisation and oxidation, particularly in the liver.

## 5. Corrected Scientific Answer

Fatty acid beta-oxidation is a catabolic pathway that breaks down fatty acyl-CoA molecules into acetyl-CoA units while generating reduced cofactors that support ATP production.

For long-chain fatty acids, the process begins with activation by acyl-CoA synthetase. The resulting fatty acyl-CoA enters the mitochondrial matrix through the carnitine shuttle, involving CPT-I, carnitine-acylcarnitine translocase, and CPT-II.

The beta-oxidation cycle consists of four reactions: oxidation, hydration, oxidation, and thiolysis. Each complete cycle generally shortens the fatty acyl-CoA chain by two carbon atoms and produces one NADH and one FADH2 equivalent. Acetyl-CoA is released, with the final cycle of an even-chain fatty acid producing two acetyl-CoA molecules.

Fatty acid oxidation is regulated in coordination with fatty acid synthesis. Malonyl-CoA, produced by acetyl-CoA carboxylase, inhibits CPT-I and reduces mitochondrial uptake of long-chain fatty acids. AMPK-mediated inhibition of acetyl-CoA carboxylase lowers malonyl-CoA levels and can favour fatty acid oxidation.

Very-long-chain fatty acids may undergo initial shortening in peroxisomes before further metabolism.
