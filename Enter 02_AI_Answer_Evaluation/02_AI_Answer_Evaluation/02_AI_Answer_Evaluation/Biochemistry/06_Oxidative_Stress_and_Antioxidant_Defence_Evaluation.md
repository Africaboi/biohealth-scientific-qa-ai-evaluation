
# Evaluation 06: Oxidative Stress and Antioxidant Defence

## 1. Scientific Question

Define oxidative stress, explain the biological roles of reactive oxygen species (ROS), and discuss the enzymatic and non-enzymatic antioxidant defence systems that protect cells against oxidative damage.

## 2. Illustrative AI-Generated Answer

Oxidative stress occurs when the production of reactive oxygen species exceeds the capacity of biological antioxidant defence systems to maintain redox balance.

Reactive oxygen species include superoxide anion, hydrogen peroxide, and hydroxyl radicals. They can be generated during normal cellular metabolism, particularly through mitochondrial electron transport, as well as by enzymes such as NADPH oxidases.

At controlled levels, ROS participate in cell signalling and physiological regulation. However, excessive or poorly controlled ROS can damage lipids, proteins, and nucleic acids.

The antioxidant defence system includes enzymatic antioxidants such as:

1. Superoxide dismutase (SOD).
2. Catalase.
3. Glutathione peroxidase.

Non-enzymatic antioxidants include glutathione, vitamin C, and vitamin E.

These systems work together to limit oxidative damage and maintain cellular redox homeostasis.

## 3. Identification of Scientific Errors and Limitations

The illustrative answer is broadly accurate but requires additional clarification.

1. **Definition of oxidative stress:** Oxidative stress is not simply the presence of ROS. It involves disruption of redox regulation and signalling, or an imbalance that favours oxidative processes and causes biological disturbance.
2. **ROS classification:** Hydrogen peroxide is a reactive oxygen species but is not a free radical. Superoxide and hydroxyl radicals are free radicals.
3. **Physiological roles:** ROS are not exclusively harmful. They participate in cell signalling, immune defence, and regulation of cellular processes.
4. **Antioxidant enzyme functions:** SOD converts superoxide into hydrogen peroxide and oxygen. Catalase and glutathione peroxidase help remove hydrogen peroxide.
5. **Antioxidant interactions:** Antioxidant systems operate as interconnected networks rather than as isolated protective molecules.

## 4. Scientific Verification

### Reactive Oxygen Species

Reactive oxygen species are oxygen-containing molecules with reactive chemical properties.

Important examples include:

- Superoxide anion (O2•−).
- Hydrogen peroxide (H2O2).
- Hydroxyl radical (•OH).

They can originate from mitochondrial electron transport, NADPH oxidases, and other cellular oxidoreductase systems.

ROS have important physiological functions, including:

- Cell signalling.
- Regulation of gene expression.
- Immune-cell antimicrobial activity.
- Modulation of cellular responses to environmental stimuli.

Excessive oxidative activity can contribute to oxidative modification of cellular components.

### Enzymatic Antioxidant Defence

**Superoxide dismutase (SOD):**

Catalyses the dismutation of superoxide into hydrogen peroxide and oxygen.

**Catalase:**

Converts hydrogen peroxide into water and oxygen.

**Glutathione peroxidase (GPx):**

Uses reduced glutathione to reduce hydrogen peroxide and certain organic hydroperoxides.

### Non-Enzymatic Antioxidant Defence

Important non-enzymatic antioxidants include:

- **Glutathione:** A major intracellular redox buffer that participates in peroxide removal and cellular redox regulation.
- **Vitamin C:** A water-soluble antioxidant that can donate electrons to reactive oxidants.
- **Vitamin E:** A lipid-soluble antioxidant that helps interrupt lipid peroxidation chain reactions.
- **Other dietary and endogenous antioxidants:** Various molecules contribute to redox regulation through different mechanisms.

### Oxidative Damage

When antioxidant and repair systems cannot adequately control oxidative processes, cellular components may undergo oxidative modification.

Examples include:

- Lipid peroxidation.
- Protein oxidation.
- Oxidative DNA damage.

The biological consequences depend on the nature, location, and extent of the damage, as well as the capacity of cellular repair systems.

## 5. Corrected Scientific Answer

Oxidative stress refers to a disturbance in redox regulation that results in excessive oxidative activity or disruption of normal redox signalling and control.

Reactive oxygen species include superoxide anion, hydrogen peroxide, and hydroxyl radicals. They arise from normal cellular metabolism and enzymatic processes. At controlled levels, ROS participate in physiological signalling and immune defence. However, excessive or poorly regulated ROS can modify lipids, proteins, and nucleic acids.

Cells possess enzymatic and non-enzymatic antioxidant systems to regulate ROS and maintain redox homeostasis.

Major enzymatic antioxidants include:

- Superoxide dismutase, which converts superoxide into hydrogen peroxide and oxygen.
- Catalase, which converts hydrogen peroxide into water and oxygen.
- Glutathione peroxidase, which uses reduced glutathione to reduce hydrogen peroxide and organic hydroperoxides.

Non-enzymatic antioxidants include glutathione, vitamin C, and vitamin E.

These antioxidant systems function together with cellular repair and regulatory mechanisms to control oxidative processes and preserve normal cellular function.
