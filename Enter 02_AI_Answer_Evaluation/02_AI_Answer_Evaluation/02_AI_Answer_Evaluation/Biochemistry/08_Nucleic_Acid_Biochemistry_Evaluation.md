
# AI Answer Evaluation: Nucleic Acid Biochemistry

## 1. Scientific Question

Explain the structural differences between DNA and RNA, describe the major types of RNA, and discuss the roles of nucleic acids in the storage, transmission, and expression of genetic information.

## 2. Illustrative AI-Generated Answer

DNA and RNA are nucleic acids composed of nucleotide units. DNA contains deoxyribose sugar and the nitrogenous bases adenine, thymine, guanine, and cytosine. RNA contains ribose sugar and uses uracil instead of thymine.

DNA is generally double-stranded, whereas RNA is generally single-stranded. DNA stores hereditary information, while RNA participates in gene expression and protein synthesis.

The three major types of RNA are messenger RNA (mRNA), transfer RNA (tRNA), and ribosomal RNA (rRNA). mRNA carries genetic instructions from DNA to ribosomes, tRNA delivers amino acids during translation, and rRNA forms part of the ribosome and contributes to peptide bond formation.

DNA replication allows genetic information to be transmitted to daughter cells. Transcription produces RNA from a DNA template, while translation uses the information in mRNA to synthesise proteins.

## 3. Identification of Scientific Errors and Limitations

The illustrative answer is broadly accurate but could be improved in the following areas:

1. **DNA and RNA structure:** It correctly identifies the sugar and base differences but does not explain the significance of the 2'-hydroxyl group in RNA, which contributes to its greater chemical reactivity compared with DNA.

2. **Strandedness:** Describing DNA as double-stranded and RNA as single-stranded is a useful generalisation, but exceptions exist. Some viruses have single-stranded DNA or double-stranded RNA genomes, and RNA molecules can form complex secondary and tertiary structures.

3. **RNA functions:** The answer identifies three major RNA types but does not mention other functional RNA molecules, such as microRNA, small nuclear RNA, and long non-coding RNA.

4. **Protein synthesis:** The relationship between transcription and translation is described correctly but could better distinguish transcription in prokaryotic and eukaryotic cells.

5. **DNA replication:** The answer mentions replication without explaining its semiconservative nature.

6. **Genetic information:** The answer could clarify that not all RNA molecules encode proteins. Many RNA molecules have regulatory, structural, catalytic, or processing functions.

## 4. Scientific Verification

The following established principles support the evaluation:

- Nucleic acids are polymers of nucleotides linked by phosphodiester bonds.
- DNA contains deoxyribose, whereas RNA contains ribose with a hydroxyl group at the 2' carbon.
- DNA normally uses thymine, while RNA normally uses uracil.
- Complementary base pairing in DNA involves A–T and G–C. In RNA, adenine commonly pairs with uracil, while guanine pairs with cytosine.

## 5. Corrected Scientific Answer

DNA and RNA are nucleic acids composed of nucleotide monomers. Each nucleotide contains a nitrogenous base, a pentose sugar, and a phosphate group. Nucleotides are joined by 3'–5' phosphodiester bonds to form polynucleotide chains.

**Structural differences**

DNA contains 2-deoxyribose sugar and the nitrogenous bases adenine, thymine, guanine, and cytosine. It usually occurs as a double-stranded helix in which complementary strands are held together by hydrogen bonding between base pairs.

RNA contains ribose sugar, which has a hydroxyl group at the 2' carbon, and uses uracil instead of thymine. RNA is commonly single-stranded, although it can fold into complex secondary and tertiary structures through intramolecular base pairing.

**Major types of RNA**

- **Messenger RNA (mRNA):** Carries genetic information copied from DNA to ribosomes, where its codons guide protein synthesis.
- **Transfer RNA (tRNA):** Delivers specific amino acids to the ribosome and uses its anticodon to recognise complementary mRNA codons.
- **Ribosomal RNA (rRNA):** Forms the structural and catalytic core of ribosomes and participates in peptide bond formation.

Other important RNA molecules include microRNAs, small nuclear RNAs, and long non-coding RNAs, which participate in gene regulation and RNA processing.

**Roles of nucleic acids in genetic information**

DNA serves as the principal hereditary material in cellular organisms. Its sequence stores genetic information, which is transmitted during cell division through semiconservative DNA replication.

During transcription, RNA polymerase synthesises an RNA molecule using one DNA strand as its template. In eukaryotes, primary RNA transcripts may undergo processing, including 5' capping, splicing, and polyadenylation, depending on the RNA type.

During translation, ribosomes interpret mRNA codons, while tRNAs deliver amino acids for polypeptide synthesis. The resulting polypeptide can fold and undergo further processing to become a functional protein.

Thus, DNA and RNA work together in the storage, transmission, regulation, and expression of genetic information.
