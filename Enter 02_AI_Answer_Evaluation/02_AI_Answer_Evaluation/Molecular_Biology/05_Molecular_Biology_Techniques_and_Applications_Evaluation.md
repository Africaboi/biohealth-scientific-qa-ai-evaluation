## 2. Scientific Question

Discuss the principles and applications of polymerase chain reaction (PCR), gel electrophoresis, DNA sequencing, restriction enzyme analysis and recombinant DNA technology. Explain how these techniques contribute to molecular biology research and disease diagnosis.

## 3. Illustrative AI-Generated Answer

Molecular biology techniques are laboratory methods used to investigate DNA, RNA and proteins. They are important in genetic research, disease diagnosis, biotechnology and forensic investigations.

Polymerase chain reaction (PCR) is a technique used to amplify a specific DNA sequence. It involves repeated cycles of denaturation, annealing and extension. During denaturation, double-stranded DNA separates into single strands. During annealing, primers bind to complementary target sequences. During extension, DNA polymerase synthesises new DNA strands.

Gel electrophoresis is used to separate DNA fragments according to their size. DNA samples are loaded into wells in an agarose gel and subjected to an electric field. Because DNA carries a negatively charged phosphate backbone, it migrates towards the positive electrode. Smaller DNA fragments generally move through the gel more rapidly than larger fragments.

DNA sequencing determines the order of nucleotides in a DNA molecule. Sanger sequencing uses chain-terminating nucleotides to generate DNA fragments of different lengths, which are analysed to determine the nucleotide sequence. Next-generation sequencing technologies allow large numbers of DNA fragments to be sequenced simultaneously.

Restriction enzymes recognise specific DNA sequences and cut DNA at or near their recognition sites. They are useful in DNA analysis and recombinant DNA technology.

Recombinant DNA technology involves combining DNA sequences from different sources to produce a DNA molecule with a desired combination of genetic information. Restriction enzymes and DNA ligase are commonly used in traditional cloning approaches to insert DNA fragments into suitable vectors.

These techniques have applications in genetic testing, pathogen identification, gene analysis, biotechnology and medical research.

## 6. Corrected and Improved Scientific Answer

Molecular biology techniques are methods used to study nucleic acids and their biological functions. They support research into gene structure, genetic variation, gene expression, disease mechanisms and biotechnology.

### Polymerase Chain Reaction

Polymerase chain reaction (PCR) is an in vitro method used to amplify a selected DNA region. It involves repeated cycles of denaturation, primer annealing and extension.

During denaturation, double-stranded DNA separates into single strands. During annealing, sequence-specific primers bind to complementary regions of the template DNA. During extension, a thermostable DNA polymerase incorporates deoxyribonucleotides to synthesise new DNA strands.

PCR is widely applied in genetic analysis, molecular diagnostics, pathogen identification and research.

### Gel Electrophoresis

Gel electrophoresis separates nucleic acid fragments according to their movement through a porous gel under an electric field.

DNA carries a net negative charge because of its phosphate backbone and therefore migrates towards the positive electrode. Smaller linear DNA fragments generally move through an agarose gel more rapidly than larger fragments.

DNA size markers provide reference bands for estimating fragment lengths. The technique is useful for assessing PCR products, analysing restriction fragments and examining DNA samples.

### DNA Sequencing

DNA sequencing determines the nucleotide order of a DNA molecule.

Sanger sequencing uses normal deoxyribonucleotides together with chain-terminating dideoxyribonucleotides. Incorporation of a dideoxyribonucleotide terminates DNA extension, generating fragments that can be analysed to determine sequence information.

Next-generation sequencing technologies enable many DNA fragments to be sequenced in parallel. They are widely used in genome analysis, transcriptomics, pathogen surveillance and genetic research.

Sequencing data require appropriate quality assessment, bioinformatic analysis and biological interpretation.

### Restriction Enzyme Analysis

Restriction enzymes are nucleases that recognise particular DNA sequences and cleave DNA at defined positions. Depending on the enzyme, cleavage may generate blunt ends or cohesive ends.

Restriction enzymes are useful in DNA mapping, genetic analysis and traditional recombinant DNA methods.

### Recombinant DNA Technology

Recombinant DNA technology involves joining DNA sequences to create a DNA molecule containing a desired combination of genetic information.

Traditional approaches commonly use suitable DNA vectors, restriction enzymes and DNA ligase. Vectors can carry inserted DNA sequences into appropriate host systems, where the DNA may be maintained, analysed or expressed.

Other modern DNA assembly methods use alternative approaches to join DNA fragments.

### Applications and Quality Assurance

Molecular biology techniques are important in the diagnosis and investigation of genetic conditions, identification of infectious agents, analysis of gene expression, forensic investigations and biotechnology.

Reliable results depend on appropriate experimental controls, validated analytical methods, accurate sample identification and careful interpretation of findings.

No single molecular technique answers every biological question. The choice of method depends on the target molecule, research objective, required sensitivity, sample characteristics and limitations of the assay.
