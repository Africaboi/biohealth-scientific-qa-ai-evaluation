## 2. Scientific Question

An enzyme-catalysed reaction follows Michaelis–Menten kinetics. The maximum reaction velocity (Vmax) is 100 µmol/min, while the Michaelis constant (Km) is 5 mmol/L.

What is the reaction velocity when the substrate concentration is 5 mmol/L?

## 3. AI-Generated Answer (Illustrative Sample)

**AI Response:**

The reaction velocity is 100 µmol/min because the substrate concentration is equal to the Michaelis constant. At this concentration, the enzyme becomes fully saturated and operates at its maximum velocity.

## 4. Scientific Assessment

The response contains a significant conceptual error concerning the interpretation of the Michaelis constant.

Under the standard Michaelis–Menten model, Km is the substrate concentration at which the initial reaction velocity is half of Vmax.

Therefore, when substrate concentration equals Km, the enzyme does not operate at its maximum velocity.

The correct reaction velocity is 50 µmol/min.

## 5. Identification of Scientific Errors

### Error 1: Incorrect interpretation of Km

**AI Claim:** When substrate concentration reaches Km, the enzyme becomes fully saturated.

**Assessment:** Incorrect.

**Scientific Correction:** Km is the substrate concentration at which the reaction velocity is half of Vmax under Michaelis–Menten conditions. It is not the concentration at which the enzyme becomes fully saturated.

### Error 2: Incorrect calculation of reaction velocity

**AI Claim:** The reaction velocity is 100 µmol/min.

**Assessment:** Incorrect.

**Scientific Correction:** When substrate concentration equals Km, reaction velocity is half of Vmax.

Therefore:

v = ½ × 100

**Correct reaction velocity = 50 µmol/min.**

## 6. Evidence-Based Scientific Verification

The Michaelis–Menten equation is:

v = (Vmax × [S]) / (Km + [S])

Where:

- v = Initial reaction velocity
- Vmax = Maximum reaction velocity
- [S] = Substrate concentration
- Km = Michaelis constant

Given:

- Vmax = 100 µmol/min
- Km = 5 mmol/L
- [S] = 5 mmol/L

Substituting:

v = (100 × 5) / (5 + 5)

v = 500 / 10

**v = 50 µmol/min.**

The calculation confirms that the AI-generated answer is incorrect.

## 7. Corrected Scientific Answer

When the substrate concentration equals the Michaelis constant (Km), the reaction velocity is half of the maximum velocity (Vmax), according to the Michaelis–Menten model.

Therefore, the reaction velocity is:

**50 µmol/min.**

This relationship is a fundamental feature of Michaelis–Menten enzyme kinetics.
