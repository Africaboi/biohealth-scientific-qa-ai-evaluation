## 2. Scientific Question

Which statement correctly describes the structural and chemical differences between DNA and RNA?

A. DNA contains ribose and uracil, whereas RNA contains deoxyribose and thymine.

B. DNA generally contains deoxyribose and thymine, whereas RNA contains ribose and uracil.

C. DNA and RNA have identical sugar components and nitrogenous bases.

D. RNA is always double-stranded, while DNA is always single-stranded.

## 3. AI-Generated Answer (Illustrative Sample)

**AI Response:**

DNA contains ribose sugar and uracil, while RNA contains deoxyribose and thymine. Both molecules have identical structures and are always double-stranded. The phosphodiester bonds connect complementary nitrogenous bases across the two strands.

## 4. Scientific Assessment

The illustrative AI response contains major errors concerning the chemical composition and structural organisation of nucleic acids.

DNA and RNA are nucleic acids composed of nucleotide units. Each nucleotide contains a nitrogenous base, a pentose sugar, and phosphate.

DNA generally contains deoxyribose and the nitrogenous bases adenine, guanine, cytosine, and thymine.

RNA generally contains ribose and the bases adenine, guanine, cytosine, and uracil.

DNA is commonly double-stranded, whereas many biological RNA molecules are single-stranded and can fold into complex secondary and tertiary structures.

Phosphodiester bonds connect nucleotides along each nucleic acid strand. Hydrogen bonds contribute to complementary base pairing between strands or within folded RNA molecules.

## 5. Identification of Scientific Errors

### Error 1: Incorrect identification of pentose sugars

**AI Claim:** DNA contains ribose, while RNA contains deoxyribose.

**Assessment:** Incorrect. The sugar components have been interchanged.

**Scientific Correction:**
- DNA contains 2-deoxyribose.
- RNA contains ribose.

The difference is the presence of a hydroxyl group at the 2′ carbon of ribose, which is absent in deoxyribose.

### Error 2: Incorrect identification of nitrogenous bases

**AI Claim:** DNA contains uracil, while RNA contains thymine.

**Assessment:** Incorrect as a description of the usual canonical bases.

**Scientific Correction:**
- DNA generally contains adenine, guanine, cytosine, and thymine.
- RNA generally contains adenine, guanine, cytosine, and uracil.

Some specialised nucleic acids and biological contexts contain modified bases, but these do not change the standard distinction.

### Error 3: Incorrect claim that DNA and RNA are structurally identical

**AI Claim:** DNA and RNA have identical structures.

**Assessment:** Incorrect.

**Scientific Correction:** DNA and RNA differ in their sugar components, usual base composition, structural tendencies, and biological functions.

DNA commonly forms a double-stranded helix, while RNA frequently exists as a single strand that can fold into complex structures.

### Error 4: Incorrect description of strand linkage

**AI Claim:** Phosphodiester bonds connect complementary nitrogenous bases across the two strands.

**Assessment:** Incorrect.

**Scientific Correction:** Phosphodiester bonds connect adjacent nucleotides within the same strand.

Hydrogen bonds contribute to complementary base pairing between nucleic acid strands.

In standard DNA base pairing:
- Adenine pairs with thymine.
- Guanine pairs with cytosine.

In RNA, adenine commonly pairs with uracil.

## 6. Evidence-Based Scientific Verification

### Nucleotide Structure

A nucleotide consists of:

- A nitrogenous base.
- A pentose sugar.
- One or more phosphate groups.

A nucleoside contains a nitrogenous base linked to a sugar but does not include phosphate.

### DNA Structure

DNA is usually organised as two antiparallel polynucleotide strands.

The sugar-phosphate backbones are located on the outside of the double helix, while complementary bases are positioned towards the interior.

The strands are stabilised by hydrogen bonding between complementary bases and by base-stacking interactions.

### RNA Structure

RNA contains ribose and uses uracil instead of thymine as its usual pyrimidine base.

Although many RNA molecules are single-stranded, they can form intramolecular base-paired regions and complex three-dimensional structures.

Examples include messenger RNA, transfer RNA, and ribosomal RNA.

### Nucleic Acid Stability

DNA is generally more resistant to alkaline hydrolysis than RNA because RNA contains a 2′-hydroxyl group.

This hydroxyl group contributes to RNA's susceptibility to backbone cleavage under alkaline conditions.

The structural and chemical differences between DNA and RNA are important for their biological roles in genetic information storage, gene expression, and protein synthesis.

## 7. Corrected Scientific Answer

**Correct option: B.**

DNA generally contains deoxyribose and thymine, whereas RNA contains ribose and uracil.

DNA is commonly double-stranded, while many RNA molecules are single-stranded and can fold into complex structures.

Phosphodiester bonds link nucleotides within each strand, while hydrogen bonds contribute to complementary base pairing.
