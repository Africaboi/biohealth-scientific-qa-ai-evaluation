
# Helminthology

## Question 1

Which characteristic distinguishes nematodes from cestodes and trematodes?

A. Nematodes are unicellular organisms.

B. Nematodes are generally cylindrical, unsegmented roundworms with a complete digestive tract.

C. Nematodes possess a flattened, leaf-shaped body.

D. Nematodes lack reproductive organs.

**Correct Answer: B**

**Explanation:** Nematodes are generally elongated, cylindrical and unsegmented worms. Most possess a complete digestive tract with separate mouth and anus openings. Their body is covered by a protective cuticle.

**Distractor Evaluation:**
- A: Incorrect. Nematodes are multicellular animals.
- C: Incorrect. This description is more characteristic of trematodes.
- D: Incorrect. Nematodes possess reproductive structures, and many species have separate sexes.

**Quality Assurance Note:** Body shape, segmentation and digestive anatomy are useful features for distinguishing major helminth groups.

## Question 2

Which helminth is commonly referred to as the giant intestinal roundworm of humans?

A. Enterobius vermicularis.

B. Ascaris lumbricoides.

C. Ancylostoma duodenale.

D. Trichuris trichiura.

**Correct Answer: B**

**Explanation:** Ascaris lumbricoides is a large intestinal nematode that causes ascariasis. Infection occurs through ingestion of embryonated eggs in contaminated food, water or soil.

**Distractor Evaluation:**
- A: Incorrect. Enterobius vermicularis is the pinworm.
- C: Incorrect. Ancylostoma duodenale is a hookworm.
- D: Incorrect. Trichuris trichiura is the whipworm.

**Quality Assurance Note:** Common names should be matched accurately with scientific names and associated diseases.

## Question 3

Which helminth is most strongly associated with iron-deficiency anaemia due to intestinal blood loss?

A. Enterobius vermicularis.

B. Taenia saginata.

C. Ancylostoma duodenale.

D. Ascaris lumbricoides.

**Correct Answer: C**

**Explanation:** Ancylostoma duodenale is a hookworm that attaches to the intestinal mucosa and feeds on blood. Chronic infection can result in intestinal blood loss and iron-deficiency anaemia.

**Distractor Evaluation:**
- A: Incorrect. Enterobius vermicularis typically causes perianal itching.
- B: Incorrect. Taenia saginata is a beef tapeworm and is not characteristically associated with chronic intestinal blood loss.
- D: Incorrect. Ascaris lumbricoides can cause intestinal obstruction and nutritional complications but is not the classic cause of hookworm-associated blood loss.

**Quality Assurance Note:** Clinical manifestations should be linked to the parasite's biological characteristics.

## Question 4

Which of the following is the usual infective stage of Taenia saginata for humans?

A. Cysticercus in undercooked beef.

B. Embryonated egg in contaminated water.

C. Cercaria in freshwater.

D. Microfilaria in mosquito saliva.

**Correct Answer: A**

**Explanation:** Humans acquire Taenia saginata infection by eating raw or inadequately cooked beef containing viable cysticerci. The cysticerci develop into adult tapeworms in the human small intestine.

**Distractor Evaluation:**
- B: Incorrect. Ingestion of eggs is not the usual route of human intestinal infection with T. saginata.
- C: Incorrect. Cercariae are associated with the transmission of certain trematodes, including Schistosoma species.
- D: Incorrect. Microfilariae are developmental stages of filarial nematodes.

**Quality Assurance Note:** The infective stage and route of transmission must be distinguished for each helminth species.

## Question 5

Which parasite is associated with human cysticercosis?

A. Taenia saginata.

B. Taenia solium.

C. Fasciola hepatica.

D. Enterobius vermicularis.

**Correct Answer: B**

**Explanation:** Human cysticercosis results from ingestion of Taenia solium eggs. The released larvae can migrate into tissues and develop into cysticerci. When the central nervous system is involved, the condition is known as neurocysticercosis.

**Distractor Evaluation:**
- A: Incorrect. Taenia saginata causes intestinal taeniasis but is not responsible for human cysticercosis.
- C: Incorrect. Fasciola hepatica is a liver fluke.
- D: Incorrect. Enterobius vermicularis is associated with enterobiasis.

**Quality Assurance Note:** Taeniasis and cysticercosis are distinct clinical conditions caused by different developmental stages of T. solium.

## Question 6

Which structure is used by adult Schistosoma species to attach to blood vessel walls?

A. Suckers.

B. Hooks arranged in a scolex.

C. Jointed appendages.

D. Cilia.

**Correct Answer: A**

**Explanation:** Adult Schistosoma worms possess oral and ventral suckers that help them maintain attachment within blood vessels. Unlike many other trematodes, schistosomes have separate male and female worms.

**Distractor Evaluation:**
- B: Incorrect. A scolex with hooks is characteristic of some cestodes.
- C: Incorrect. Jointed appendages are characteristic of arthropods.
- D: Incorrect. Cilia are not the attachment structures of adult Schistosoma.

**Quality Assurance Note:** Schistosomes are an important exception to the usual hermaphroditic condition of many trematodes.

## Question 7

Which intermediate host is essential in the life cycle of Schistosoma species?

A. Freshwater snail.

B. Cattle.

C. Housefly.

D. Domestic chicken.

**Correct Answer: A**

**Explanation:** Freshwater snails serve as intermediate hosts for Schistosoma species. Asexual multiplication occurs within the snail, producing cercariae that are released into freshwater.

**Distractor Evaluation:**
- B: Incorrect. Cattle are not the essential intermediate host for human Schistosoma species.
- C: Incorrect. Houseflies are not involved in the normal Schistosoma life cycle.
- D: Incorrect. Domestic chickens are not the essential intermediate host.

**Quality Assurance Note:** Identification of intermediate hosts is important for understanding parasite transmission and environmental control.

## Question 8

Which helminth is commonly diagnosed using the perianal adhesive tape test?

A. Ascaris lumbricoides.

B. Enterobius vermicularis.

C. Schistosoma mansoni.

D. Taenia solium.

**Correct Answer: B**

**Explanation:** Enterobius vermicularis, commonly called the pinworm, deposits eggs around the perianal region, particularly at night. The adhesive tape test is used to collect eggs for microscopic examination.

**Distractor Evaluation:**
- A: Incorrect. Ascaris infection is commonly investigated through stool examination for eggs.
- C: Incorrect. Schistosoma mansoni diagnosis may involve stool examination for characteristic eggs.
- D: Incorrect. Taenia solium infection is commonly investigated through stool examination for eggs or proglottids, although additional methods may be needed for tissue infection.

**Quality Assurance Note:** Diagnostic methods should be selected according to the parasite's egg-laying behaviour and anatomical location.

## Question 9

Which of the following best describes lymphatic filariasis?

A. A disease caused by intestinal tapeworms.

B. A disease caused by filarial nematodes that affect the lymphatic system.

C. A disease caused by liver flukes that obstruct bile ducts.

D. A disease caused by protozoa that invade red blood cells.

**Correct Answer: B**

**Explanation:** Lymphatic filariasis is caused by filarial nematodes, principally Wuchereria bancrofti, Brugia malayi and Brugia timori. Chronic infection can lead to lymphatic dysfunction and lymphoedema.

**Distractor Evaluation:**
- A: Incorrect. Intestinal tapeworms are cestodes.
- C: Incorrect. Liver flukes can cause hepatobiliary disease but do not cause lymphatic filariasis.
- D: Incorrect. Red blood cell invasion is characteristic of malaria parasites.

**Quality Assurance Note:** The causative organism, affected anatomical system and disease manifestation should be correctly linked.

## Question 10

Which statement best explains why helminth infections can persist in human populations?

A. All helminths reproduce exclusively within human blood.

B. Their complex life cycles, environmental survival stages and intermediate hosts can sustain transmission.

C. Helminths are unaffected by environmental conditions.

D. All helminths are transmitted directly from one human to another.

**Correct Answer: B**

**Explanation:** Many helminths have complex life cycles involving environmental stages, intermediate hosts or other animal reservoirs. These features can maintain transmission within communities, particularly where sanitation, safe water and preventive measures are inadequate.

**Distractor Evaluation:**
- A: Incorrect. Helminths occupy different anatomical locations and have diverse reproductive strategies.
- C: Incorrect. Environmental conditions strongly influence the development and survival of many helminths.
- D: Incorrect. Transmission routes vary and may involve soil, food, water, vectors or intermediate hosts.

**Quality Assurance Note:** Understanding transmission ecology is essential for designing effective helminth control programmes.
