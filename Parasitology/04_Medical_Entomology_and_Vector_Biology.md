
# Medical Entomology and Vector Biology

## Question 1

Medical entomology is primarily concerned with the study of:

A. All microorganisms that cause human disease.

B. Arthropods of medical importance and their roles in disease transmission.

C. The chemical composition of human blood.

D. The classification of intestinal helminths.

**Correct Answer: B**

**Explanation:** Medical entomology examines arthropods that affect human health, including mosquitoes, ticks, fleas, lice and other medically important insects and arachnids. Some act as vectors of pathogens, while others cause direct harm through bites, infestation or allergic reactions.

**Distractor Evaluation:**
- A: Incorrect. This describes a broader area of medical microbiology.
- C: Incorrect. This is associated with haematology and clinical biochemistry.
- D: Incorrect. This falls within helminthology.

**Quality Assurance Note:** Medical entomology includes both disease-vector biology and the direct medical effects of arthropods.

## Question 2

Which mosquito genus is the principal vector of human malaria?

A. Aedes.

B. Culex.

C. Anopheles.

D. Mansonia.

**Correct Answer: C**

**Explanation:** Female mosquitoes belonging to the genus Anopheles transmit human malaria parasites. During blood feeding, an infected mosquito can introduce Plasmodium sporozoites into a human host.

**Distractor Evaluation:**
- A: Incorrect. Aedes mosquitoes transmit diseases such as dengue, yellow fever and chikungunya.
- B: Incorrect. Some Culex species transmit lymphatic filariasis and arboviruses.
- D: Incorrect. Certain Mansonia species are associated with the transmission of lymphatic filariasis.

**Quality Assurance Note:** Vector identification should be linked to the specific pathogen and transmission cycle.

## Question 3

Which statement correctly describes the role of a female mosquito in malaria transmission?

A. It transmits malaria because it feeds exclusively on infected human blood.

B. It serves as the definitive host of Plasmodium because sexual reproduction occurs within the mosquito.

C. It serves only as a mechanical carrier without supporting parasite development.

D. It transmits malaria through contaminated mosquito faeces.

**Correct Answer: B**

**Explanation:** The female Anopheles mosquito is the definitive host of Plasmodium because sexual reproduction occurs within it. The parasite undergoes development in the mosquito before sporozoites reach the salivary glands.

**Distractor Evaluation:**
- A: Incorrect. Female mosquitoes require blood meals for egg development, but they do not feed exclusively on infected blood.
- C: Incorrect. Plasmodium undergoes essential developmental and reproductive stages within the mosquito.
- D: Incorrect. Malaria transmission occurs through the bite of an infected female Anopheles mosquito.

**Quality Assurance Note:** The biological role of the mosquito is fundamental to understanding the malaria life cycle.

## Question 4

Which arthropod is the principal vector of African trypanosomiasis?

A. Sandfly.

B. Tsetse fly.

C. Blackfly.

D. Housefly.

**Correct Answer: B**

**Explanation:** Tsetse flies of the genus Glossina transmit Trypanosoma brucei, the causative organism of human African trypanosomiasis.

**Distractor Evaluation:**
- A: Incorrect. Sandflies transmit Leishmania parasites.
- C: Incorrect. Blackflies of the genus Simulium transmit Onchocerca volvulus.
- D: Incorrect. Houseflies may mechanically carry microorganisms but are not the biological vector of African trypanosomiasis.

**Quality Assurance Note:** Distinguishing biological vectors is important in identifying appropriate disease prevention strategies.

## Question 5

Which arthropod is associated with the transmission of Leishmania parasites?

A. Phlebotomine sandfly.

B. Anopheles mosquito.

C. Tsetse fly.

D. Body louse.

**Correct Answer: A**

**Explanation:** Phlebotomine sandflies transmit Leishmania parasites during blood feeding. Depending on the parasite species, infection may produce cutaneous, mucocutaneous or visceral leishmaniasis.

**Distractor Evaluation:**
- B: Incorrect. Anopheles mosquitoes transmit malaria parasites.
- C: Incorrect. Tsetse flies transmit African trypanosomes.
- D: Incorrect. Body lice are associated with the transmission of certain bacterial diseases, not leishmaniasis.

**Quality Assurance Note:** The vector–parasite relationship should be interpreted at the appropriate taxonomic and epidemiological level.

## Question 6

Which vector is responsible for transmitting Onchocerca volvulus, the causative organism of river blindness?

A. Blackfly of the genus Simulium.

B. Mosquito of the genus Aedes.

C. Flea of the genus Xenopsylla.

D. Tick of the genus Ixodes.

**Correct Answer: A**

**Explanation:** Onchocerca volvulus is transmitted by infected female blackflies belonging to the genus Simulium. The disease is associated with skin and eye complications and is particularly prevalent in areas near suitable flowing-water breeding habitats.

**Distractor Evaluation:**
- B: Incorrect. Aedes mosquitoes transmit several arboviruses.
- C: Incorrect. Certain Xenopsylla fleas are associated with plague transmission.
- D: Incorrect. Ixodes ticks transmit pathogens responsible for diseases such as Lyme disease.

**Quality Assurance Note:** Vector ecology, including breeding habitat, can influence the geographical distribution of vector-borne diseases.

## Question 7

Which of the following is an example of mechanical transmission by an arthropod?

A. Development of Plasmodium within an Anopheles mosquito.

B. Multiplication of a pathogen within a biological vector.

C. Transfer of microorganisms on the body surface of a housefly without essential development in the fly.

D. Sexual reproduction of a parasite within an insect vector.

**Correct Answer: C**

**Explanation:** Mechanical transmission occurs when an arthropod passively transfers an infectious agent from one location or host to another without the pathogen undergoing essential development or multiplication within the arthropod.

**Distractor Evaluation:**
- A: Incorrect. This involves biological development within the mosquito.
- B: Incorrect. This describes biological transmission.
- D: Incorrect. Sexual reproduction within an arthropod is a feature of biological transmission.

**Quality Assurance Note:** Mechanical and biological transmission are distinguished by whether the pathogen undergoes essential development or multiplication in the vector.

## Question 8

Which characteristic is commonly used to distinguish ticks from insects?

A. Ticks have six legs throughout their development.

B. Adult ticks generally have four pairs of legs, whereas adult insects have three pairs.

C. Ticks possess wings, whereas insects do not.

D. Ticks are unicellular organisms.

**Correct Answer: B**

**Explanation:** Ticks are arachnids belonging to the class Arachnida. Nymphs and adults generally possess four pairs of legs, while larval ticks possess three pairs. Adult insects typically have three pairs of legs.

**Distractor Evaluation:**
- A: Incorrect. Larval ticks have three pairs of legs, but nymphs and adults generally have four pairs.
- C: Incorrect. Ticks are wingless arachnids.
- D: Incorrect. Ticks are multicellular arthropods.

**Quality Assurance Note:** Developmental stage matters when comparing tick anatomy because larval ticks differ from nymphs and adults.

## Question 9

Which mosquito genus is particularly associated with the transmission of dengue virus?

A. Aedes.

B. Anopheles.

C. Glossina.

D. Simulium.

**Correct Answer: A**

**Explanation:** Aedes mosquitoes, particularly Aedes aegypti, are important vectors of dengue virus. Aedes albopictus can also contribute to transmission in suitable ecological settings.

**Distractor Evaluation:**
- B: Incorrect. Anopheles mosquitoes are the principal vectors of human malaria.
- C: Incorrect. Glossina transmits African trypanosomes.
- D: Incorrect. Simulium transmits Onchocerca volvulus.

**Quality Assurance Note:** Vector competence and geographical distribution influence the epidemiology of dengue.

## Question 10

Why is understanding vector ecology important in controlling vector-borne diseases?

A. It proves that all vectors transmit the same pathogens.

B. It helps identify environmental and biological factors that influence vector survival, distribution and disease transmission.

C. It eliminates the need for disease surveillance.

D. It ensures that every vector can be controlled using identical methods.

**Correct Answer: B**

**Explanation:** Vector ecology examines factors such as breeding habitats, temperature, humidity, host availability and seasonal abundance. Understanding these factors supports evidence-based vector surveillance and integrated disease prevention.

**Distractor Evaluation:**
- A: Incorrect. Different vectors transmit different pathogens, although some vectors may transmit more than one pathogen.
- C: Incorrect. Disease surveillance remains essential for evaluating transmission patterns and intervention outcomes.
- D: Incorrect. Vector-control approaches must be adapted to vector biology and local environmental conditions.

**Quality Assurance Note:** Effective vector control requires an understanding of local ecology, vector behaviour and disease transmission patterns.
