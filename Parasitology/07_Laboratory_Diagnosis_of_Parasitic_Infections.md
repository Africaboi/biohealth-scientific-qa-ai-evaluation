
# Laboratory Diagnosis of Parasitic Infections

## Question 1
Which microscopic feature is most useful for identifying *Plasmodium* species in a properly prepared thick and thin blood film?

A. Presence of bacterial endospores  
B. Morphology of parasite stages within red blood cells  
C. Presence of fungal hyphae  
D. Arrangement of leukocytes in clusters  

**Correct Answer: B. Morphology of parasite stages within red blood cells**

**Explanation:** Microscopic examination of stained blood films allows identification of malaria parasites. Thick films are more sensitive for detecting parasites, while thin films preserve red blood cell morphology and help with species identification.

**Distractor Evaluation:**
- A: Endospores are bacterial structures.
- C: Hyphae are characteristic of filamentous fungi.
- D: Leukocyte arrangement is not a diagnostic feature of malaria species.

**Quality Assurance Note:** Blood film quality, staining, and microscopist expertise influence diagnostic accuracy.

## Question 2
What is the main advantage of a thick blood film over a thin blood film in malaria diagnosis?

A. Better preservation of red blood cell morphology  
B. Improved detection of low-level parasitaemia  
C. Automatic identification of drug resistance  
D. Detection of all blood-borne parasites  

**Correct Answer: B. Improved detection of low-level parasitaemia**

**Explanation:** Thick films concentrate a larger volume of blood in a smaller examination area, increasing the likelihood of detecting malaria parasites when parasite density is low.

**Distractor Evaluation:**
- A: Thin films better preserve red blood cell morphology.
- C: Routine microscopy does not establish drug resistance.
- D: Thick films do not detect every blood-borne parasite.

**Quality Assurance Note:** A negative result should be interpreted in relation to specimen quality, parasite density, and clinical context.

## Question 3
Which technique is commonly used to concentrate intestinal parasite eggs and cysts from stool specimens?

A. Formalin–ethyl acetate sedimentation  
B. Gram staining  
C. Ziehl–Neelsen staining  
D. Acid-fast bacterial culture  

**Correct Answer: A. Formalin–ethyl acetate sedimentation**

**Explanation:** Formalin–ethyl acetate sedimentation is a stool concentration technique that helps recover many helminth eggs and protozoan cysts for microscopic examination.

**Distractor Evaluation:**
- B: Gram staining is primarily used for bacterial classification.
- C: Ziehl–Neelsen staining is used for acid-fast organisms and modified methods may help detect certain coccidian parasites.
- D: Acid-fast bacterial culture is not a routine stool parasite concentration method.

**Quality Assurance Note:** Concentration methods improve detection but may not recover every parasite stage equally well.

## Question 4
Which statement best describes the Kato–Katz technique?

A. It is a molecular technique for detecting parasite DNA.  
B. It is a quantitative stool thick-smear method used to estimate helminth egg intensity.  
C. It is a serological test for detecting parasite antibodies.  
D. It is a culture method for growing intestinal protozoa.

**Correct Answer: B. It is a quantitative stool thick-smear method used to estimate helminth egg intensity.**

**Explanation:** Kato–Katz is a parasitological technique used particularly in epidemiological surveys of soil-transmitted helminths and schistosomiasis. Egg counts can be expressed as eggs per gram of faeces.

**Distractor Evaluation:**
- A: Molecular tests detect nucleic acids.
- C: Serological tests detect antibodies or antigens.
- D: Kato–Katz does not involve parasite culture.

**Quality Assurance Note:** Egg counts can be influenced by specimen consistency, timing, and the species being investigated.

## Question 5
What is the principal purpose of staining a clinical specimen before microscopic examination?

A. To increase the size of microorganisms  
B. To improve contrast and reveal diagnostic structures  
C. To eliminate all background material  
D. To determine antimicrobial susceptibility directly  

**Correct Answer: B. To improve contrast and reveal diagnostic structures**

**Explanation:** Staining improves contrast between organisms and the background, making important morphological features easier to observe and interpret.

**Distractor Evaluation:**
- A: Staining does not physically enlarge organisms.
- C: Background material may remain visible.
- D: Antimicrobial susceptibility requires separate testing methods.

**Quality Assurance Note:** Appropriate staining procedures and controls are important for reliable interpretation.

## Question 6
Which diagnostic approach detects parasite-specific antibodies in a patient's serum?

A. Serological testing  
B. Direct wet-mount microscopy  
C. Stool concentration  
D. Haematocrit determination  

**Correct Answer: A. Serological testing**

**Explanation:** Serological methods detect antibodies produced by the host in response to parasite antigens. They can be useful when parasites are difficult to detect directly, although antibodies may persist after infection has resolved.

**Distractor Evaluation:**
- B: Wet-mount microscopy directly examines organisms or their stages.
- C: Stool concentration improves recovery of parasites from faeces.
- D: Haematocrit measures the proportion of blood volume occupied by red blood cells.

**Quality Assurance Note:** A positive antibody result does not always establish an active infection.

## Question 7
Which method detects parasite genetic material in a clinical specimen?

A. Polymerase chain reaction (PCR)  
B. Routine Gram staining  
C. Haemoglobin estimation  
D. Direct agglutination without molecular detection  

**Correct Answer: A. Polymerase chain reaction (PCR)**

**Explanation:** PCR amplifies selected DNA sequences, allowing detection of parasite genetic material. Molecular methods can provide sensitive and species-specific identification when appropriately designed and validated.

**Distractor Evaluation:**
- B: Gram staining identifies morphological and staining characteristics.
- C: Haemoglobin estimation measures a blood parameter.
- D: Agglutination is an immunological method rather than a nucleic acid amplification method.

**Quality Assurance Note:** Molecular results depend on specimen quality, assay validation, and appropriate contamination controls.

## Question 8
In diagnostic test evaluation, what does sensitivity describe?

A. The ability of a test to correctly identify individuals without the infection  
B. The ability of a test to correctly identify individuals who have the infection  
C. The proportion of positive results that are false  
D. The reproducibility of a test under identical conditions  

**Correct Answer: B. The ability of a test to correctly identify individuals who have the infection**

**Explanation:** Sensitivity is the proportion of truly infected individuals who test positive. A highly sensitive test has a relatively low false-negative rate.

**Distractor Evaluation:**
- A: This describes specificity.
- C: This relates to false-positive results.
- D: Reproducibility concerns precision rather than sensitivity.

**Quality Assurance Note:** Sensitivity estimates depend on the reference standard and the population being studied.

## Question 9
What is the most appropriate interpretation of a diagnostic test with high specificity?

A. It correctly identifies most infected individuals.  
B. It correctly identifies most individuals who do not have the infection.  
C. It always produces a positive result in infected individuals.  
D. It eliminates the need for clinical assessment.

**Correct Answer: B. It correctly identifies most individuals who do not have the infection.**

**Explanation:** Specificity is the proportion of truly uninfected individuals who test negative. High specificity means fewer false-positive results.

**Distractor Evaluation:**
- A: This describes sensitivity.
- C: No diagnostic test is guaranteed to detect every infection.
- D: Laboratory results should be interpreted alongside clinical and epidemiological information.

**Quality Assurance Note:** Predictive values also depend on disease prevalence in the tested population.

## Question 10
Which practice is most important for maintaining quality assurance in a parasitology diagnostic laboratory?

A. Reporting results without reviewing specimen quality  
B. Using validated procedures, appropriate controls, and documented quality checks  
C. Replacing standard operating procedures with personal preference  
D. Ignoring discrepancies between repeated examinations  

**Correct Answer: B. Using validated procedures, appropriate controls, and documented quality checks**

**Explanation:** Quality assurance involves standardized procedures, trained personnel, equipment maintenance, appropriate controls, accurate documentation, and review of unexpected or inconsistent findings.

**Distractor Evaluation:**
- A: Specimen quality can directly affect diagnostic reliability.
- C: Standard operating procedures promote consistency.
- D: Discrepant results should be investigated.

**Quality Assurance Note:** Internal quality control and external quality assessment help monitor laboratory performance.
