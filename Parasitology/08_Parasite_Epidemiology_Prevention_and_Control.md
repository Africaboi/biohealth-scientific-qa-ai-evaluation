
# Parasite Epidemiology, Prevention and Control

## Question 1
What does the term prevalence mean in parasite epidemiology?

A. The number of new infections occurring over a specified period  
B. The proportion of a population with an existing infection at a specified time or period  
C. The number of deaths caused by a parasite annually  
D. The duration between infection and appearance of symptoms  

**Correct Answer: B. The proportion of a population with an existing infection at a specified time or period**

**Explanation:** Prevalence measures the proportion of a population affected by a condition at a particular time (point prevalence) or over a defined period (period prevalence). It reflects both the occurrence and duration of infection.

**Distractor Evaluation:**
- A: This describes incidence.
- C: This describes mortality.
- D: This relates to the incubation period.

**Quality Assurance Note:** Prevalence estimates should specify the population, location, and reference period.

## Question 2
Which epidemiological measure describes the occurrence of new parasite infections in a population over time?

A. Incidence  
B. Prevalence  
C. Case fatality  
D. Specificity  

**Correct Answer: A. Incidence**

**Explanation:** Incidence measures new cases arising in a population during a defined period. It is useful for assessing the risk of acquiring an infection and monitoring changes in transmission.

**Distractor Evaluation:**
- B: Prevalence includes existing cases.
- C: Case fatality describes the proportion of diagnosed cases that result in death.
- D: Specificity is a measure of diagnostic test performance.

**Quality Assurance Note:** Incidence estimates require a clearly defined population at risk and observation period.

## Question 3
Which factor is particularly important in determining the geographical distribution of many vector-borne parasitic diseases?

A. Climate and environmental conditions affecting vector survival  
B. The colour of buildings in a community  
C. The number of schools in a district alone  
D. The blood group distribution of the population alone  

**Correct Answer: A. Climate and environmental conditions affecting vector survival**

**Explanation:** Temperature, rainfall, humidity, vegetation, and water availability can influence vector breeding, survival, and parasite development. These factors help shape the geographical distribution and seasonality of vector-borne diseases.

**Distractor Evaluation:**
- B: Building colour alone is not a major determinant of vector distribution.
- C: School numbers alone do not determine transmission patterns.
- D: Blood group distribution alone cannot explain the geographical distribution of vector-borne diseases.

**Quality Assurance Note:** Environmental factors should be considered alongside human behaviour, socioeconomic conditions, and healthcare access.

## Question 4
Which intervention is central to preventing malaria transmission in many endemic communities?

A. Use of insecticide-treated mosquito nets  
B. Routine use of antibiotics by healthy individuals  
C. Avoidance of all fruit consumption  
D. Treatment of every fever without diagnostic assessment  

**Correct Answer: A. Use of insecticide-treated mosquito nets**

**Explanation:** Insecticide-treated nets reduce human contact with malaria-transmitting mosquitoes and can lower malaria transmission when used consistently as part of an integrated control programme.

**Distractor Evaluation:**
- B: Antibiotics do not prevent malaria infection.
- C: Fruit consumption is not a malaria transmission route.
- D: Fever has several possible causes, so appropriate assessment and testing are important.

**Quality Assurance Note:** Prevention programmes should consider net access, correct use, local vector behaviour, and insecticide resistance.

## Question 5
What is the main public health objective of mass drug administration (MDA) in selected parasitic disease control programmes?

A. To provide preventive treatment to eligible populations without requiring individual diagnosis before each treatment  
B. To replace all sanitation and health education programmes  
C. To treat only individuals with severe symptoms  
D. To eliminate the need for epidemiological surveillance  

**Correct Answer: A. To provide preventive treatment to eligible populations without requiring individual diagnosis before each treatment**

**Explanation:** MDA involves providing medicines to defined populations at risk, according to disease-specific public health guidelines. It is used in programmes targeting selected neglected tropical diseases.

**Distractor Evaluation:**
- B: MDA complements rather than replaces sanitation and health education.
- C: MDA is population-based and not restricted to severely symptomatic individuals.
- D: Surveillance remains essential for monitoring programme effectiveness and transmission.

**Quality Assurance Note:** Eligibility, medicines, treatment frequency, and safety procedures must follow current national and WHO guidance.

## Question 6
Which environmental intervention helps reduce transmission of many soil-transmitted helminth infections?

A. Improved sanitation and safe disposal of human faeces  
B. Increased use of untreated wastewater on food crops  
C. Reduction of access to clean drinking water  
D. Avoidance of routine hand hygiene  

**Correct Answer: A. Improved sanitation and safe disposal of human faeces**

**Explanation:** Safe sanitation reduces environmental contamination with helminth eggs and larvae. Combined with access to clean water, hygiene education, and appropriate treatment, it helps interrupt transmission.

**Distractor Evaluation:**
- B: Untreated wastewater may increase contamination.
- C: Clean drinking water supports infection prevention.
- D: Hand hygiene is an important preventive measure.

**Quality Assurance Note:** Sanitation interventions should be evaluated in relation to local transmission routes and community practices.

## Question 7
Why is epidemiological surveillance important in parasite control programmes?

A. It provides information for monitoring infection patterns and evaluating interventions.  
B. It guarantees that transmission will stop immediately.  
C. It eliminates the need for laboratory diagnosis.  
D. It is useful only after a disease has been eradicated.

**Correct Answer: A. It provides information for monitoring infection patterns and evaluating interventions.**

**Explanation:** Surveillance involves systematic collection, analysis, interpretation, and use of health data. It helps identify changes in transmission, detect outbreaks, identify high-risk populations, and assess control programme outcomes.

**Distractor Evaluation:**
- B: Surveillance supports control but cannot guarantee immediate interruption of transmission.
- C: Laboratory diagnosis may be an important component of surveillance.
- D: Surveillance is useful before, during, and after control interventions.

**Quality Assurance Note:** Reliable surveillance requires consistent case definitions, suitable diagnostic methods, and timely reporting.

## Question 8
What is the main purpose of integrated vector management?

A. To rely exclusively on one insecticide for all vector species  
B. To combine appropriate evidence-based methods for sustainable vector control  
C. To eliminate the need for community participation  
D. To focus only on treating infected individuals  

**Correct Answer: B. To combine appropriate evidence-based methods for sustainable vector control**

**Explanation:** Integrated vector management combines suitable environmental, biological, chemical, and personal protection measures based on local evidence. It aims to improve effectiveness, sustainability, and efficient use of resources.

**Distractor Evaluation:**
- A: Reliance on one insecticide may increase resistance concerns.
- C: Community participation is important for sustainable vector control.
- D: Vector management focuses on reducing vector populations or human-vector contact, not only treating infections.

**Quality Assurance Note:** Control strategies should be adapted to local vector ecology and monitored for effectiveness and unintended effects.

## Question 9
Which concept best explains the use of a One Health approach in controlling parasitic diseases?

A. Human health is considered independently of animal and environmental health.  
B. Human, animal, and environmental health are interconnected and should be considered together.  
C. Parasite control should focus only on hospital-based treatment.  
D. Environmental conditions have no influence on parasite transmission.

**Correct Answer: B. Human, animal, and environmental health are interconnected and should be considered together.**

**Explanation:** One Health recognises that human health is connected to animal health and the environment. This approach is relevant to zoonotic parasites and infections influenced by ecological and environmental conditions.

**Distractor Evaluation:**
- A: This contradicts the One Health concept.
- C: Prevention and control extend beyond hospitals.
- D: Environmental conditions can strongly influence parasite transmission.

**Quality Assurance Note:** One Health programmes benefit from collaboration across human health, veterinary, environmental, and public health sectors.

## Question 10
Which indicator provides the strongest evidence that a parasite control programme has reduced transmission in a target population?

A. Increased number of health education posters  
B. A sustained reduction in infection prevalence or incidence measured using comparable methods  
C. Increased number of programme meetings alone  
D. A single negative diagnostic result from one individual  

**Correct Answer: B. A sustained reduction in infection prevalence or incidence measured using comparable methods**

**Explanation:** A sustained reduction in infection indicators, assessed through reliable and comparable surveillance, provides evidence of programme impact. Interpretation should account for changes in diagnostic methods, population coverage, and other factors that may influence transmission.

**Distractor Evaluation:**
- A: Posters are an activity indicator, not direct evidence of reduced transmission.
- C: Meetings measure programme activity rather than infection outcomes.
- D: One negative result cannot establish population-level impact.

**Quality Assurance Note:** Programme evaluation should use baseline and follow-up data, consistent diagnostic methods, and clearly defined indicators.
